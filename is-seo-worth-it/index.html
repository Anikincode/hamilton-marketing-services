Is SEO worth it for a small business? An honest answer

For most local service businesses, yes. But not for every business, and not at every stage. Here's how to tell which side you're on, with maths you can do on the back of an invoice.

Kirill Anikin · October 30, 2026 · 6 min read

Key takeaways

  • SEO is worth it when people already search for what you sell, one job is worth real money and you can take on more work.
  • It's often not worth it yet for a brand-new business with no reviews, a service nobody searches for or a very small service area.
  • The payback maths is simple: your monthly SEO cost divided by the profit on one job tells you how many extra jobs break even.
  • SEO builds slowly and keeps paying. Ads are faster, and they stop the day you stop paying.

Here's an answer you won't hear from many SEO companies: sometimes SEO isn't worth it. Not yet, anyway.

It depends on what you sell, who's searching for it and what a customer is worth to you. Let's go through each one, then do the maths with your own numbers.

When SEO is worth it

SEO works best when four things are true at the same time.

People already search for what you sell

Someone with a dead furnace in January types "furnace repair Hamilton" into Google. Someone with water on the basement floor searches "emergency plumber Hamilton Mountain". Those people already want to buy.

SEO puts you in front of them at the exact moment they're looking. If your customers search like that, there's very little marketing more direct.

One job is worth real money

A furnace replacement, a kitchen renovation or a full landscaping job is worth a lot more than one coffee. The bigger the job, the fewer extra customers SEO needs to bring in before it pays for itself.

You can take more work

Extra calls only help if someone answers them and a crew can show up. If you're booked solid for months, sort out capacity first. SEO will still be there.

You plan to be around

Google Ads are like renting a billboard: the calls stop the day you stop paying. SEO is more like owning the building. It takes longer to set up, but a page that ranks can keep bringing in calls month after month.

When SEO isn't worth it (yet)

You're brand new with no reviews

On Google Maps, reviews carry a lot of weight. A new Binbrook landscaper with no reviews is up against companies that have plenty.

In that spot, the first money should go to a properly set-up Business Profile and a habit of asking every customer for a review, not a full SEO retainer. Our guide to SEO for a new business covers what to do first.

Nobody searches for what you sell

Some services are real but rarely searched. You can check before you spend a cent: free keyword tools show rough monthly search numbers. If the answer is close to zero, SEO won't fill your calendar however well it's done.

We ran this test on our own business. Searches for SEO plus Stoney Creek, Ancaster or Grimsby come in at 0 to 10 a month. If we only served Ancaster, ranking for "SEO Ancaster" alone wouldn't pay the bills. So we built for the whole city instead.

Your service area is tiny

If you only work within a few streets of Westdale, fewer people search there than across the whole city. Word of mouth, a good Business Profile and a few happy regulars may be all the marketing you need.

You need calls this week

SEO is slow. Quick fixes can show up within weeks, but new pages and new rankings usually take months. If you need the phone ringing now, paid ads are the faster tool. Our comparison of SEO vs Google Ads covers how the two fit together.

Your website is broken at the root

Some sites can't rank however much content goes on them. We've audited a live site whose robots.txt told Google to ignore the whole thing. On another, Google had indexed about 5 of its 48 pages.

Fix the foundation first. Otherwise SEO money just pours through the cracks.

The payback maths, with your own numbers

You don't need a spreadsheet. You need four numbers that only you know.

What you needCall itWhere to find it
Profit on an average job, after materials and labour$XYour invoices
Monthly SEO cost$YThe quote
Calls you quote to win one jobNYour own records
Jobs a customer brings over time, counting repeat work and referralsRYour customer list

Now the maths:

  • Extra jobs to break even each month = Y ÷ X
  • Extra calls you need each month = (Y ÷ X) × N
  • Real value of a new customer = X × R

A worked example

Say you replace furnaces in Ancaster and Stoney Creek. Each job leaves you $X in profit. SEO costs you $Y a month.

If one furnace job covers a month of SEO, a single extra job breaks even. If you win one job for every N calls you quote, you need N extra calls a month to get there. Every job after that is profit.

Then add R. The customer whose furnace you replaced might call you back for an air conditioner, and tell the neighbour. That's why a customer is usually worth more than their first invoice.

The honest part: SEO is front-loaded. The early months usually cost more than they return, because pages take time to rank. Judge it over a long enough stretch for that to happen, not month by month from the start.

Why the maths tends to improve over time

With ads, every click costs money, every month. A page that ranks for "drain cleaning Dundas" costs the most in the month it's written. After that, every call it brings in costs you less.

That's the case for SEO in one line: the cost comes first, and the returns stack up.

There's a catch. Rankings you've earned need protecting from deleted pages, broken redirects and careless edits. We've seen a page deleted with no redirect, even though it ranked for a search with real monthly volume. All that value, gone in one click.

How to tell if it's working

Judge SEO by calls, form leads and booked jobs, not by pretty charts. Google Search Console is free, and it shows which searches you appear for and how many people click.

Be careful with keyword tool estimates. On one site, a keyword tool guessed traffic about five times higher than Search Console measured. Tools model. Search Console measures. Our guide to what an SEO report should show covers the numbers worth watching.

Common questions about whether SEO is worth it

How long before SEO pays for itself?

There's no honest fixed answer. Quick fixes, like a corrected Business Profile, can show up within weeks. New pages and new rankings usually take months. The bigger your average job, the fewer extra jobs you need to break even.

Is SEO worth it for a one-person business?

Often, yes, as long as you can take more work. A solo appliance repair tech in Hamilton doesn't need much: a strong Business Profile, steady reviews and one good page per service. You don't need a giant budget to compete locally.

Should I start with SEO or Google Ads?

If you need calls now, start with ads. If you can wait, start with SEO. For a lot of local businesses the answer is both: ads for speed while SEO builds, then less ad spend as calls from search grow.

What if my competitors aren't doing SEO?

Then it's a good time to start. Being the only well-built site in your trade in Stoney Creek or Waterdown is much easier than fighting a crowd for the same spot later.
