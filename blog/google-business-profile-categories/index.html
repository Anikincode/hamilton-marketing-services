Google Business Profile categories: how to pick the ones that bring calls

One dropdown on your Business Profile decides which searches you can appear for on Google Maps. Here's how to choose it, and what to do with the rest.

Kirill Anikin · October 30, 2026 · 6 min read

Key takeaways

  • Your primary category decides which searches you can show for. It's the most important choice on your whole profile.
  • Google asks you to choose categories that say what your business is, not every service it offers.
  • Add secondary categories only for real lines of work you want calls for.
  • Each category should have a matching page on your website. The profile makes the claim, and the page backs it up.
  • Change one thing at a time, write down the date, and watch what happens.

You can have great reviews, beautiful photos and a fast website, and still be invisible on Google Maps. Often the reason is one dropdown you filled in years ago and never looked at again.

That dropdown is your Google Business Profile category. It's the first thing we check in every engagement, before keywords and before content, because it decides which searches you can show up for at all.

What are Google Business Profile categories?

Categories tell Google what kind of business you are. You pick them from Google's own fixed list. You can't type your own.

Every profile has one primary category and can add additional categories (people often call them secondary). The primary category is the one Google shows under your business name on Maps.

Think of categories as the aisle signs in a big hardware store. If your furnace company is shelved under "Plumber", the people walking down the heating aisle never see you. They don't know you exist.

Why the primary category matters most

The primary category is the strongest signal on your profile about what you do. In our method, it's step one for a reason: it decides which searches you can even compete for.

Here's a Hamilton example. A heating and cooling company on the Mountain sets its primary category to something narrow, like "Air conditioning contractor". In July, that's fine. In January, when everyone on Upper James is searching "furnace repair Hamilton Mountain", it's fighting with one hand tied behind its back.

A broader, accurate primary category like "HVAC contractor" describes the whole business. Then the narrower ones go in as additional categories.

Google's rule: what you are, not what you do

Google's guidelines give a simple test. Your categories should complete the sentence "This business is a…", not "This business has a…" or "This business does…".

Google also asks you to use as few categories as you need to describe your core business, and to pick the most specific ones that are still accurate. Categories aren't a place for keywords.

So a landscaper in Waterdown is a "Landscaper". It might also be a "Lawn care service" if that's a real part of the business. It isn't a "Snow removal service" just because the owner plowed two driveways last winter.

How to choose your primary category

  1. Start with your money. Which kind of work brings in most of your revenue, and which do you want more of? That's usually your primary.
  2. Look at who's winning. Search your main service the way a customer would, like "plumber Stoney Creek" or "accountant Ancaster". The map pack shows each business's primary category under its name. Note what the top listings use.
  3. Pick the most specific accurate option. If Google has a category that matches your core business exactly, use it over a vague parent.
  4. Check the wording in your own dashboard. Google adds, renames and retires categories, so the list in your profile is the source of truth.

Category names we refer to here, like "Plumber", "HVAC contractor", "Landscaper" or "Accountant", are as they appear in Google's list at the time of writing. Always pick from the list you see.

Secondary categories: each one opens a door

Every additional category gives Google another reason to show you, for a different set of searches. That's the upside.

The downside is that each one is a promise. If you add categories for work you rarely do, you'll show up for jobs you don't want and dilute the message of the whole profile.

A few sensible pairings, as examples:

BusinessPrimary categorySecondary categories worth considering
Heating and cooling companyHVAC contractorFurnace repair service, Air conditioning contractor, Heating contractor
Landscaping companyLandscaperLawn care service, Landscape designer
Cleaning companyHouse cleaning serviceCarpet cleaning service, Window cleaning service

Only add the ones that are true for you. A cleaning company that has never cleaned a window shouldn't list "Window cleaning service".

Common category mistakes

One category and a list of services nobody sells

We audited a profile with a single category and a services list full of stock options the business doesn't even offer. It looked busy. In practice it told Google the wrong story twice: too little in the categories, too much in the services.

Categories used as keywords

Adding every vaguely related category hoping to "rank for more" rarely works the way people hope. Google's guidance is to stay specific and accurate.

Swapping the primary with the seasons

Switching from "Air conditioning contractor" in June to "Furnace repair service" in November feels clever. But every swap changes which searches you show for, and it makes it impossible to tell what's working. Pick a primary that covers the whole business and leave it.

Copying a competitor's full list

Looking at what wins is smart. Copying it wholesale isn't. Their list reflects their business, not yours.

Categories should shape your website

Here's the part that's easy to miss. Your categories aren't only a profile setting. They're the outline of your website.

In the Core 30 method we use, every category becomes a main page on the site, and every service under it gets its own page. An HVAC company with "Furnace repair service" as a category should have a furnace repair page. A plumber in Stoney Creek with "Drainage service" should have a drain page. You can read the full approach on our how we work page.

Why does that matter? Because Google says your position in the regular web results is part of how it judges a business for the map pack. A profile that claims "Furnace repair service" with no furnace page behind it is making a claim it can't back up. Our article on map pack vs organic results explains how the two connect.

Keep it to one page per service. If one page tries to cover furnace repair, AC installation and duct cleaning, it usually ranks for none of them. That's covered in one page, one keyword, and our guide to service pages that rank shows how to write each one.

How to change categories safely

Changing a category is a bit like moving a store to a new aisle. Regular customers might take a while to find you again.

  • Change one thing at a time. If you swap the primary and add three secondaries on the same day, you'll never know which change did what.
  • Write down the date. A simple log saves a lot of guessing later.
  • Expect a review. Edits to a profile can go into review before they show, and big edits can occasionally trigger extra verification.
  • Watch calls, not only rankings. Check your profile's performance numbers and your rankings across the service area over the following weeks.

FAQ

How many categories should I use on my Google Business Profile?

As few as it takes to describe your business accurately. Google asks you to keep to your core business and to be specific. There's no prize for using the maximum, and each extra category should be work you actually want.

Does changing my primary category hurt my ranking?

It can change which searches you show for, which is the point. If the new category is more accurate, that's usually a good trade. Change it deliberately, note the date, and watch your calls and rankings afterwards.

Can I see a competitor's categories?

Their primary category shows under the business name on Google Maps. Their additional categories are harder to see without third-party tools. The primary is the one that tells you most.

What if my exact service isn't a category?

Pick the closest accurate category, then add the service itself in your profile's services list and build a page for it on your website. Not every service needs its own category.
