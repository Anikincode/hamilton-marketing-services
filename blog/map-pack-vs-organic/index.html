Map pack vs organic results: what's the difference, and which should you chase?

The same Google search shows two kinds of free results: the map with a few businesses, and the list of websites below it. They're won in different ways, and most local businesses need both.

Kirill Anikin · October 30, 2026 · 5 min read

Key takeaways

  • The map pack shows Business Profiles. The organic results show web pages. Different listings, won in different ways.
  • Google says local results depend on relevance, distance and prominence, and that your position in web results is part of prominence.
  • The map moves with the searcher. Organic results move much less, which lets a good page reach further than your pin.
  • Urgent, "come now" searches lean on the map. Research and price questions lean on organic pages and AI answers.
  • For most local service businesses the answer is both, tracked separately.

Search "emergency plumber Hamilton Mountain" and look at the page. There's a map with a few businesses on it, and below that, a list of websites.

It's easy to think of that as one result. It isn't. It's two separate competitions on one page, with different rules, different winners and different ways in.

What's on a local results page

A typical local search in Hamilton can show several things, top to bottom:

  • Ads. Sponsored results, marked as such. For the trades in Canada, there can also be Local Services Ads.
  • An AI Overview, on some searches. A summary answer written by Google's AI, with links to sources.
  • The map pack. A map and a short list of businesses, pulled from Google Business Profiles.
  • Organic results. The classic list of web pages.

This article is about the two free ones: the map pack and the organic results. For the paid side, see SEO vs Google Ads. For the AI side, see Google AI Overviews for local businesses.

The map pack: your Business Profile competing

The map pack lists Business Profiles, not web pages. When someone taps a listing, they usually land on your profile, where they can call, get directions or visit your website.

Google says local results are based on three things:

  • Relevance: how well your profile matches the search. Your categories, services and description all feed into it.
  • Distance: how far you are from the searcher, or from the place named in the search.
  • Prominence: how well known your business is, based on things like reviews, links and what the rest of the web says about you.

Google also says your position in web results is part of prominence. That's the bridge between the two worlds, and it's why we don't treat them as separate jobs.

Organic results: your web pages competing

The organic results list web pages. Your homepage, your service pages, your blog posts. What ranks here depends on the page itself: whether it clearly answers the search, whether Google can crawl and index it, how fast and usable it is, and what other sites link to it.

Titles matter a lot here, because they decide what the searcher sees. A small agency we studied had one town page titled with the exact search people used. That page ranked and brought in half of its traffic. Its other town pages, titled generically, didn't.

Ranking isn't the same as getting clicked, either. On our own site, Search Console showed a page sitting at an average position of 3–5 with zero clicks from 233 impressions. Same ranking, wrong title. That's an organic problem with an organic fix.

The biggest difference: who moves

The map moves with the searcher. Organic results move much less.

Picture a furnace company based in Stoney Creek. Someone searching "furnace repair" from Winona or Fruitland is close by, and the company may well show in the map pack. The same search from Westdale, across the city, shows a different set of businesses, ones closer to Westdale.

Organic results are more stable across a city. A strong "furnace repair Hamilton" page can show for searchers in Stoney Creek and Westdale alike. That's how a business reaches further than its pin.

The only way to see this properly is to check from many points, not just your own office. That's what geo-grid rank tracking is for.

Which matters more for your business?

It depends on how your customers search.

An emergency plumber depends heavily on the map. An interior designer in Ancaster, whose clients research for weeks, needs strong organic pages just as much. Most trades sit somewhere in between.

How to win the map pack

  • Get your primary category right, and add only real secondary categories.
  • List every real service in your profile, with plain descriptions.
  • Build a steady flow of reviews, and reply to every one.
  • Keep your name, phone and area identical everywhere.
  • Strengthen your website, because web position feeds into prominence.

How to win the organic results

  • One page per service, each built around one search, like "drain cleaning Stoney Creek".
  • Titles that name the service and the place, so the searcher knows the page is for them.
  • A site Google can crawl and index, that loads quickly on a phone.
  • Pages that answer real questions with real detail.
  • Links from real local and industry sites.

The service page is where the two meet. It's what your profile links to, and it's what ranks organically. Our guide to service pages that rank shows how to build one.

Track them separately

Because they work differently, measure them differently:

  • Map pack: a geo-grid across your service area, plus the calls, website clicks and direction requests in your Business Profile's performance report.
  • Organic: Google Search Console, which shows the searches, impressions, clicks and average position for each page.

If you only check one, you'll misread the other. A business can climb in the organic results and still be invisible on the map from much of its service area, or the other way round.

FAQ

Is the map pack the same as Google Maps?

They use the same Business Profiles, but they're not quite the same view. The map pack is the short list inside Google Search. Google Maps is the full map app, where people can scroll through many more businesses.

Can I rank in the map pack without a website?

It's possible, but it's harder. Google says web position is part of prominence, and a website gives your profile somewhere useful to send people. Without one, you're competing with one hand.

Why do I rank on the map at home but not across town?

Because distance counts. The map pack changes with where the searcher is. Check your rankings from many points across your area to see where you actually show.

Should I focus on the map pack or organic first?

Fix your Business Profile first, because it's quick and the map is front and centre on urgent searches. Then build the service pages that win organic results and strengthen your profile at the same time.
