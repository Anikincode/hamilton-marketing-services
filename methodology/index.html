How we work

How we do SEO: the Core 30 method

Most SEO starts with a keyword list. Ours starts with your Google Business Profile category. Here's every step, in order, and why it's there.

Why a fixed method

Same steps, same order, every time.

Think of it like a home inspection. A good inspector doesn't wander around looking at whatever catches their eye. They follow the same checklist in every house, so the cracked foundation doesn't get missed because the kitchen looked nice.

SEO works the same way. We call our checklist the Core 30 framework. Each step feeds the next, so nothing important gets skipped.

The method at a glance

  • Steps 1–2: find out where you stand
  • Steps 3–4: learn what your customers actually search and ask
  • Steps 5–6: plan the pages, then write them
  • Step 7: maintain and improve, every month

The Core 30 framework

Seven steps, from audit to upkeep.

Here's what happens at each step, with the kind of thing we find along the way.

  1. Audit your Business Profile categories

    Your primary category decides which searches you can show up for. An HVAC company with the wrong one will struggle to appear for "furnace repair Stoney Creek", no matter how good the website is. We once found a profile with a single category and a list of stock services the business didn't even sell.

  2. Crawl the site and find the gaps

    We crawl every page and compare your site with the businesses that outrank you. What do they have that you don't? Along the way we catch the quiet problems: wrong-city titles, missing headings, template text from another business.

  3. Cluster keywords by intent

    "Emergency plumber Hamilton Mountain" is someone with water on the floor. "Bathroom renovation cost Hamilton" is someone planning. Same trade, different intent, so they get grouped, and served, separately.

  4. Mine "People also ask"

    Google shows the questions people ask around a search. Those are the questions your customers have before they call, like "how long does a furnace install take?" Good pages answer them.

  5. Plan the page architecture

    One page per service and intent. When two pages chase the same search, they compete. We've seen a new page "replace" a homepage on six searches, and all six dropped.

  6. Write the pages to a set formula

    Every page is written to the same formula, so each one answers the search it was built for, in plain English, with a clear way to call or book.

  7. Maintain it every month

    A Search Console click-through audit. Pages ranking 4–10 with weak clicks get new titles, and we recheck them after 30 days. More on this below.

Our three pillars

What runs underneath every step.

The seven steps are the order of work. These three pillars are the habits that sit under all of it.

Keyword tracking

We track the searches that lead to calls across your service area, using geo-grid rank tracking. The map pack moves with the searcher. A cleaning company near Limeridge Mall can lead on the Mountain and be invisible in Ancaster. One ranking from one spot would never show you that.

Defensive SEO

Protecting the rankings you already have from spam links, deleted pages, broken redirects and careless edits. We once found a network of 60 spam domains pointing at a small business site, and built a disavow file for it.

AI implementation

Structured data, llms.txt and content that AI assistants can quote. We also built a tool that shows the hidden searches ChatGPT runs when someone asks it a question like "who's the best HVAC company in Hamilton?"

Step 7, up close

Monthly maintenance: the step most SEO skips.

Here's the problem. A page can rank well and still get almost no clicks. That isn't a ranking problem. It's a title problem.

It happened on our own site. Search Console showed one page at an average position of 3–5, with zero clicks from 233 impressions. People saw it and scrolled right past.

So every month we pull the pages ranking 4–10 with weak click-through, rewrite their titles, and recheck after 30 days. If the new title didn't help, we try again.

What else happens each month

  • Geo-grid tracking for the searches that lead to calls
  • Defensive checks for spam links, missing pages and broken redirects
  • Daily Business Profile posts, if they're part of your plan, scheduled through Local Viking
  • A plain-English report on calls, leads, rankings, what we did and what didn't work

Tools

Measured, not guessed.

Keyword tools model. Search Console measures. We've seen a keyword tool estimate a site's traffic at about five times what Search Console showed. So when the two disagree, Search Console wins.

Each step uses the tool that fits it, and nothing more.

Which tool, which step

  • Google Business Profile: the category audit in step 1
  • Ubersuggest: keyword volume and difficulty in step 3
  • PageSpeed Insights: phone speed checks during the crawl
  • BrightLocal: geo-grid tracking and citations
  • Google Search Console: the monthly click-through audit
  • Local Viking: scheduling Business Profile posts

FAQ

Questions about how we work.

Why do you start with the Business Profile and not the website?

Because for most local searches, the map pack shows up before the regular results, and the map pack comes from your Business Profile. If the primary category is wrong, the website can't fix it.

Why not put all my services on one page?

Because "AC installation Hamilton" and "furnace repair Stoney Creek" are different searches from different people. A page that tries to answer both usually ranks for neither.

My site already ranks well. Do I still need this?

Maybe not all of it. But ranking well is exactly when defensive SEO matters most. One careless redesign or deleted page can quietly undo a ranking you spent a long time earning.

Can you guarantee the method gets me to #1?

No. Google says nobody can guarantee a #1 ranking. A clear method means the right work gets done in the right order, and you can see exactly what was done.
