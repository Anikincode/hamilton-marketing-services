AI SEO pricing: what you should (and shouldn't) pay for

A new acronym usually arrives with a new invoice. Here's which parts of AI search work are worth paying for, and which you're probably paying for already.

Kirill Anikin · October 30, 2026 · 5 min read

Key takeaways

  • Most AI search work is SEO work: clear pages, consistent business details, reviews and structured data.
  • The genuinely extra parts are small: quotable answers, structured data, llms.txt and checking what AI tools say about you.
  • AI search work is usually best bought as part of SEO or as a one-time project, not as a separate monthly product.
  • Nobody controls what ChatGPT or Google's AI Overviews say. Walk away from guaranteed AI mentions.

Someone emails you offering "AI SEO" for a monthly fee. Is it new work, or the same SEO with a new label?

Usually, it's mostly the second. Here's how to tell what's actually extra, how it's priced and what to watch for before you pay.

Why AI search work is mostly SEO

When someone asks ChatGPT, Gemini or Google's AI Overviews "who does furnace repair in Stoney Creek?", the tool has to find businesses somewhere. It pulls from websites, Business Profiles, directories and reviews, and it often runs web searches in the background first.

We built a tool that shows the hidden searches ChatGPT runs when it answers a question. The lesson from it is simple: if you don't show up for those searches, you're unlikely to be named in the answer. Good SEO is the entry ticket.

So the core of AI search optimization is work your SEO should already cover:

  • One clear page per service, with plain answers near the top
  • The same name, address and phone number everywhere
  • A strong Business Profile with real reviews
  • A site that Google can crawl and index

Our guide to AEO vs GEO vs SEO explains how the terms overlap.

What's genuinely extra

A few jobs go beyond standard SEO. They're real, and they're mostly one-time:

  • Structured data. Code that labels your business name, services, area and hours so machines can read them without guessing.
  • llms.txt. A plain text file that summarizes your site for AI tools. It's a young idea and support varies, so it's cheap insurance, not a magic switch. More in what is llms.txt?
  • Quotable answers. Rewriting key sections so an AI tool can lift one clear sentence: what you do, where you do it and how to reach you.
  • Checking what AI says about you. Asking AI tools the questions your customers ask, and recording who gets named. Our guide on checking if AI recommends your business shows how.
  • Cleaning up conflicting facts. We've seen a website showing three different review counts. Mixed signals like that give AI tools nothing solid to repeat.

If you already pay for SEO, the first question is simple: which of this is my current plan not doing?

What a one-time AI search project should hand you

If you pay for a project, you should be able to point at what you got. A fair list looks like this:

  • A before record. The questions tested, like "who's a good plumber on Hamilton Mountain?", and which businesses each AI tool named.
  • The hidden searches. What ChatGPT actually searched for when it answered, so you know which searches you need to rank for.
  • Structured data, added and tested. Your business details labelled on the pages that matter.
  • Rewritten answers. Your key service pages opening with one plain sentence on what you do, where and how to book.
  • A list of fixed listings. Every directory where your name, address, phone or hours didn't match, and what was changed.
  • An after record. The same questions tested again, later, so you can compare.

If a proposal can't describe its output this clearly, you're paying for activity, not results.

What to be wary of

  • Guaranteed AI mentions. Nobody controls what ChatGPT, Gemini or AI Overviews say. Anyone promising you'll be named is guessing.
  • "Rank #1 in ChatGPT." There's no fixed ranking inside an AI answer. The same question can get different answers on different days.
  • Paid "submission" to AI tools. There's no sign-up that makes ChatGPT recommend your business.
  • Mass AI-written pages. Piles of thin pages written by AI to catch every possible question. Google treats mass-produced pages made to game rankings as spam.
  • Scores with no link to calls. An "AI visibility score" means little if nobody can show it's bringing in customers.
  • Paying twice. A separate AI fee for work your SEO plan already includes.

Questions to ask before you pay

  1. What exactly will you change on my site and my listings?
  2. Which of this isn't already in my SEO plan?
  3. How will you check what AI tools say about my business, before and after?
  4. Which questions and searches will you test, for example "best furnace repair in Ancaster"?
  5. How will we know if it brought in customers?

A worked example: is it worth it?

Say a one-time AI search project costs $P, and a new customer is worth $X in profit to you. It pays for itself after P ÷ X new customers.

The hard part is knowing which customers came from AI. Someone who hears your name from ChatGPT may then search your business name on Google. So watch searches for your business name in Search Console, and ask new callers how they found you. Simple, and far better than guessing.

Common questions about AI SEO pricing

Do I need AI SEO if I already do SEO?

If your SEO is done well, you already have most of it. The extras, structured data, llms.txt and quotable answers, are usually a small project rather than a new monthly bill.

Can anyone guarantee my business shows up in ChatGPT?

No. AI tools decide what to say, and their answers change. What you can do is make your business easy to find, easy to understand and consistent everywhere.

Is llms.txt worth paying for?

It's a small job, so it shouldn't be priced like a big one. Support varies between AI tools, so treat it as a sensible extra, not the core of the work.

How do I know if AI tools mention my business?

Ask them, the way a customer would, in a fresh chat. Try questions like "who repairs furnaces in Ancaster?" and note who gets named.
