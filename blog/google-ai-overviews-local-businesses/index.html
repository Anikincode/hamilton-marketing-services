Google AI Overviews and your local business: what actually changes

Google now answers some searches before anyone scrolls. Here's what that summary at the top means for a local business, and what to do about it without chasing hype.

Kirill Anikin · October 30, 2026 · 6 min read

Key takeaways

  • An AI Overview is a summary Google writes at the top of some searches, with links to the pages it drew from.
  • Google says there's no special trick to appearing in one. A page has to be indexed and eligible to show a snippet, and normal SEO best practice applies.
  • For many "I need someone now" searches, the map pack is still where the calls come from. AI Overviews matter more on the questions people ask before they're ready to call.
  • The local business that gets quoted is the one with clear answers on its own pages and a complete, accurate Business Profile.

Search "why does my furnace keep turning on and off" and Google may answer before you scroll. A few paragraphs, written by AI, with small links to the websites it read.

That's an AI Overview. For a furnace company in Stoney Creek, it raises a fair question: if Google answers the question itself, does anyone still visit my website?

The short answer is that some searches change a lot and some barely change at all. Here's how to tell which is which, and what to do about each.

What is a Google AI Overview?

An AI Overview is a short, AI-written answer that Google shows at the top of some search results. It's built with Google's Gemini models, and it draws on pages from Google's own search index.

Each Overview links to some of the pages it used. Those links are the new prize. Being one of them puts your business in front of the searcher before any regular result.

Google also has AI Mode, a separate, more conversational way to search. Google has described how it breaks one question into many related searches at once and pulls the results together. Keep that idea in mind. It's how most AI search tools work, and it comes up again below.

Do AI Overviews show up on local searches?

It helps to split local searches into two kinds.

The first kind is "I need someone now". Think "emergency plumber Hamilton Mountain" or "furnace repair Stoney Creek". On searches like these, the map pack and ads usually still own the top of the page.

The second kind is "I'm trying to understand my problem". Think "why is my basement damp in spring" or "how long does a furnace replacement take". These questions are where AI Overviews tend to appear.

That said, Google changes what it shows from one search to the next, and over time. Don't trust anyone's general rule, including ours. Search the handful of phrases that matter most to your business and look at what actually appears.

Where does Google get the answer?

From pages it has already crawled and indexed. Google's own guidance says there are no extra requirements to appear in an AI Overview. A page needs to be indexed and eligible to show a snippet in regular search, and the usual best practices apply. You can read it in Google's documentation on AI features and your website.

That's good news for a local business. There's no secret "AI Overview SEO" you're missing. But it also means the basics are not optional.

A page Google hasn't indexed can't be quoted. On one site we audited, Google had indexed about 5 of its 48 pages. The rest sat in Search Console as "Discovered – currently not indexed". None of those pages could appear in an Overview, however good they were. If you've never checked yours, start with our guide to Google Search Console for beginners.

Will AI Overviews take my clicks?

Some, on some searches. Let's be honest about that.

If someone searches "what size of water heater do I need" and the Overview answers it well, they may not click anywhere. For a blog post written to catch that search, that's a real loss.

But here's the thing about service businesses. The searches that pay end in a phone call, not a paragraph. An Overview can explain why a furnace short-cycles. It can't come to a house in Binbrook and fix one.

So the goal shifts a little. On question searches, you want to be the source the Overview links to, so your name is in front of the person while they're still learning. On "I need someone now" searches, you want to be in the map pack. Our article on the map pack vs organic results explains how those two parts of the page work.

What a Hamilton business should do about AI Overviews

None of this is exotic. It's the work that wins local search anyway, with a sharper focus on answering questions clearly.

  1. Make sure your pages are indexed. Check the Pages report in Search Console. Fix anything blocked or ignored before you write a word.
  2. Answer the questions customers really ask. Look at the "People also ask" box for your main services. "How long does a furnace replacement take?" deserves a clear answer on your furnace replacement page, near the top.
  3. Write answer-first. Put the direct answer in the first sentence or two, then explain. AI summaries lift clear sentences more easily than long build-ups.
  4. Keep one page per service. A page about "AC installation Hamilton" that also covers furnace repair gives Google a muddled picture of both.
  5. Keep your Business Profile complete. It's Google's own record of your business: categories, services, hours, service area. Our Google Business Profile optimization service covers it end to end.
  6. Keep your facts consistent. We once found three different review counts shown on the same website. When a page contradicts itself, a summary can pick the wrong version.

What not to do

Don't try to opt out by accident. Google's snippet controls, like the nosnippet setting, also limit how your content can be used in AI Overviews. Some sites switch these on without realizing they're hiding from both.

Don't churn out dozens of thin, AI-written blog posts aimed at every question you can think of. Google's spam policies cover mass-produced pages made mainly to rank. They don't help, and they can hurt the pages that do.

And don't panic-rewrite your best pages. If a service page already ranks and brings in calls, protect it. Small, clear improvements beat a rewrite every time.

How to tell whether you're showing up

Search your key questions yourself, on a phone and on a computer, and note which websites the Overview links to. If the same competitor or directory keeps appearing, look at what their page does that yours doesn't.

Search Console includes clicks and impressions from Google's AI features in its normal Performance report. It doesn't split them out, so you won't see a separate AI Overview line. Watch the pages that answer questions, and whether their impressions rise over time.

Finally, ask new customers how they found you. "I saw your name when I googled my problem" is the kind of answer that tells you this is working.

FAQ

Can I stop Google using my site in AI Overviews?

Google's snippet controls, such as nosnippet, limit how your content is shown, including in AI Overviews. But they also limit your regular search snippets. For most local businesses, being quoted is worth more than being hidden.

Do I need special markup to appear in AI Overviews?

No. Google says there are no extra requirements beyond normal SEO best practice. Structured data still helps Google understand your business, but it isn't a ticket into an Overview.

Do AI Overviews replace the Google map pack?

Not for most "I need someone now" searches. The map pack is still where many local calls start, so your Business Profile and reviews matter as much as ever. Check your own searches to see what appears for your trade.

Is an AI Overview the same thing as Gemini?

They're related but different. AI Overviews appear inside Google Search and are built with Gemini models. Gemini is also Google's separate assistant app. Both are Google products, so the same groundwork helps with both.
