What your SEO report should show, and what's just filler

If your monthly SEO report is pages of charts and you still can't tell whether the phone rang more, it isn't written for you. Here's what a useful one looks like.

Kirill Anikin · October 30, 2026 · 5 min read

Key takeaways

  • A good SEO report answers three questions: did more customers find us, did more of them get in touch, and what was done this month?
  • Lead with calls, form fills and Business Profile actions. Rankings and traffic come second, as the explanation.
  • Map rankings should be tracked across your service area, and traffic should come from Search Console, not a tool's estimate.
  • Every report should list what was done, what's next and what didn't work.

You open the monthly SEO report. There are charts, a domain score, a list of keywords with little green arrows. It looks busy.

Then you put it down and still can't answer the only question you care about: is this bringing in more work?

That's not your fault. It's the report's. Here's what a useful SEO report should show a local business, section by section.

The three questions every SEO report should answer

  1. Did more of the right people find us? On Google Maps, in the regular results and in AI answers.
  2. Did more of them get in touch? Calls, forms, direction requests, bookings.
  3. What did you do, and what's next? In plain words, with no padding.

If a report answers those three on the first page, everything after it is supporting detail. If it doesn't, the rest is decoration.

Section 1: Calls, leads and enquiries

Start with the results that pay the bills.

  • Calls and website clicks from your Business Profile. Google shows these in the profile's performance figures.
  • Direction requests. Important if customers come to you, like an auto shop on Upper James or a restaurant on Locke Street.
  • Form submissions and tap-to-call clicks from your website.

Compare each month with the same month last year where you can, not just with last month. Search demand is seasonal. Even searches for SEO services in Hamilton swing about ten times between the busy September-to-January stretch and the quiet spring and summer. A furnace company's slow month says nothing about its SEO.

Section 2: Visibility on Google Maps, across your area

For most local trades, the map pack is where the calls start. A report should show where you appear in it.

Here's the catch: map results change with the searcher's location. A plumber can rank in the top three for "emergency plumber" near their shop in Stoney Creek and not appear at all for someone on the Mountain.

So a single ranking number is misleading. A good report shows a geo-grid: a map of your rankings checked from many points across your service area. Our article on geo-grid rank tracking explains how to read one.

Section 3: Search Console results

Search Console is Google's own data about your website: impressions, clicks, click-through rate and average position, by page and by search.

A useful report doesn't just paste these totals. It spots the stories in them.

Here's one from our own site. Search Console showed a page sitting at an average position of 3 to 5, with zero clicks from 233 impressions. That's not a ranking problem. Google was showing the page, and people weren't choosing it. The fix is the title and description, and a good report would flag it as the month's to-do.

If Search Console is new to you, start with our guide to Google Search Console for beginners.

Section 4: Rankings for the searches that pay

A report tracking hundreds of keywords is usually tracking the wrong ones.

A short list is better: one or two searches per service, in the areas you serve. "Furnace repair Stoney Creek", "AC installation Hamilton", "furnace replacement Ancaster". These are the searches people make right before they call.

Watch for padding. Long, odd phrases nobody types, like "best affordable professional furnace repair Hamilton Ontario Canada", are easy to rank for and bring in nobody.

Section 5: Work done this month

This section should be specific enough that you could check it yourself.

  • "Published the heat pump installation page" rather than "content optimization".
  • "Rewrote titles on the drain cleaning and sump pump pages" rather than "on-page improvements".
  • "Fixed the phone number on two directory listings" rather than "citation work".

Vague work descriptions aren't always a sign of bad work. But they make it impossible for you to tell.

Section 6: What's next, and what didn't work

A good report looks forward. What's planned for next month, and why?

It should also say plainly when something didn't work. A title rewrite that didn't lift clicks. A page that hasn't been indexed yet. That honesty is what makes the good news believable.

And it should track whether fixes actually ship. We've seen a business with 9 of its 10 service pages missing a main heading, still unfixed after four audits. A report that keeps finding the same problem, month after month, without saying who will fix it and when, isn't doing its job.

Red flags in an SEO report

  • Traffic estimates presented as fact. Keyword tools model traffic. On one site we checked, a tool estimated traffic about five times higher than Search Console showed. Real reports use real data.
  • No mention of calls or leads. Rankings and traffic are means, not ends.
  • Only good news. Every month has something that didn't move.
  • One ranking from one location. The map pack doesn't work that way.
  • Ads mixed in with SEO. If you also run Google Ads, the report should keep paid and organic separate, and show which ad clicks became calls. We've seen ad accounts running with no conversion tracking at all, so nobody could tell.

FAQ

How often should I get an SEO report?

Monthly works for most local businesses. SEO moves slowly enough that weekly reports are mostly noise, and anything less often makes it hard to catch problems early.

What if traffic went down but calls went up?

Calls matter more. Traffic can fall when a page stops ranking for searches that never brought customers. A good report explains which searches changed and why the calls held up.

Why do numbers differ between tools?

Because most tools estimate and Search Console measures. Ranking tools also check from different locations and at different times. For your own site's clicks and impressions, trust Search Console.

Should my SEO report include AI search?

It's worth a short section. Whether you're named when customers ask ChatGPT or Gemini the questions that matter, and whether your details were right. Treat it as a pattern over months, not a monthly score.
