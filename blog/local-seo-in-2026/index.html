Local SEO in 2026: what changed, what didn't, and what to do first

AI answers get the headlines, but the businesses that win local search still do the same few things properly. Here's what's actually different this year, and where to start.

Kirill Anikin · October 30, 2026 · 6 min read

Key takeaways

  • AI answers are a new place to be found, but they draw on the same things: your Business Profile, your website, your reviews and what the web says about you.
  • The basics are still broken on a lot of sites. When we scored 35 local-business websites in September 2026, the average was 34 out of 100.
  • Measure with real data. Search Console and a geo-grid beat keyword-tool estimates.
  • Protect what you've already earned: deleted pages, spam links and careless edits undo rankings quietly.
  • The order of work hasn't changed: profile, technical fixes, service pages, reviews, then everything else.

Every year someone announces that local SEO is dead. Then a homeowner on the Mountain searches "furnace repair near me" on a cold night, taps the map, and calls whoever is there.

Local SEO in 2026 does look different on the surface. There are more places for a customer to find you, and more ways to get it wrong. But underneath, the businesses that win are still the ones that do a handful of things properly and keep doing them.

What's actually different in 2026

Answers come from more places

A customer looking for a plumber in Stoney Creek might see an AI Overview at the top of Google. They might ask ChatGPT or Gemini directly. They might never type a search at all.

We built a tool that shows the hidden searches ChatGPT runs while it answers a question like that. Watching it work makes one thing obvious: the AI is still searching the web. It's reading your website, your listings and what other sites say about you, then building an answer from what it finds.

So AI search isn't a separate world. It's a new reader of the same material. The businesses it can describe confidently are the ones with consistent details, clear pages and real reviews.

Vague pages lose twice

A service page that says "quality service at competitive prices" was always weak. Now it loses in Google and gives an AI assistant nothing worth quoting.

The pages that do well answer the real question early, in plain sentences, with specifics: what's included, which areas, how pricing works. Our AI search optimization page covers how we approach it.

Mass-produced pages are a bigger risk

AI makes it easy to churn out piles of near-identical town pages in an afternoon. Google's spam policies specifically target pages mass-produced to rank without adding real value. A page for "drain cleaning in Binbrook" needs to say something true about Binbrook jobs, or it shouldn't exist.

What hasn't changed at all

Here's the part that should reassure you. The foundations are exactly the same, and plenty of your competitors still haven't laid them.

On September 20, 2026, we scored 35 live local-business websites on our audit rubric. The average was 34 out of 100. The problems weren't exotic:

  • A business with 9 of 10 service pages missing an H1 heading, still unfixed after four audits
  • A live site whose robots.txt told Google to ignore the whole thing
  • Wrong city names in titles and headings
  • Text left over from someone else's template, including a fake phone number on 19 pages
  • A Business Profile with one category and a list of stock services the business doesn't sell

None of that needs AI to fix. It needs someone to look, and then do the boring work.

The things that matter most in local SEO are still:

  • Your Business Profile category. Your primary category decides which searches you can show for. See how to choose Business Profile categories.
  • One page per service. "Furnace repair Stoney Creek" and "AC installation Hamilton" are different searches with different pages.
  • Reviews, steadily. Asked for after every job, answered every time. Our guide on how to get more Google reviews has the system.
  • Consistent details. The same name, phone and area everywhere.
  • A site Google can read. Indexed, fast on a phone, with clear titles and headings.

Measure with real numbers

Here's a trap that's getting worse, not better. Keyword tools are useful for ideas, but their traffic numbers are estimates. We've seen a tool estimate a site's traffic about five times higher than Search Console showed.

Tools model. Search Console measures. For your website, trust Search Console.

For the map, use a geo-grid: the same search run from many points across your service area. Your rank from your own office tells you very little about the Mountain, the lower city and Stoney Creek all at once.

And watch the gap between ranking and clicks. On our own site, Search Console showed a page at an average position of 3–5 with zero clicks from 233 impressions. It wasn't a ranking problem. It was a title problem.

Defend what you've already got

Most talk about SEO is about winning new rankings. Plenty get lost quietly, too.

  • Pages deleted without a redirect. We've seen one go that ranked for a search with real monthly volume.
  • Two pages competing for one search. On one site, a new page "replaced" the homepage on six searches, and all six dropped.
  • Spam links. We found a network of 60 spam domains pointing at one small business site.
  • Edits nobody approved. Changes to your Business Profile, or a staff member "tidying up" the website.

Protecting existing rankings is one of our three pillars. Our article on defensive SEO explains how to do it.

Time your work to your season

Search demand moves with the calendar. In our own trade, every "SEO plus city" search we looked at swings about tenfold: high from September to January, low from April to August.

Your trade has its own rhythm. A furnace company knows its phones ring at the first cold snap. A landscaper in Waterdown knows spring is the rush. Search Console will show you your own pattern.

The lesson is the same either way. Do the work before the busy season, not during it. New pages and fixes take time to be picked up, so a furnace page polished in August is ready for October.

What to ignore

  • Guarantees. Google itself says nobody can guarantee a #1 ranking. Anyone who promises one is selling something else.
  • Keyword-stuffed business names and fake addresses. Both break Google's Business Profile guidelines, and both can get a profile suspended.
  • Bought links and bought reviews. They can undo years of honest work.
  • Hype about the "end of SEO". The tools change. People still need a plumber.

The order of work for 2026

If you only have a few hours a month, spend them in this order:

  1. Fix your Business Profile. Primary category, real services, correct hours, a phone number only you use.
  2. Remove anything that blocks Google. Indexing problems, robots.txt mistakes, broken redirects.
  3. Build or fix one page per service. Clear title with service and city, one H1, real detail.
  4. Start a review habit. Ask after every job, reply to every review.
  5. Make your details consistent on your site, your profile and the main directories.
  6. Make your pages easy to quote. Direct answers, structured data and an accurate llms.txt.
  7. Track properly. Search Console for your site, a geo-grid for the map.
  8. Keep it up monthly. Rewrite titles on pages ranking 4–10 with weak clicks, then recheck after 30 days.

FAQ

Is local SEO still worth it in 2026?

Yes. People still search for local services, and the map pack, organic results and AI answers all draw on the same foundations. Because so many local sites still have basic problems, doing the basics well is still a real advantage.

Has AI search replaced Google Maps for local businesses?

No. AI tools are another place customers ask, and they lean on the same information: your profile, your site, your reviews and your listings. Get those right and you're ready for both.

What's the single most important local SEO task this year?

Check your Google Business Profile's primary category. It decides which searches you can show for at all, and it takes minutes to review.

Do I need to change my whole strategy because of AI?

Probably not. Most of what helps in AI answers, like clear pages, consistent details and real reviews, already helps in Google. Add structured data and plain, quotable answers on top.
