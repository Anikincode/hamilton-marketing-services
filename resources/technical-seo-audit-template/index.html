Technical SEO audit template: copy it, fill it in, fix in order

A header sheet, a check sheet and a findings log you can copy into any spreadsheet. It turns a pile of warnings into a short list of fixes, each with a name and a date.

Kirill Anikin · October 30, 2026 · 8 min read

Key takeaways

  • A technical audit is only useful if it ends in a short, ordered list of fixes, each with an owner and a date.
  • Check crawling and indexing first. A blocked or unindexed page can't rank, however fast or well written it is.
  • Rate every finding by severity, and fix critical problems on money pages before tidying anything else.
  • Save each audit as a new, dated file. Overwriting the last one destroys your proof of progress.

A crawler will happily hand you hundreds of warnings. Plenty of owners look at that list once, feel sick, and close the tab.

A template fixes that. It makes you check things in the right order, write each problem down once, and give every fix a name and a date. This technical SEO audit template is the structure we use, laid out so you can copy it into a spreadsheet.

If you want the reasoning behind each check, read our technical SEO audit playbook alongside it. This page is the form. That one is the manual.

How to set it up

  1. Make a spreadsheet with three tabs: Header, Checks and Findings.
  2. Name the file with today's date, for example "2026-10-30 technical audit". Never overwrite an old audit. The history is how you prove what improved.
  3. Open your free tools: Google Search Console, PageSpeed Insights and a phone. A crawler (Screaming Frog is one well-known option) speeds things up on bigger sites, but a small local site can be checked by hand.
  4. Work through the Checks tab in order. Every time a check fails, add one row to Findings.

Tab 2: Checks

Mark each check Pass, Fix or N/A. Add a short note, especially for "Fix".

A. Crawling and indexing

A2No important page has a noindex tagURL Inspection, or a crawler
A3The XML sitemap is submitted and lists only live pagesSearch Console, Sitemaps; open the sitemap itself

Don't skip A1 because it sounds too basic. We've audited a live site whose robots.txt blocked all indexing. Common slip-ups are in our post on robots.txt mistakes.

C. Speed and Core Web Vitals

Core Web Vitals are Google's measures of how a page feels to use: how fast the main content appears, how quickly the page responds to a tap, and whether things jump around while it loads. PageSpeed Insights labels each one good, needs improvement or poor.

IDCheckWhere to lookResultNotes

C2Interaction to Next Paint (response to taps)PageSpeed Insights, real-user section if shown
C3Cumulative Layout Shift (content jumping)PageSpeed Insights
C4Images are compressed and sized for the screenPageSpeed Insights suggestions

Test your homepage and your top service pages, not only the homepage. We've measured a main-content time of 27.7 seconds on mobile on one site and 9.9 seconds on another. Our guide to page speed for local businesses covers the usual causes.

Severity: what each level means

SeverityMeansExamples
CriticalStops Google indexing the site, or tells customers something falserobots.txt blocking everything, a wrong phone number, someone else's city
HighCosts rankings or calls on pages that bring in workMissing H1s on service pages, deleted pages with no redirect, very slow mobile pages
MediumWeakens pages without breaking themDuplicate meta descriptions, missing schema, thin internal linking
LowTidy-upA few long titles on old blog posts

Fix in this order: critical first, then high on service pages, then high everywhere else. A renovation company's "kitchen remodel Ancaster" page matters more than a blog post from years ago.

After the fixes

  1. Tell Google. In Search Console, use "Validate fix" on any issue you've solved, and "Request indexing" in URL Inspection for your most important changed pages.
  2. Fill in "Fixed on". Then come back a few weeks later and fill in "Rechecked on" once you've confirmed the fix held.
  3. Rerun the whole template every few months, as a new dated file, and compare it with the last one.

That comparison is one of the most useful things a business owner can have. We've seen a business with service pages still missing their H1s after four audits. The template doesn't fix anything on its own. The "Who" and "Fixed on" columns are what make things happen.

FAQ

Do I need a paid crawler to use this template?

Not for a small local site. Search Console, PageSpeed Insights, your browser and your phone cover most of it. A crawler saves time once you have more pages than you can comfortably click through.

Can my web designer do the fixes?

Usually, yes. Most fixes are edits to titles, headings, redirects and settings. Hand them the Findings tab, not the whole template. Every row should say what to change and where.

How often should I run a technical SEO audit?

Every few months, and always after a redesign, a platform move or a big batch of new pages. Those are the moments things break quietly.

What should I fix first if everything looks broken?

Anything that stops Google indexing the site, then anything that gives customers wrong information. After that, start with the service pages that bring in your best work.
