Free SEO tools for small business owners that actually help

You don't need an expensive subscription to start doing SEO. The most useful tools for a local business are free, and most of them come from Google itself.

Kirill Anikin · October 30, 2026 · 6 min read

Key takeaways

  • The most useful free SEO tools are Google's own: Search Console, your Google Business Profile and PageSpeed Insights.
  • Add Bing Webmaster Tools, Bing Places and Apple Business Connect so you're covered beyond Google.
  • Free keyword tools give estimates, not facts. On one site we checked, a keyword tool estimated traffic about five times higher than Search Console showed.
  • Google's own search page, with its autocomplete and "People also ask" box, is a free keyword tool hiding in plain sight.

Search "free SEO tools" and you'll find lists that go on forever. Plenty of the entries are free trials of paid software, or tools built for people who do SEO all day.

If you run a plumbing company in Stoney Creek or a cleaning business on the Mountain, you need far fewer. Here are the free SEO tools for small business owners that are worth your time, and what to look at first in each.

Start with the free tools Google gives you

Google Search Console

If you only set up one tool, make it this one. Search Console shows how Google sees your website: which pages it has indexed, which searches you appear for, and how many people click.

It measures real data from Google, not estimates. That makes it the tool to trust when other tools disagree.

Check first: the Pages report, to see whether Google is indexing your important pages, and the Performance report, to see which searches bring people in. Our guide to Google Search Console for beginners walks through both.

Google Business Profile

Your Business Profile is what shows up on Google Maps and in the map pack. It's free, and it comes with its own performance figures: how many people called, asked for directions or clicked through to your website.

Check first: your primary category. It decides which searches you can show up for. A furnace company listed only under a broad category may never appear for "furnace repair Ancaster".

PageSpeed Insights

PageSpeed Insights tells you how fast your pages load, on mobile and desktop, and what's slowing them down.

This matters more than most owners think. On one site we audited, the main content took 27.7 seconds to appear on a phone. On another, 9.9 seconds. Most people looking for an emergency plumber won't wait that long.

Check first: your homepage and your busiest service page, on mobile. Look at Largest Contentful Paint, the time it takes for the main content to show.

Rich Results Test

Google's Rich Results Test checks the structured data on a page: the code that tells search engines your business name, phone, hours and services. It flags errors in plain terms.

Google Analytics, Keyword Planner and Trends

Google Analytics is free and shows what visitors do once they're on your site. Google Keyword Planner is free with a Google Ads account. It's built for advertisers, so its search numbers can be broad ranges. Google Trends compares interest in searches over time.

Trends is especially handy for seasonal trades. Searches for furnace repair and AC repair don't peak in the same months, and knowing your own pattern helps you plan. Even searches for SEO services swing hard: in the Ubersuggest data we pulled for Hamilton, they run about ten times higher from September to January than in spring and summer.

Free tools beyond Google

Not everyone uses Google. Some customers search with Bing, ask Microsoft Copilot, or use Apple Maps on their iPhone.

  • Bing Webmaster Tools is Bing's version of Search Console. Bing's index also powers Microsoft Copilot, so it's worth a one-time setup.
  • Bing Places for Business is Bing's free business listing, the equivalent of your Google Business Profile.
  • Apple Business Connect lets you claim and update your business on Apple Maps for free.

Use exactly the same name, phone, address or service area and hours on all of them. Mismatched details confuse search engines and AI tools alike.

Free keyword research, without a keyword tool

Google's own search page is the most underrated free keyword tool there is.

Start typing "furnace repair Ancaster" and watch the autocomplete suggestions. Those are real searches people make. Then search it and open the "People also ask" box. Every question there is something a customer wonders before they call.

Mining "People also ask" is one of the steps in our own process. It's free, it's fast and it tells you exactly which questions your service pages should answer. For the full method, see local keyword research.

Free versions of paid keyword tools

We use Ubersuggest for search volume and keyword difficulty, and it has a limited free version. Ahrefs, Semrush, Moz and Screaming Frog are also well known, and some offer free tools or limited free versions. We don't review those here, because we don't run our client work on them.

One warning applies to all of them: they estimate. They model how much traffic a site gets and how many people search a phrase. On one site we checked, a keyword tool estimated traffic about five times higher than Search Console showed. Tools model; Search Console measures.

Use keyword tools to compare phrases against each other. Use Search Console to see what's really happening on your site.

The free AI tools are tools too

ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and Copilot are free to try, and they're now part of how people find local businesses.

Ask them what your customers would ask: "who's good for drain cleaning in Hamilton?" See who gets named and which websites they cite. Our guide on checking whether AI recommends your business turns that into a proper test.

Where free stops being enough

Free tools tell you a lot. They don't tell you everything a local business needs.

They won't show how you rank on the map from different parts of Hamilton. The map pack changes as the searcher moves, so a business can rank well near its shop in Dundas and nowhere on the Mountain. That needs geo-grid tracking, which is a paid service. And no tool, free or paid, decides which fix matters most. That still takes judgement.

FAQ

What's the most useful free SEO tool for a small business?

Google Search Console for your website, and your Google Business Profile for Maps. Between them they cover most of what a local business needs to know, and both use Google's own data.

Are free keyword tools accurate?

They're estimates. They're useful for comparing one phrase with another, but the numbers can be far off. For your own site, trust Search Console, which measures real searches and clicks.

Do I need paid SEO tools?

Not to get started. An owner can do a lot with free tools. Paid tools earn their keep when you need to track rankings across a whole service area, watch many keywords or manage several locations.

Is Google Analytics the same as Search Console?

No. Search Console shows how people find you in Google Search, before they click. Analytics shows what they do after they land on your site. They answer different questions.
