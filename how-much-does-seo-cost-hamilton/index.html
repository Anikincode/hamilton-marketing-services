How much does SEO cost in Hamilton? What you're really paying for

There's no single price, and anyone who quotes one before looking at your business is guessing. Here's what moves the number, and what cheap SEO quietly leaves out.

Kirill Anikin · October 30, 2026 · 6 min read

Key takeaways

  • SEO has no fixed price. The cost depends on how much is broken, how many services and areas you need to cover, and how much of the work you do yourself.
  • Agencies charge in three main ways: a monthly retainer, a one-off project or an hourly rate.
  • Cheap SEO usually saves money by skipping the parts that bring in calls: real service pages, your Business Profile and honest tracking.
  • Walk away from guaranteed rankings, long lock-ins and any company that keeps your accounts in its own name.

Asking how much SEO costs is like asking how much a renovation costs. Swapping a bathroom tap and gutting a basement are both "renovations". Nobody sensible quotes either one over the phone.

SEO works the same way. The word covers everything from fixing one setting in an afternoon to building a website's worth of new pages. So instead of a made-up average, here's how the price actually gets built, and how to tell a fair quote from a cheap one.

Why there's no single price for SEO

Picture two Hamilton businesses. A plumber in Stoney Creek has a decent website, steady reviews and one problem: the main service pages have no headings, so Google isn't sure what they're about. That's a short, cheap fix.

Now picture a new contractor in Waterdown with no website, no reviews and a Business Profile set up in a hurry. That business needs pages written, a profile rebuilt and months of steady work.

Same word, "SEO". Very different job. That's why a fair price starts with a look at your business, not a menu.

What drives the cost of SEO

How much is broken right now

Fixing is usually cheaper than building, but someone has to find the problems first. And there are usually problems. When we scored 35 live local-business websites on our audit rubric on Sep 20, 2026, the average was 34 out of 100.

Some of what we find is quick to fix once it's spotted. One live site had a robots.txt file telling Google to ignore the whole site. Another had 9 of its 10 service pages missing a main heading. Neither needs a big budget. Both need someone looking.

How many services and areas you cover

Every service you want to rank for needs its own page, because every service is its own search. "Furnace repair Hamilton" and "AC installation Hamilton" are different people with different problems.

An HVAC company that does repairs, installs and duct cleaning across Hamilton Mountain, Stoney Creek and Ancaster needs far more pages than a home inspector who does one thing. More pages means more research, more writing and more upkeep.

How hard your searches are to win

Some trades are crowded. An emergency plumbing search on the Mountain has plenty of companies fighting for the three spots in the map pack. A niche service in Binbrook might have almost nobody. The harder the fight, the more work it takes to win and hold a spot.

The state of your website

A site that's slow on a phone, built on a cloned template or missing basic pages costs more to fix than a clean one. We've seen a site whose main content took 27.7 seconds to appear on a phone. No amount of keyword work fixes that on its own.

If your site needs replacing rather than repairing, that's a separate cost. Our guide to website design cost in Hamilton covers what changes that price.

How much you do yourself

You can cut the bill by doing the parts only you can do well: asking every customer for a review, sending photos of real jobs and answering questions about how you work. An agency that has to chase all of that will charge for the chasing.

How SEO is usually priced

Most SEO is sold in one of three ways:

  • Monthly retainer. Ongoing work for a monthly fee. Best when you want steady growth across several services or areas.
  • One-off project. A fixed job for a fixed price, like an audit, a cleanup or a set of new service pages.
  • Hourly. Advice and second opinions, usually for businesses that have their own web or marketing person.

Each model suits a different situation, and each has its own traps. Our guide to how SEO pricing works walks through the models and what you should get at each level.

Red flags in cheap SEO

Here's the problem with a very low price. It rarely means the same work for less. It usually means different work, and some of it can hurt you.

Guaranteed rankings

Google's own advice on hiring an SEO says nobody can guarantee a #1 ranking. A company that promises page one for "plumber Hamilton" by a set date is either guessing or planning to rank you for a search nobody makes.

Long lock-in contracts

A contract that ties you in for a long stretch, with a fee to leave, protects the agency, not you. Good work keeps clients without a lock.

You don't own your accounts

Your Google Business Profile, Search Console, analytics, ad account, website and domain should all be in your name. If an agency sets them up under its own login, leaving gets messy fast. Ask before you sign, not after.

Links by the bucketful

"Hundreds of backlinks a month" sounds like value. Links sold in bulk tend to come from sites nobody reads. We once had to build a disavow file for a small business site with a network of 60 spam domains pointing links at it. Cleaning up costs more than doing it right the first time.

Copy-and-paste pages

Cheap content is often a template with the city name swapped. We've found a paragraph naming a different Ontario city sitting on several service pages, and a fake phone number from a cloned template on 19 pages. A customer in Dundas who reads about the wrong town won't call.

Reports that measure the wrong thing

A report showing hundreds of "ranked keywords" tells you nothing if none of them bring calls. Watch for traffic numbers pulled from keyword tools, too. On one site, a keyword tool estimated traffic about five times higher than Search Console showed. Tools model. Search Console measures.

How to get a real number for your business

You'll get a far more useful answer by following a simple process:

  1. Write down what you want: more calls from Maps, a new site, or more work in one area like Ancaster or Dundas.
  2. Ask each company to look at your site and Business Profile before it quotes.
  3. Compare what's included, line by line, not just the bottom number.
  4. Ask everyone the same questions. Our list of questions to ask an SEO company is a good place to start.

Common questions about SEO cost

Is cheap SEO always a waste of money?

Not always. A small, cheap job can be exactly right, like fixing your Business Profile categories or rewriting weak page titles. The risk is cheap ongoing SEO that promises everything, because the low price has to come from somewhere.

Why do SEO quotes vary so much?

Because they're quoting different work. One quote might cover a page or two and a report. Another might cover your Business Profile, reviews, technical fixes and tracking across your whole service area. Compare the scope before the price.

Do I have to pay for SEO forever?

No. Once the foundations are in place, some businesses drop to lighter maintenance or an occasional project. But competitors keep working and rankings need protecting, so most businesses that rely on search keep some ongoing work.

Can I pay only when I rank?

Some companies offer that. Be careful: they choose which searches count, and it's easy to rank for searches nobody makes. Pay for work you can see, and judge it by calls and leads.
