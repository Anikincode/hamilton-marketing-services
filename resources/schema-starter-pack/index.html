Local business schema examples: a copy-ready starter pack

Four JSON-LD blocks a local service business can copy, edit and paste today, plus the rules that keep them honest and the free tools that check them.

Kirill Anikin · October 30, 2026 · 9 min read

Key takeaways

  • Schema is a label on your page that tells machines, in their own format, who you are and what the page is about.
  • Most local service businesses need four blocks: LocalBusiness (or a trade subtype), Service, FAQPage and BreadcrumbList.
  • Every value must match what's visible on the page and on your Business Profile. Schema that disagrees with your profile does more harm than good.
  • Service-area businesses that hide their address should only put the city, province and country in their schema.
  • Test every block with Google's free Rich Results Test before and after you publish.

Your website is written for people. Schema is the same information, written for machines.

Think of it as the label on a moving box. You can guess what's inside by shaking it, but a clear label saves everyone time. Schema (also called structured data) is that label. Search engines and AI assistants read it to understand who you are, what you do and where you work.

This local business schema example pack gives you four blocks to copy, with every field explained. They're written for a made-up plumbing company in Hamilton, so swap in your own details before you use them.

Before you paste anything: the rules

Schema is easy to add and easy to get wrong. These rules keep you out of trouble.

  • Match the page. Google's guidelines say structured data must describe content people can see on the page. Don't mark up services, questions or hours that aren't there.
  • Match your Business Profile. Same name, same phone number, same areas. If your schema says one thing and your profile says another, you've just given Google two versions of your business.
  • Never mark up your own review stars. Google doesn't show review stars for a local business that marks up reviews of itself. Copying your Google reviews into schema won't earn stars either, and it goes against Google's review markup guidelines.
  • Use JSON-LD. Google recommends this format. It's a block of text that sits in the page code and doesn't change what visitors see.
  • Replace every placeholder. The examples below use "yourbusiness.ca" and a 555 phone number on purpose. We've audited a site that had a fake phone number from a cloned template sitting on 19 pages. Don't let a template's details go live under your name.

Block 1: LocalBusiness for a service-area business

This is the main block. It describes your business as a whole, so it usually goes on your homepage and contact page.

The example uses the type "Plumber", which is a more specific kind of LocalBusiness. It's set up for a service-area business: the street address is left out because the business hides it on its Business Profile and visits customers instead.

<script type="application/ld+json">
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "Plumber",
  "@id": "https://www.yourbusiness.ca/#business",
  "name": "Your Business Name",
  "url": "https://www.yourbusiness.ca/",
  "telephone": "+1-905-555-0100",
  "email": "hello@yourbusiness.ca",
  "description": "Residential plumbing, drain cleaning and water heater repair across Hamilton, Stoney Creek and Ancaster.",
  "image": "https://www.yourbusiness.ca/images/your-team-photo.jpg",
  "logo": "https://www.yourbusiness.ca/images/your-logo.png",
  "address": {
    "@type": "PostalAddress",
    "addressLocality": "Hamilton",
    "addressRegion": "ON",
    "addressCountry": "CA"
  },
  "areaServed": [
    { "@type": "City", "name": "Hamilton, ON" },
    { "@type": "Place", "name": "Stoney Creek, Hamilton, ON" },
    { "@type": "Place", "name": "Ancaster, Hamilton, ON" },
    { "@type": "City", "name": "Burlington, ON" }
  ],
  "openingHoursSpecification": [
    {
      "@type": "OpeningHoursSpecification",
      "dayOfWeek": ["Monday", "Tuesday", "Wednesday", "Thursday", "Friday"],
      "opens": "08:00",
      "closes": "18:00"
    }
  ],
  "sameAs": [
    "https://maps.app.goo.gl/your-profile-share-link",
    "https://www.facebook.com/yourbusiness"
  ]
}
</script>

What each part does:

FieldWhat to put there
@typeThe most specific type that fits your trade. See the table below
@idA permanent name for your business inside your site: your homepage address plus "#business". Other blocks point back to it
nameYour real business name, exactly as it appears on your Business Profile. No added keywords
telephoneYour main local number, in international format: +1, then the area code and number
addressFor a service-area business, only the city, province and country. A storefront adds the street and postal code
areaServedThe places you really work. Use the same list as your Business Profile
openingHoursSpecificationYour real hours. Leave days out if you're closed
sameAsLinks to your official profiles: your Business Profile's share link from Google Maps, your Facebook page, and so on

Why only the city for a service-area business? Because your schema shouldn't publish what your profile hides. If a plumber working out of a house in Binbrook hides that address on Google, the website shouldn't print it in code. Our article on service-area businesses on Google Maps covers the profile side.

If you're open around the clock

An emergency plumber who really answers calls on the Mountain in the middle of the night can mark the business as open all day, every day. Google's documentation uses "00:00" to "23:59" for this. Swap this in for the hours section above, and only if it's true.

"openingHoursSpecification": [
  {
    "@type": "OpeningHoursSpecification",
    "dayOfWeek": ["Monday", "Tuesday", "Wednesday", "Thursday", "Friday", "Saturday", "Sunday"],
    "opens": "00:00",
    "closes": "23:59"
  }
]

If customers come to you

A storefront, like a restaurant on James Street North or a print shop near Ottawa Street, should include its full address. Replace the address section with this, using the exact address on your Business Profile:

"address": {
  "@type": "PostalAddress",
  "streetAddress": "Your street address and unit number",
  "addressLocality": "Hamilton",
  "addressRegion": "ON",
  "postalCode": "Your postal code",
  "addressCountry": "CA"
}

Which type should you use?

Schema.org has specific types for many trades. Use the most specific one that's accurate. If there isn't one, use the closest parent type.

Block 2: Service, one per service page

Every service page gets its own Service block. It says what this page sells, where, and who provides it.

Google doesn't show a special search result for Service markup. It's there to make the page's purpose impossible to misread, which matters more now that AI assistants summarize businesses for people. If you want the longer argument, read schema markup for AI search.

<script type="application/ld+json">
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "Service",
  "name": "Drain cleaning in Hamilton",
  "serviceType": "Drain cleaning",
  "url": "https://www.yourbusiness.ca/drain-cleaning-hamilton/",
  "description": "Clogged and slow drains cleared for homes across Hamilton, from the lower city to the Mountain.",
  "provider": {
    "@type": "Plumber",
    "@id": "https://www.yourbusiness.ca/#business",
    "name": "Your Business Name"
  },
  "areaServed": { "@type": "City", "name": "Hamilton, ON" }
}
</script>

Notice the provider section. It repeats the business's @id from Block 1, so the two blocks connect: this service belongs to that business. Keep one service per block and one block per page, the same way you keep one service per page.

Block 3: FAQPage, only where the page has a FAQ

If a page ends with real questions and answers, FAQPage markup labels them as questions and answers.

Be realistic about what it does. Google now shows FAQ rich results only for a small set of well-known government and health websites. A plumber in Stoney Creek won't get the dropdowns in Google's results. The markup still states the questions and answers plainly for any system that reads structured data.

<script type="application/ld+json">
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "FAQPage",
  "mainEntity": [
    {
      "@type": "Question",
      "name": "Do you clear drains on Hamilton Mountain?",
      "acceptedAnswer": {
        "@type": "Answer",
        "text": "Yes. We clear drains across the Mountain, the lower city, Stoney Creek and Ancaster."
      }
    },
    {
      "@type": "Question",
      "name": "Do I get a price before you start?",
      "acceptedAnswer": {
        "@type": "Answer",
        "text": "Yes. You get a quote before any work begins, and nothing changes without your OK."
      }
    }
  ]
}
</script>

The questions and answers in the markup must match the ones on the page, word for word. Don't add questions to the schema that visitors can't see.

Block 4: BreadcrumbList

Breadcrumbs are the "Home / Services / Drain cleaning" trail at the top of a page. The markup describes that trail, which helps Google understand how your pages fit together.

<script type="application/ld+json">
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "BreadcrumbList",
  "itemListElement": [
    { "@type": "ListItem", "position": 1, "name": "Home", "item": "https://www.yourbusiness.ca/" },
    { "@type": "ListItem", "position": 2, "name": "Services", "item": "https://www.yourbusiness.ca/services/" },
    { "@type": "ListItem", "position": 3, "name": "Drain cleaning", "item": "https://www.yourbusiness.ca/drain-cleaning-hamilton/" }
  ]
}
</script>

Put it on every page below the homepage. The trail in the markup should match the breadcrumb people see.

Where the code goes

A JSON-LD block can sit in the head or the body of the page. Separate blocks on one page are fine.

  • Wix and several other website builders let you add custom code or structured data in a page's settings.
  • WordPress sites usually add it through an SEO plugin or a small code snippet.
  • Custom or static sites can paste it straight into the page template.

One warning: many platforms and plugins already add some schema on their own. Check what's there before you add more, so you don't end up with two LocalBusiness blocks that disagree.

How to test it

  1. Paste the page address into Google's Rich Results Test. It shows what Google can read and flags errors.
  2. Run the same page through the Schema Markup Validator, which checks the schema itself, not only the parts Google uses.
  3. Read the output against your Business Profile. Same name, same phone, same areas.
  4. After publishing, check Search Console for any structured data errors it reports.

Google's own local business structured data guide lists the properties it supports, and it's the source to trust if anything here ever falls out of date.

Common mistakes we see

  • The wrong city. A page about Ancaster with schema that says Hamilton Mountain, because it was copied from another page.
  • A different phone number from the one on the Business Profile. Consistency is the whole point. More in NAP consistency and citations.
  • Hours that promise too much. Marking yourself open around the clock because a competitor does.
  • Review stars from your own reviews. Covered above. Don't.
  • Set and forget. You change your hours or add a service, and the schema still describes the business as it was years ago.

FAQ

Does schema markup improve my rankings?

Google says structured data helps it understand a page, but it doesn't promise rankings or special results for it. Think of schema as removing doubt, not as a shortcut. It works best on a site that's already clear and accurate.

Do I need schema if I already have a Google Business Profile?

Yes. Your profile describes the business on Google Maps. Schema describes it on your own website, in a format any search engine or AI tool can read. When the two agree, both are easier to trust.

Should every page have LocalBusiness schema?

Put the full LocalBusiness block on your homepage and contact page. On service pages, use a Service block that points back to the business with the same @id. That keeps one clear description of the business instead of many slightly different copies.

I hide my address on Google. What do I put in the address field?

Only the city, province and country, as in Block 1. List the places you serve in areaServed. Never publish an address in your schema that you've hidden on your profile.

Can I get review stars in Google for my business?

Not by marking up your own reviews. Google doesn't show those stars for a local business reviewing itself. Your stars show where Google already shows them: on your Business Profile.
