What does an SEO company do? The work, month by month

You pay every month. So what actually happens in those months? Here's the work behind local SEO, in the order it usually gets done.

Kirill Anikin · October 30, 2026 · 5 min read

Key takeaways

  • An SEO company makes your site easy for Google to read, relevant to what your customers search and trusted enough to rank.
  • The first months are mostly fixing and planning: your Business Profile, technical problems, research and page structure.
  • Then come new pages and Business Profile work, followed by monthly maintenance that protects what you've earned.
  • Every month, you should get a plain-English report on what was done and what changed.

You pay an SEO company every month. Then a report shows up, full of charts. What happened in between?

It's a fair question, and too many agencies dodge it. Here's what the work looks like, month by month, using the same steps we follow ourselves.

The short answer

An SEO company does three jobs:

  1. Make your site readable. Google has to be able to find, load and understand every page.
  2. Make it relevant. Every service you sell needs a page and a place on your Business Profile, so you match what people search.
  3. Make it trusted. Reviews, real links and consistent details tell Google you're a real business worth showing.

Then it measures what changed and protects what's working. Everything below fits into one of those jobs.

The timing depends on your site. A small, healthy site moves faster. A big cleanup takes longer. The order matters more than the dates.

Month one: audit and fix the foundations

Check the Business Profile categories

We start with your Google Business Profile, because the primary category decides which searches you can show up for. A heating company listed under a general category can struggle to appear for "furnace repair Hamilton", however good its website is.

Crawl the website

Next comes a full crawl of your site, page by page. This is where the quiet problems turn up. In our own audits we've found a robots.txt file blocking a whole site, service pages with no main heading, wrong cities in page titles and a site so slow that its main content took 9.9 seconds to appear on a phone.

Set up measurement

Google Search Console gets connected, and calls and form leads need to be countable. Without that, nobody can tell you later whether anything worked. It's the same reason we check ad accounts for conversion tracking: we've seen Google Ads running with none, so nobody could tell which clicks became calls.

Month two: research and plan

Find the gaps against competitors

We compare your site with the businesses beating you on your main searches. Which services do they have pages for that you don't? That gap is usually the first list of pages to build.

Group searches by intent

Searches get sorted by what the person wants. "Emergency furnace repair Hamilton Mountain" is someone with a cold house tonight. "Best time to replace a furnace" is someone planning ahead. They need different pages.

Mine the questions people ask

Google's "People also ask" boxes show the questions customers type before they pick up the phone. Those questions shape what each page covers.

Plan the page structure

All of that turns into a map: one page per service and intent, with clear links between them. A plumber might end up with separate pages for drain cleaning, water heaters and emergency calls, instead of one long "Services" page.

Month three onward: build

Write the pages

Pages get written to a set formula: the service, the area, the questions customers ask and the facts only you know, like how you price and how quickly you can get to Waterdown. You review each page before it goes live.

Work the Business Profile

Services, photos and regular posts go on your profile. We schedule daily Business Profile posts for clients who want them. And every business needs a simple, steady way to ask customers for reviews.

Fix your details across the web

Your name, address and phone number get checked and corrected across directories, so Google sees one consistent business instead of three slightly different ones.

Every month after that: maintain and protect

This is the part most people never see, and it's where rankings are kept or lost.

The click-through check

Each month we look in Search Console for pages ranking in positions 4 to 10 with weak clicks. Those pages are close, and the fix is often a better title. We rewrite it, then recheck after 30 days.

We've seen this on our own site. One page sat at average position 3 to 5 with zero clicks from 233 impressions. The ranking was fine. The title wasn't.

Tracking across your area

Map rankings change from street to street. We track them across your service area with geo-grid rank tracking, so you know whether you show up on the Mountain, in the lower city and out in Stoney Creek, not just from one spot.

Defensive SEO

Rankings can slip without anyone doing anything "wrong". A page gets deleted. A redirect breaks. Someone edits a title. A spammy site starts linking to you. Defensive SEO means watching for those changes and fixing them before they cost calls.

What you should get each month

A report you can read over a coffee: what was done, what changed in calls and rankings, what didn't work and what's next. If something didn't move, a good SEO company says so.

What an SEO company can't do

  • Guarantee rankings. Google says nobody can promise a #1 spot.
  • Delete honest bad reviews. You can reply to them, and report the ones that break Google's rules. That's it.
  • Fake a location. A made-up address breaks Google's Business Profile rules. If you're not based in Grimsby, a good company won't pretend you are.
  • Answer your phone. SEO brings the call. Winning the job is still up to you.

Common questions about what SEO companies do

Do I have to do anything while the SEO company works?

Yes, a little. Answer questions about your services, approve new pages, send photos of real jobs and ask customers for reviews. The more of your real knowledge goes in, the better the pages come out.

What's the difference between an SEO company and a web designer?

A web designer builds the site. An SEO company makes sure people find it. The two overlap a lot, which is why some businesses use one company for both. See SEO and web design: one company or two?

Why does SEO need to be monthly?

Because search doesn't stand still. Competitors add pages, Google changes its results and things on your own site break. Monthly work keeps your gains and builds on them.

Can an SEO company work on my existing website?

Usually, yes. We build on Wix and as fast static sites, and work on WordPress. What matters is being able to edit titles, headings, pages and redirects.
