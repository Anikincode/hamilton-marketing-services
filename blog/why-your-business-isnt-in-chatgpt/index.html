Why isn't my business in ChatGPT? The usual causes, and the fixes

Your competitor gets named and you don't. It's rarely bad luck. It's usually one of a short list of problems, and most of them are fixable.

Kirill Anikin · October 30, 2026 · 6 min read

Key takeaways

  • ChatGPT can only name businesses it finds in its searches and can read clearly. Most "invisible" businesses fail one of those two tests.
  • The usual causes are blocked or unindexed pages, a site that doesn't clearly say what you do, wrong details, mismatched listings and too few mentions elsewhere.
  • We find these in real audits: a robots.txt blocking a whole live site, template text naming the wrong city, two businesses sharing one phone number.
  • Fix them in order. There's no point polishing pages an AI can't reach.

You've been fixing furnaces in Stoney Creek for years. Your reviews are good. Your customers come back.

Then someone shows you ChatGPT's answer to "best furnace repair Stoney Creek", and your competitor is in it. You're not.

So why isn't your business in ChatGPT? It's rarely random. Here are the causes we actually find, roughly in the order you should check them.

First, make sure it's a real pattern

One missing answer isn't proof of anything. AI answers change with the wording, the person's location and from one try to the next.

Before you fix anything, test properly: many questions, several tools, a clean session. Our guide on how to check whether AI recommends your business sets that up in an afternoon. If you're consistently missing, read on.

1. Your site is blocked or not indexed

This is the cause to rule out first, because nothing else matters until it's fixed.

In one audit we found a live business website whose robots.txt file blocked all indexing. In plain English, the site was telling every search engine to stay out. Every page, every service, invisible.

AI tools have their own crawlers too. OpenAI says its OAI-SearchBot crawler is what surfaces websites in ChatGPT's search answers, and its crawler documentation explains how to allow or block it. A robots.txt rule or a security setting that blocks it can keep you out of those answers.

Indexing is the other half. On one site we checked, Google had indexed about 5 of its 48 pages. When a search engine ignores most of a site, that's a strong hint the pages look thin or repetitive. Our article on common robots.txt mistakes covers the blocking side in detail.

2. Your website doesn't clearly say what you do

Many local websites have one "Services" page with a bulleted list. Furnace repair, AC installation, duct cleaning, water heaters, all in one place, a line each.

When ChatGPT searches "heat pump installation Waterdown", that list has nothing to offer. There's no page about heat pumps, nothing about Waterdown, no detail worth quoting.

Structure matters as well. One business we audited had 9 of its 10 service pages missing an H1, the main heading that tells people and machines what a page is about. It was still unfixed after four audits. Pages like that leave a reader guessing.

The fix is one page per service, each with a clear heading, a direct answer near the top and the areas you serve written out in sentences.

3. Your website says the wrong things

Sometimes the problem isn't missing information. It's wrong information that nobody noticed.

Here's what we've found on live local sites:

  • A paragraph naming a different Ontario city, left over from a template, on several service pages.
  • A project page whose body was copied from a template written for a company in another country.
  • A fake phone number from a cloned template, on 19 pages.
  • Titles and main headings naming the wrong neighbourhood.

Now picture an AI reading those pages. Which city do you serve? Which phone number is real? When a site contradicts itself, the safest move for an AI is to recommend someone whose story is clear.

4. Your details don't match across the web

AI tools don't just read your website. They read your Google Business Profile, Bing and Apple Maps listings, directories, social profiles and anything else that mentions you.

If those disagree, you look less certain. An old address on one directory. A former business name on another. A phone number that changed and only got updated in half the places.

The worst version we've seen: two separate businesses sharing one phone number across their listings. To a system trying to work out which business is which, that's a reason to doubt both.

5. Nobody else is talking about you

Your website is your side of the story. AI tools look for other people backing it up.

That means reviews, directory listings, local news, supplier pages, association member lists and local roundups. A Binbrook contractor with a good website and no outside mentions is a harder recommendation than one that shows up in several independent places.

This one takes time. But it compounds, and it helps your Google rankings too.

6. You don't rank for the searches ChatGPT actually runs

This is the cause most people never think of.

When a customer asks ChatGPT a long question, it often turns that question into shorter web searches of its own. The customer never sees those searches. If your pages don't show up for them, you won't be in the answer, however good your site is.

We use a tool we built that reveals those hidden searches. Once we know what ChatGPT is searching for in your trade and area, the job becomes clear: build or fix the pages that should rank for each one.

7. You're new

A business that opened last spring has few reviews, little history and a new domain. AI tools, like search engines, have less to go on.

That's not a flaw you can fix overnight. But there's a right order to build things in, and we cover it in SEO for a new business.

Fix it in this order

  1. Access. Make sure search engines and AI crawlers can reach and index your site.
  2. Accuracy. Remove template leftovers, wrong cities, fake numbers and contradictions.
  3. Clarity. One page per service, clear headings, direct answers, areas written out.
  4. Consistency. Identical name, phone, address or service area and hours everywhere.
  5. Reputation. Steady reviews and genuine mentions on other sites.
  6. Coverage. Rank for the hidden searches ChatGPT runs for your trade and area.

The first two are usually technical SEO work, and they're often quick. The rest builds over months.

FAQ

I'm on Google's first page. Why am I not in ChatGPT?

ChatGPT often searches different phrases from the one you rank for, and it reads several sources before it answers. You may rank for "furnace repair Hamilton" but not for the shorter searches it runs, or your details may not match elsewhere.

Does blocking GPTBot keep me out of ChatGPT?

OpenAI separates its crawlers. It describes GPTBot as the crawler for training its models, and OAI-SearchBot as the one that surfaces sites in ChatGPT's search answers. Check which ones your robots.txt allows before assuming either way.

Can I just tell ChatGPT about my business?

Telling it in your own chat doesn't change what it says to other people. The way in is through the open web: your site, your listings, your reviews and the pages that mention you.
