SEO and web design: should one company do both?

Your website and your SEO are built from the same pieces. Here's when it pays to have one company handle both, and how to make it work if you use two.

Kirill Anikin · October 30, 2026 · 5 min read

Key takeaways

  • Web design and SEO overlap on almost every decision: which pages exist, titles, headings, speed, structured data and redirects.
  • With two vendors, the usual failures are deleted pages with no redirect, launch settings that block Google and leftover template text.
  • One vendor gives you one person accountable. Two vendors can work well with clear rules about who owns what.
  • If you use two, the SEO side should sign off on the page list before design starts and check redirects before launch.

Building a website and then hiring someone to "add the SEO" is like building a house and then calling the electrician. Possible, sure. But someone's cutting into finished drywall.

Lots of local websites are built by one company and optimized by another. When rankings drop after a launch, each one points at the other. Here's how to avoid that, whichever way you go.

Why web design and SEO can't really be separated

Almost every design decision is also an SEO decision:

  • Which pages exist. One page per service is how you rank for each service. A designer who merges "drain cleaning" and "water heaters" onto one page to tidy the menu has just made both harder to rank.
  • Titles and headings. We once audited a business with 9 of 10 service pages missing a main heading. It looked fine to a visitor. Google saw pages without a clear topic.
  • Speed. Big images and heavy effects look great on a desktop and crawl on a phone. One site we audited took 27.7 seconds to show its main content on mobile.
  • Structured data. The code that tells Google your business name, area and services is easiest to add while the site is being built.
  • The addresses of old pages. Every page that already ranks lives at an address. Change the address without a redirect, and the ranking has nowhere to go.

What goes wrong with two vendors

None of these failures need anyone to be careless on purpose. They happen in the gap between two jobs.

Pages deleted with no redirect

The designer cleans up the menu and drops an old page. Nobody tells the SEO company. We've seen the result: a page deleted with no redirect, even though it ranked for a search with real monthly volume.

A setting that blocks Google

Sites in progress are often hidden from Google on purpose. If that setting survives launch day, Google is told to ignore the live site. We've audited a live site whose robots.txt blocked all indexing.

Leftover template text

Templates come with sample words, and sample words get forgotten. We've found a project page whose body was copied from a template for a company in another country, and a fake phone number from a cloned template on 19 pages.

Two pages fighting over one search

A new page goes up for the main service, and nobody checks it against the homepage. On one site, a new page "replaced" the homepage on six searches, and all six dropped. Our article on keyword cannibalization explains why.

The case for one vendor

  • One person accountable. If rankings drop, there's nobody else to point at.
  • Built to rank from the first day. Page structure, speed and schema are decided before the design, not patched in afterwards.
  • Redesigns that respect your traffic. The people who know which pages rank are the same people moving them.
  • Faster fixes. One call, one change, no waiting on another company's schedule.

We build sites on Wix and as fast static sites, and work on WordPress, so we can handle both halves. If you're on Wix, our page on Wix SEO covers that platform's quirks.

The case for two vendors

One vendor isn't always right. Two can make sense when:

  • You already have a web designer you trust, and they're happy to work alongside an SEO company.
  • Your business sells on looks, like an interior designer in Ancaster, and you want a design specialist for the visuals.
  • You're partway through a website contract you can't change.

How to make two vendors work

If you go with two, put these rules in writing before work starts:

  1. The SEO side signs off on the page list before any design work begins.
  2. No page is deleted or renamed without a redirect and a heads-up to the SEO side.
  3. A redirect map is checked before launch, matching every old address to a new one.
  4. Someone checks robots.txt and indexing settings on launch day.
  5. One owner for titles, descriptions and headings, so they don't get overwritten.
  6. Both vendors can see Search Console, so problems show up for everyone.

Our guide to redesigning a website without losing rankings walks through the launch steps in detail.

Questions to ask any web designer about SEO

Whether you hire one company or two, these questions show quickly whether search has been thought about. Ask them before you sign.

  • Will each service get its own page? For a contractor, "basement renovations" and "kitchen renovations" should be separate pages, not two lines on one.
  • Who writes the titles and headings? If nobody can answer, they'll end up as "Home" and "Services".
  • How fast will it load on a phone, and how will you check? PageSpeed Insights is free, so there's no reason not to test before launch.
  • Will you add structured data? Your name, phone number, service area and hours, labelled in code.
  • What happens to my old page addresses? The only good answer involves a redirect map.
  • How will you make sure Google can see the site on launch day? Listen for robots.txt and Search Console in the answer.
  • Who owns the site and the domain? It should be you.

Common questions about SEO and web design

Will a new website hurt my Google rankings?

It can, if pages are deleted, renamed without redirects or launched with Google blocked. Done carefully, a redesign keeps what you've earned, and a faster, clearer site gives you room to improve.

Should I redesign before starting SEO?

Not always. If the site is very slow, broken or built on a cloned template, rebuild first. If it's basically sound, fixing titles, headings and your Business Profile can do more than starting over.

Can you do SEO on a site someone else built?

Yes, as long as we can edit pages, titles, headings and redirects. We work on WordPress, and build on Wix and as fast static sites.

How much does a website cost?

It depends on the number of pages, the platform, who writes the copy and where the photos come from. Our guide to website design cost in Hamilton explains each one.
