Service area businesses on Google Maps: hide your address, still get found

If you drive to your customers, Google treats you differently from a shop with a front door. Here's how to set up your profile properly, and why your service area alone won't win you the whole city.

Kirill Anikin · October 30, 2026 · 6 min read

Key takeaways

  • If customers don't come to your address, Google says you should hide it and list the areas you serve instead.
  • Hiding the address hides it from the public. Your business is still tied to the location you verified.
  • Listing a town as a service area doesn't, on its own, make you rank there. Distance from the searcher still counts.
  • Virtual offices, P.O. boxes and fake addresses in other towns break Google's guidelines.
  • To reach further, build real service pages, earn reviews across your area and track rankings with a geo-grid.

You're a plumber. You work out of a van. Nobody ever comes to your house, and you'd rather they didn't. So why does Google keep asking for your address?

Because Google needs to know where your business is based, even if customers never see it. The good news: you can hide it. The catch: hiding it doesn't work the way most people expect.

What is a service-area business?

Google splits local businesses into three rough types:

  • Storefront: customers come to you. A restaurant on James Street North, an auto shop on Upper James, a print shop near Ottawa Street.
  • Service-area business: you go to the customer. Plumbers, HVAC techs, cleaners, landscapers, movers, home inspectors.
  • Hybrid: both. An appliance repair company with a parts counter customers can visit, whose techs also do house calls.

If you're a service-area business, your Google Business Profile should be set up differently from a storefront. A lot of the confusion we see comes from treating a van-based business like a shop.

What Google allows (and what it doesn't)

Google's Business Profile guidelines are fairly clear on the basics:

  • If you don't serve customers at your address, hide it. Leave the address off your public profile and list your service areas instead.
  • You still need a real location. Google verifies where the business is based, even when the public can't see it.
  • No P.O. boxes, and no virtual offices unless they're staffed during your business hours.
  • No fake addresses. Renting a mailbox in Ancaster to "be in Ancaster" breaks the rules.
  • One profile per real business. Separate profiles for Hamilton, Burlington and Grimsby without a real, staffed location in each can get your listings suspended.
  • Keep the service area realistic. Google's guidelines limit how far your service area can stretch from where you're based. Listing all of Southern Ontario doesn't help.

Verification is up to Google. It may ask a service-area business to verify with a short video showing things like the vehicle, tools or workspace, so have those ready.

How to set it up

  1. In your Business Profile, open the location settings and choose not to show your address to customers.
  2. Add your service areas. Use the places you really work: Hamilton, Stoney Creek, Ancaster, Dundas, Waterdown, Binbrook, Burlington. You can pick cities and areas from Google's suggestions.
  3. Use your real business name with nothing added. "Plumber Hamilton Mountain" isn't a business name, and Google's guidelines ban stuffing keywords into it.
  4. Set a local phone number that only this business uses.
  5. Match your website. Your site should show your name, phone and the areas you serve. It shouldn't print the home address you just hid.

What hiding your address actually does

This is where people get surprised.

Hiding your address hides it from the public. That's all. Google still knows where your business is based, because that's the location you verified.

And Google says local results are based on three things: relevance, distance and prominence. Distance means how far the business is from the person searching, or from the place named in their search.

So listing "Burlington" as a service area puts Burlington on your profile. It doesn't move your business to Burlington. If you're based in Binbrook, a homeowner searching from Binbrook or Mount Hope is close to you. A homeowner searching from downtown Burlington is not.

Think of your service area as the delivery zone printed on a pizza menu. It tells people where you'll go. It doesn't put your shop on their street.

What that looks like across Hamilton

Hamilton is a big, spread-out city, and the escarpment splits it in two. A business based on the Mountain and one based in the lower city can see very different map results, even a short drive apart.

Picture a landscaper based in Binbrook. On searches made around Binbrook, Mount Hope and the south Mountain, they may show up well. In Dundas or Waterdown, they're competing with businesses that are physically much closer to the searcher.

That isn't a failure. It's how the map works. The question is what you do about it.

The only way to see this clearly is to check rankings from many points across your area, not just your own phone. That's what geo-grid rank tracking shows.

How to get found beyond your base

You can't change distance. You can strengthen everything else.

Build real service pages

Your website can rank in the regular results across a wider area than your map listing. And Google says your position in web results also feeds into how it judges your business for local results. Start with one strong page per service. Our guide to service pages that rank shows the formula.

Add area pages only when you have something real to say

A page for "drain cleaning in Waterdown" can work if it shows real jobs, local detail and a reason to exist. A copy of your Hamilton page with the town name swapped in won't. If you can't make it genuinely different, don't build it.

Earn reviews from across your area

Ask every customer, wherever they are. Over time, reviews from customers in Ancaster and Stoney Creek show that you really work there. Just don't script what they write.

Keep your details identical everywhere

For a service-area business, consistency means your name, phone number and service area match on your profile, website and directories. Some directories insist on an address. Use their option to hide it where they offer one. Our article on NAP consistency and citations walks through it.

Use ads for the areas the map won't give you yet

Search ads can target any area you choose. For the trades, Local Services Ads are another option in Canada. If Burlington is important to you and you're based on the Mountain, ads can cover it while your organic presence grows. See Google Ads for Hamilton businesses.

Mistakes that get service-area businesses in trouble

  • Showing a home address you didn't mean to publish, on the profile or the website.
  • Fake offices: a mailbox or an unstaffed desk in each town to get a pin there.
  • Keyword names: "Emergency Plumber Stoney Creek" as a business name.
  • A service area wider than you really serve, which just brings calls you'll turn down.
  • A website that disagrees with the profile about where you work.

FAQ

Does hiding my address hurt my ranking?

Hiding it changes what the public sees, not where Google understands you to be based. If customers don't come to you, hiding it is what Google's guidelines ask for.

Can I rank in Burlington if I'm based in Hamilton?

On the map, it's harder, because distance from the searcher counts. In the regular results, a strong, genuinely useful page for your service in Burlington can rank. Ads can fill the gap in the meantime.

Can I use a co-working space or virtual office as my address?

Google's guidelines don't allow virtual offices unless they're staffed during your business hours. If you're unsure whether your space qualifies, read Google's Business Profile guidelines before you list it.

How many service areas should I list?

The places you actually serve, within a reasonable distance of your base. There's no prize for listing more, and an inflated list just brings calls you'll turn away.

Should I make one profile for each city I work in?

No. One real business gets one profile, unless it has a real, staffed location in another city. Duplicate profiles for towns you only drive to can get your listings suspended.
