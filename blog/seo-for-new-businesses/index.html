SEO for a new business: what to do first when Google doesn't know you yet

You've opened your doors, or your van's doors. You have a phone number and maybe a website. Google has no idea you exist. Here's what to do first, in order.

Kirill Anikin · October 30, 2026 · 6 min read

Key takeaways

  • A new business starts with no reviews, no history and a new domain. Google has little reason to trust it yet, so the early work is about proving you're real.
  • Settle your name, phone and service area first, then set up your Google Business Profile with the right primary category.
  • Build a simple website with one page per service, connect Search Console, and get your first reviews from real customers.
  • Use Google Ads, or Local Services Ads if you're in the trades, for early calls while SEO builds.

You've just started a cleaning company in Waterdown, or a renovation business in Ancaster. You're good at the work. You've got your first few customers through friends and neighbours.

Now you search for your own service on Google, and you're nowhere. Not on the map, not on page one, not on page five.

That's normal. Here's what SEO for a new business looks like, and what to do first.

Why SEO is harder when you're new

Google is trying to recommend businesses its users can trust. A new business gives it very little to go on.

  • No reviews. Google says review count and rating are part of how it ranks local results.
  • No history. Your Business Profile and website have no track record.
  • A new domain. No other websites link to you or mention you yet.

Think of it like a new restaurant on James Street North. The food might be the best in the city, but until people start talking about it, most diners walk past to the place with the queue.

The good news: everything that builds trust can be started on day one. You just have to do it in the right order.

Step 1: Settle your name, phone number and service area

Before you list your business anywhere, decide exactly how it will appear everywhere.

  • Your name: your real business name, without extra keywords. Google's Business Profile guidelines ban stuffing words like "Best Plumber Hamilton" into the name.
  • Your phone number: one number, used everywhere. A local 905, 289 or 365 number reads as local to Hamilton customers.
  • Your service area: the places you really go. Hamilton, Stoney Creek and Binbrook, for example, if that's where you work.

Get this right now and you'll never have to clean it up later. We've audited two separate businesses sharing one phone number across their listings. Untangling that is far harder than avoiding it.

Step 2: Set up your Google Business Profile properly

For most local trades, your Business Profile matters more than your website at the start. It's what appears in the map pack.

Pick the right primary category. It decides which searches you can show up for. A heating company listed as a general contractor will struggle to show for "furnace repair Stoney Creek", however good the rest of its profile is. Add secondary categories only for services you actually provide.

List real services. We've audited a profile with one category and a list of stock services the business didn't sell. That tells Google the wrong story from the start.

Don't fake an address. If you work from home and go to customers, you're a service-area business, and Google lets you hide your address. Google's guidelines ban virtual offices and fake addresses used to appear somewhere you're not. Our Google Business Profile optimization service covers the full setup, and you can read Google's own guidelines for representing your business.

Step 3: Build a simple website that says what you do

You don't need a big website. You need a clear one.

  • A homepage that says what you do and where, in the first lines.
  • One page per main service. "Drain cleaning" and "water heater installation" are different searches, so they need different pages.
  • A contact page with the same name, phone and service area as your Business Profile.
  • Fast on a phone. Plenty of local searches happen on a phone, often in a hurry.

If you start from a template, read every word before launch. In our audits we've found template text left over from other businesses: a paragraph naming a different Ontario city on several service pages, and a fake phone number from a cloned template on 19 pages. Google reads all of it.

If you'd rather have it built properly from day one, that's what our web design service does: one page per service, fast on mobile, with structured data in place.

Step 4: Connect Google Search Console

Search Console is Google's free tool for website owners. Set it up as soon as your site is live and submit your sitemap.

It tells you whether Google has found and indexed your pages, and later, which searches you're starting to appear for. For a new site, the first job is simply confirming that your pages are being indexed at all.

Step 5: Get your first reviews the right way

Reviews are the fastest trust signal a new business can build.

  • Ask every happy customer, right after the job, while they're pleased.
  • Make it easy. Send the direct review link from your Business Profile by text or email.
  • Reply to every review, good or bad.
  • Don't pay for reviews or offer discounts in exchange. Google's rules ban it.
  • Don't only ask the people you think are happy. Google's rules also ban selectively asking for positive reviews.

Our guide on how to get more Google reviews has the practical details.

Step 6: List your business in the main places

Once your Google profile is set, claim your free listings on Bing Places and Apple Business Connect, plus the directories and associations that matter in your trade.

Use exactly the same name, phone and service area every time. These listings help customers find you, and they help search engines and AI tools confirm you're real.

Step 7: Use ads to bridge the gap

SEO takes months to build. You need calls now.

Google Ads can put you at the top of the results within days, and Local Services Ads, which in Canada cover the trades only, charge per lead. Set up conversion tracking first, so you know which ads bring calls. We've seen ad accounts running without it, and nobody could tell what was working.

We compare the two properly in SEO vs Google Ads.

What not to do in your first year

  • Don't buy cheap link packages or "submission to hundreds of directories". At best they do nothing. At worst you inherit a mess to clean up.
  • Don't buy fake reviews. They can be removed, and they put your profile at risk.
  • Don't build dozens of near-identical town pages with the city name swapped. Google treats pages made only to rank for place names as spam.
  • Don't add numbers you can't back up. Impressive-looking figures with nothing behind them are a risk, especially before you pay for traffic.
  • Don't expect instant results. Anyone promising page one by next month is selling something else.

FAQ

How long does SEO take for a new business?

Expect months, not weeks. A properly set-up Business Profile can start showing for nearby searches fairly quickly. Ranking pages and building reviews takes longer, which is why ads often fill the gap at the start.

Can I set up a Google Business Profile before I have a website?

Yes. You can create and verify a Business Profile without a website. Add the website link once it's live, and make sure the name, phone and service area match.

Can I rank on Google Maps without an office?

Yes. Service-area businesses can hide their address and list the areas they serve instead. What you can't do is use a fake or virtual address to appear somewhere you aren't.

Is SEO worth it for a brand-new business?

Usually, if you plan to be around for years. But the timing matters, and sometimes ads should come first. Our guide is SEO worth it? walks through when it pays and when it doesn't yet.
