Ecommerce SEO

Ecommerce SEO in Hamilton for small online stores

Category pages that match what people search, product pages Google can tell apart, and the duplicate-content mess cleaned up. Built for small stores, not giant catalogues.

Who this is for

SEO for small online stores, not giant catalogues.

Picture a Hamilton print shop that takes banner orders online, a supplier with a modest catalogue, or a local brand shipping across Canada. That's who this page is for.

If you run a huge catalogue with its own development team, you need a specialist ecommerce agency. If you have a small store, a busy week and no idea why Google ignores half your products, keep reading.

Small stores tend to stall for the same three reasons.

Category pages with no words

A grid of product photos gives Google almost nothing to rank. Yet the category page is the one that matches what most shoppers search.

Copied descriptions

Paste in the manufacturer's text and your page reads the same as every other store selling that product. Google has no reason to pick yours.

The same product at many addresses

Variants, filters and categories quietly create copies of your pages. Google wastes its visits on the copies and misses the real ones.

Category pages

Category pages win the bigger searches.

Here's what most store owners miss. People rarely search for one exact product. They search for a type: "custom vinyl banners", "used large-format printers", "gift boxes Hamilton".

That search matches a category page, not a product page. And most category pages are a wall of thumbnails with nothing to read.

We give each important category a title and H1 that match the search, a short intro that answers what a buyer wants to know, links to subcategories and best sellers, and a few answers to the questions people really ask.

One category, one search

"Custom banners" and "vehicle decals" are different searches. If one category tries to cover both, it usually ranks for neither.

It's the same rule we use on service pages: one page, one intent. Why two pages competing for one search both lose.

Product pages

Product pages Google can tell apart.

A product page has two jobs: rank for the specific searches, and convince the buyer once they land. These are the basics we fix first.

Your own descriptions

Start with your best sellers. Say who the product is for, what it's made of, and what buyers ask before they order.

Titles that match how people search

Product name plus the detail buyers type: size, material, colour or model number.

Real reviews only

Product reviews help buyers and can show as stars in search results. They must come from real customers. Never paste them in by hand.

Useful image alt text

Describe what's actually in the photo. It helps image search and people using screen readers.

Fast on a phone

Huge photos and too many add-ons slow product pages down, and shoppers leave before the price loads.

Sold-out products handled right

Coming back? Keep the page live. Gone for good? Redirect it to the closest product or category instead of leaving an error page.

Duplicate content

Why online stores create duplicate content without trying.

Stores copy themselves. Not on purpose. The platform does it while you're busy filling orders.

Google then has to guess which version to show, and its limited attention goes to the copies instead of your real pages.

We've seen a site where Google indexed about 5 of its 48 pages. The rest sat in Search Console as "Discovered – currently not indexed". Stores hit the same wall when copies crowd out the pages that matter. What "Discovered – currently not indexed" means and how to fix it.

The fixes are mostly technical: canonical tags that point copies to the main version, keeping filter and search pages out of the index, and a clean sitemap. Our technical SEO work covers the rest.

Where the copies come from

  • Variants. One shirt in several colours can turn into several URLs.
  • Filters and sorting. Every mix of size, price and colour can make a new address.
  • Several categories. A product listed in more than one category can get a path through each.
  • Internal search. Your store's own search results pages, open for Google to crawl.

Product schema

Product schema: tell Google the price, stock and reviews.

Schema is the label on the back of the box. Shoppers never see it, but Google reads it.

Product structured data tells Google the product name, price, currency, availability and, if you have real ones, reviews. It's what makes richer product listings in search results possible.

Most store platforms add some of it on their own. It's still worth checking, because themes and review add-ons sometimes output incomplete or conflicting markup.

Selling from a Hamilton showroom or pickup counter too? Then your Google Business Profile matters as much as the store. Someone in Westdale searching "near me" sees the map before any product page.

What good product schema covers

  • Product name and image
  • Price, in Canadian dollars
  • Availability: in stock or sold out
  • Brand, SKU or model number
  • Ratings, only from real customer reviews

How we work

How an ecommerce SEO project works.

Foundations first, then the pages that earn traffic.

  1. Crawl the store and check Search Console

    We find duplicate URLs, missing titles, blocked pages, and what Google has actually indexed.

  2. Pick the categories that matter

    We match your main categories to real searches, using Ubersuggest for volume estimates and Search Console for what's really happening.

  3. Fix the foundations

    Canonical tags, indexing rules, redirects for removed products, schema and speed.

  4. Write the pages that earn traffic

    Category intros first, then your top products, then answers to the questions buyers ask.

We work with the platform you already have. Shopify has fixed URL patterns and app bloat to watch (Shopify SEO). WooCommerce runs on WordPress, so plugins and updates matter (WordPress SEO). Wix and Squarespace stores have their own settings, but the same rules apply.

FAQ

Ecommerce SEO questions.

Is ecommerce SEO different from local SEO?

Yes. Local SEO is about the map pack and your service area. Ecommerce SEO is about category and product pages competing across Canada. If you also have a Hamilton storefront, you need both.

Should I write my own product descriptions?

For your main products, yes. Copied manufacturer text gives Google no reason to rank you over every other store selling the same thing. Start with best sellers and work down.

What happens to my SEO when a product sells out?

If it's coming back, keep the page and mark it out of stock. If it's gone for good, redirect it to the closest product or category. Don't just delete it.

Do I need product schema?

It helps Google understand price, stock and reviews, and it's what makes richer product results possible. Most platforms add some automatically, so the job is checking it's complete and correct.
