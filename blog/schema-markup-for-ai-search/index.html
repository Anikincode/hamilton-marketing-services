Schema markup for AI search: what it does, and what it doesn't

Schema markup won't get you into ChatGPT by itself. But it's cheap, it removes doubt about who you are, and it's easy to get wrong. Here's how to get it right.

Kirill Anikin · October 30, 2026 · 6 min read

Key takeaways

  • Schema markup, also called structured data, is code that states facts about your page in a format machines read without guessing: name, phone, hours, service area, services.
  • It helps search engines understand you, and some of Google's rich results depend on it. For AI tools it removes doubt. It doesn't create trust on its own.
  • The rule that matters most: the markup must match what's visible on the page.
  • On the sites we build, we add it from day one and generate it from the page itself, so the two can't drift apart.

Schema markup is having a moment. Some people now sell it as the secret to getting into ChatGPT.

It isn't a secret, and it isn't magic. But it is useful, it costs very little, and it's surprisingly easy to get wrong.

Here's what schema markup for AI search really does, in plain English.

What is schema markup?

Your web page is written for people. A person reading your homepage can tell which number is your phone line and which line of text is your opening hours.

A machine has to guess. Schema markup removes the guessing. It's a small block of code, usually in a format called JSON-LD, that says: this is the business name, this is the phone number, these are the hours, these are the areas served.

Think of it like the address label on a parcel. The letter inside is for the reader. The label is printed in a standard format so the sorting machine gets it right first time.

The vocabulary comes from schema.org, a shared standard that Google, Microsoft and others use. Google recommends JSON-LD for adding it.

How does schema help with AI search?

Let's separate what's known from what isn't.

What's known. Google says structured data helps it understand the content of a page, and it powers certain rich results in search. Google also says there are no special requirements, structured data included, to appear in its AI Overviews. Normal SEO best practice applies.

What isn't. We haven't seen detailed public documentation from the companies behind ChatGPT or Perplexity on how their systems treat structured data. Their tools read your pages. Whether they lean on the markup, the visible text or both, we can't say for sure, and neither can anyone selling schema as a shortcut.

So here's the honest case for it. Schema states your key facts once, clearly, in a standard format. When those facts match your page, your Business Profile and your listings, every machine that reads you gets the same story. That consistency is what AI tools appear to reward, and it costs almost nothing to add.

Which schema types does a local business need?

Most local service businesses need only a handful of types.

TypeWhere it goesWhat it says
LocalBusiness, or a more specific typeHomepage, or a contact or about pageName, phone, website, hours, address or service area, links to your profiles
ServiceEach service pageThe service, who provides it and where
FAQPagePages with a visible FAQThe questions and answers shown on the page
BreadcrumbListEvery pageWhere the page sits in your site
Article or BlogPostingBlog postsHeadline, author, date

Use the most specific business type that fits. Schema.org has types such as Plumber, HVACBusiness, Electrician, RoofingContractor, MovingCompany, AutoRepair, Restaurant and AccountingService. A furnace company in Stoney Creek is better described as an HVACBusiness than as a generic LocalBusiness.

A note on FAQ markup: Google now shows FAQ rich results only for a small set of well-known government and health websites. The markup still describes your page accurately. Just don't expect the old drop-down questions under your search listing.

For copy-ready examples you can adapt, see our local business schema starter pack. Google's own LocalBusiness structured data guide lists the properties it supports.

What about service-area businesses?

Many trades work from home or a yard and go to the customer. Google lets these service-area businesses hide their address on their Business Profile.

If you've hidden your address on Google, don't publish it in your schema either. Use the areas-served property instead and list the places you actually work, for example Hamilton, Stoney Creek, Ancaster and Dundas.

The point is consistency. Your website, your schema and your Business Profile should tell the same story about where you are and where you go. Our article on service-area businesses on Google Maps covers the profile side.

The schema mistakes we see most

Markup that doesn't match the page

Google's guidelines say structured data should describe what's visible on the page. If your schema says you're open 24 hours and your page says 8 to 6, something is wrong, and a machine has to pick one.

Review counts are a common example. We once found three different review counts shown on the same website. Add a fourth in the schema and you've made the confusion worse, not better.

Self-serving review stars

Plenty of local sites add review markup to their own business, hoping for gold stars in search. Google doesn't show star ratings for reviews a business publishes about itself in its LocalBusiness or Organization markup. Leave it out.

Schema copied from another business

The same copy-and-paste that left a fake phone number on 19 pages of one site we audited can leave another company's name, phone or city sitting in your structured data. Nobody sees it, so nobody fixes it. Machines read it anyway.

Plugin output nobody checked

Many website plugins and builders add schema automatically. Some fill it with blank fields, the wrong business type or a generic "Organization" with no location. Automatic doesn't mean correct.

How to check your schema

You don't need to read code to check it.

  • Google's Rich Results Test shows which structured data Google finds on a page and flags errors.
  • The Schema Markup Validator at validator.schema.org checks any schema.org markup, not just the types Google uses.
  • Search Console lists structured data it has found across your site, with errors and warnings.

Then do the human check. Read what the tool found and ask: is every fact here true, and does it match the page, our Business Profile and our listings?

What we do on the sites we build

We add structured data from the first day: a specific business type with the real service area, service markup on each service page, FAQ markup from the FAQs on the page, breadcrumbs everywhere and article markup on posts.

We generate it from the page content itself rather than typing it separately. This site works the same way. The article and FAQ markup on this page comes from the text you're reading, so the two can't disagree. We pair it with an llms.txt file, which we explain in what is llms.txt.

FAQ

Does schema markup help me rank higher?

Not directly. Google has said structured data isn't a general ranking boost. It helps Google understand your page and can make it eligible for certain rich results. The benefit is clarity, not a shortcut.

Do ChatGPT and Perplexity read schema markup?

Their makers haven't published much detail on it. Their tools read your pages, and clear, consistent facts help either way. Treat schema as a way to remove doubt, not as a way in on its own.

Can I add schema on Wix or WordPress?

Yes. Wix adds some structured data automatically and lets you add your own in a page's SEO settings. On WordPress, SEO plugins usually handle it. Either way, check the output with Google's Rich Results Test.

Should I add review stars to my own website's schema?

No. Google doesn't show star ratings for reviews a business marks up about itself. Put your effort into getting genuine reviews on your Google Business Profile instead.
