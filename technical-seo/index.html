Technical SEO

Technical SEO in Hamilton: fix what stops Google reading your site

Great service pages can't win calls if Google can't find them, read them or load them on a phone. Technical SEO fixes the quiet problems underneath.

What it is

The plumbing behind the drywall.

You can write the best furnace repair page in Hamilton and still get no calls from it.

If Google is told to skip the page, can't load it quickly on a phone, or can't work out which city it's for, the words don't matter.

Technical SEO is the plumbing behind the drywall. Nobody sees it. When it fails, everything upstairs gets wet.

It isn't mysterious, and most fixes are small. The hard part is knowing where to look.

What technical SEO covers

  • Indexing: can Google find and keep your pages?
  • Crawl rules: robots.txt and noindex tags
  • Speed: Core Web Vitals on mobile
  • Redirects: when pages move or get deleted
  • Titles and headings: one H1, the right city
  • Structured data: schema for your business details

What we see

Real problems from real audits.

On Sep 20, 2026 we scored 35 live local-business websites. The average score was 34 out of 100. These are the technical problems behind scores like that, all found on working business sites.

A site that told Google to go away

Its robots.txt file blocked all indexing. Google was being told to ignore the entire website. See the most common robots.txt mistakes.

5 of 48 pages in Google

Google indexed about 5 of one site's 48 pages. The rest sat in Search Console as "Discovered – currently not indexed". Here's what that status means.

27.7 seconds to load

One site's main content took 27.7 seconds to appear on mobile. Another took 9.9 seconds. Picture waiting that long with water on the basement floor.

Deleted pages, no redirects

Pages removed with no redirect, including one that ranked for a search with real monthly volume. The ranking went with it.

Missing headings

9 of 10 service pages had no H1 heading, still unfixed after four audits. Google had to guess what each page was about.

Wrong city, leftover templates

A Toronto page with a different neighbourhood in its title. A paragraph naming another Ontario city on several service pages. A fake phone number from a cloned template on 19 pages.

None of these needed a new website. They needed someone to find them, and someone to fix them. Wrong-city titles are common enough that we wrote about title tags for local SEO.

Speed

Core Web Vitals, in plain English.

Google measures how a page feels to a real visitor with three Core Web Vitals:

  • Largest Contentful Paint (LCP): how long until the main content shows. The 27.7-second site failed here.
  • Interaction to Next Paint (INP): how quickly the page reacts when you tap a button or open a menu.
  • Cumulative Layout Shift (CLS): whether things jump around while loading, so you tap the wrong thing.

We test key pages in PageSpeed Insights on mobile, because that's how someone with a broken furnace in Dundas is searching.

What usually slows a local site

  • Huge photos uploaded straight from a phone
  • Too many apps, widgets and tracking scripts
  • Sliders and videos at the top of the page
  • Fonts loaded from several places

Protect what you have

Keeping the rankings you already earned.

Defensive SEO is one of our three pillars. Some of the costliest technical problems aren't old. They're new, caused by a redesign, a deleted page or a careless edit.

Redirects

When a page moves or goes, its old address should point to the closest new page with a permanent redirect. Otherwise the ranking it earned is simply lost.

Redesigns

Before a new site launches, every old URL gets mapped to a new one. Our web design service builds that in from the start.

Structured data

Schema markup states your business name, services, areas and hours in a format search engines read reliably. It guarantees nothing, but it removes guesswork.

Spam links

One small business site had a network of 60 spam domains pointing links at it. We built a disavow file to ask Google to ignore them.

How it works

How a technical SEO project runs.

Technical work is easy to over-sell. We keep it tied to the pages that bring in calls.

  1. Find the problems

    We crawl the site, check Search Console and test key pages in PageSpeed Insights. If you haven't had one, start with an SEO audit.

  2. Rank them by cost

    A blocked site comes before a slow footer. Problems on the pages that bring in calls come before the blog.

  3. Fix them, or hand over a clear list

    We build on Wix and as fast static sites, and work on WordPress. If your web person prefers to make the changes, we give them a plain list.

  4. Confirm in Search Console

    We request indexing for the fixed pages and check that they move into Google's index.

  5. Watch for drift

    Sites break quietly. We recheck after changes, so a new plugin or a quick edit doesn't undo the work.

FAQ

Technical SEO questions.

What's the difference between technical SEO and an SEO audit?

An audit finds the problems and ranks them. Technical SEO is the work of fixing them. Most projects start with an audit, so nobody spends time fixing things that don't matter.

How do I know if Google is indexing my site?

Open Google Search Console and look at the Pages report. It shows which pages are indexed and why others aren't. If you don't have Search Console set up, that's the first fix.

Will a faster website rank higher?

Speed is one signal among many, so it won't carry a weak page on its own. But a slow page can lose the caller before rankings even matter. Fix it for people first.

Can you fix technical SEO on Wix or WordPress?

Yes. We build on Wix and as fast static sites, and work on WordPress. Each platform has its limits, and we'll tell you plainly when something can't be changed.

Is technical SEO a one-time job?

The big fixes often are. But sites keep changing: redesigns, new pages, plugin updates. A light monthly check catches problems before they cost you calls.
