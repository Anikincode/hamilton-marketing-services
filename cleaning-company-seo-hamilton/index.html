How customers search

"Cleaning" covers at least four different businesses

You're asking a stranger to hand over their house keys, or the alarm code to their office. That's why cleaning customers search carefully, and why the type of cleaning they type matters so much.

Residential

Regular, deep and one-time cleans. Recurring clients are worth the most over time.

"house cleaning Ancaster" · "biweekly cleaner Hamilton Mountain" · "deep cleaning Dundas"

Deadline cleans

Move-out, end-of-lease and post-renovation. Time-pressured, and often booked within days.

"move out cleaning Hamilton" · "end of lease cleaning Westdale" · "post construction cleaning Binbrook"

Commercial

Offices, clinics, shops. Decided by a manager comparing quotes, often for a contract.

"office cleaning downtown Hamilton" · "commercial cleaning Stoney Creek"

Specialty

Carpets, upholstery, windows, pressure washing. Searched by the surface, not the company.

"carpet cleaning Stoney Creek" · "window cleaning Waterdown"

Each of those buyers wants to see their exact job on your site. A homeowner booking a move-out clean shouldn't have to read about office contracts to find your price.

Google Business Profile

Categories for cleaners, and the one to lead with

Google can only show you for the searches your categories match. Lead with the service that earns you the most, then add the rest truthfully.

  • House cleaning service. The usual primary for residential cleaners.
  • Janitorial service. The category for office and commercial cleaning.
  • Carpet cleaning service and Upholstery cleaning service. For companies with the machines and the training.
  • Window cleaning service, Gutter Cleaning Service or Pressure washing service. For exterior specialists.
  • Air duct cleaning service. Only if you really own the equipment for it.

Category names on Google shift over time. Use the closest match you're offered.

Service area, not a home address

Cleaners go to the client, so most should register as a service-area business: home address hidden, coverage areas listed. Never use a borrowed or virtual address to look closer to Burlington or Grimsby.

Then keep your name, phone and website identical everywhere you're listed. Mismatched details across directories make Google less sure which listing is really you. Read more on NAP consistency and citations.

Trust and reviews

Reviews are your references

Before letting a cleaner into the house, people read reviews the way an employer reads references. They look for honesty, punctuality and care, and they look for names.

Recurring clients are happy, but they rarely think to leave a review on their own. Ask after the first clean, when the difference is most obvious, with a direct link by text or email.

Encourage them to mention the type of clean and the area: "move-out clean in Westdale, done the day before we handed back the keys". That's the review the next renter is looking for.

Google forbids paying for reviews and asking only the clients you know loved you. Ask everyone the same way. Our guide covers how to get more Google reviews within the rules.

Trust signals on the site

  • Real photos of your team, not stock smiles.
  • Insurance and background checks, stated only if true.
  • What's included in each clean, as a checklist.
  • Prices or price ranges where you can give them.

Seasons and ads

When cleaning demand moves

Cleaning runs all year, but the mix shifts. Building pages ahead of each shift is how you catch it.

Moving season

Move-out and move-in cleans follow the moving calendar. If you work with student rentals near McMaster or Mohawk, plan for the end of the school year.

Spring and fall

For many cleaners, deep cleans and window work pick up when the weather turns. Have those pages ready before then.

Local Services Ads

Google has offered Local Services Ads to cleaning businesses in Canada, but which cleaning services qualify can vary. Confirm yours is on the list before you budget for it. Our Google Ads service sets up tracking before any spend.

Common website problems

Where cleaning websites go wrong

These are the first things we check on a cleaning company's site. Most are quick to fix once someone spots them.

  • One page for every kind of clean. Residential, commercial, carpet and windows all on a single "Services" page, so none of them ranks.
  • A quote form that breaks on a phone. If the booking form is hard to fill on mobile, the search work is wasted at the last step.
  • A site that tells Google to go away. We once found a live business site whose robots.txt file blocked all indexing. Google was told to ignore the whole thing. See common robots.txt mistakes.
  • No areas named. A cleaner who covers Ancaster, Dundas and the Mountain should say so on the page, in plain words.

We'd start with the Business Profile and the page structure, then build out one page per type of clean. That's the heart of our local SEO work.

FAQ

Cleaning company SEO questions

Should residential and commercial cleaning have separate pages?

Yes. A homeowner and an office manager search differently, care about different things and pay differently. Give each a page that speaks to them, and link between the two.

Which primary category should a carpet cleaning company use?

"Carpet cleaning service", if that's most of your work. Add upholstery or tile cleaning as secondaries only if you offer them regularly.

Should I put prices on my cleaning website?

If you can, yes. Even a starting price or a range answers the first question most people have. It also filters out callers who were never going to book.

How do I get reviews from clients who've been with me for years?

Just ask, once, with a direct link. Long-time clients are often happy to help; they simply never thought of it. A short personal message works better than an automated email.

I clean in Burlington too. Can I rank there?

You can, but distance matters on the map. Add Burlington to your service areas, mention the jobs you do there on your site, and track your visibility from Burlington itself rather than assuming.
