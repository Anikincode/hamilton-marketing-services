SEO vs Google Ads: which should a local business pay for first?

Every local business owner asks this sooner or later. The honest answer is "it depends", so here's exactly what it depends on.

Kirill Anikin · October 30, 2026 · 6 min read

Key takeaways

  • Google Ads are rent: calls can start quickly and stop the day you stop paying. SEO is ownership: slower to build, but a page that ranks keeps working.
  • Before you spend on ads, set up conversion tracking. We've seen ad accounts running with none, so nobody could tell which clicks became calls.
  • Ads make sense for a new business, urgent work, seasonal pushes and testing which searches turn into jobs.
  • For most local businesses the answer is both, in proportions that shift over time.

You've got a budget for marketing and two obvious places to put it. Google Ads, where you pay and show up at the top. Or SEO, where you invest and wait.

People who sell one will tell you the other is a waste. Neither is. They do different jobs, and the right mix depends on your business, your timing and your patience.

Here's a balanced look at SEO vs Google Ads for a local business.

How Google Ads works for a local business

With Google Ads, you choose the searches you want to appear for, write an ad, and pay each time someone clicks. Your ad can appear at the top of the results within days of setting it up.

That speed is the big advantage. A plumber who wants calls for "emergency plumber Hamilton Mountain" tonight can have an ad running quickly.

The catch is that every click costs money, and the calls stop the moment the budget does. To give a sense of scale, Ubersuggest puts the cost per click for the search "google ads hamilton" at about $26. Every trade has its own price. None of them are free.

In Canada there's also Local Services Ads, which appear above regular ads for some searches. They're available for the trades only, and you pay per lead rather than per click. If you're a plumber, an HVAC company or another eligible trade, they're worth a look.

How SEO works for a local business

SEO is the work of showing up without paying per click: in the map pack, in the regular results and, increasingly, in AI answers.

It means a strong Google Business Profile, a website with one clear page per service, steady reviews and a technically sound site. It takes longer. New pages and new rankings usually take months, not days.

But once a page ranks, it keeps bringing calls without a charge for each click. That's why we call it ownership. It isn't free, because the work costs time or money. It just doesn't stop when you stop paying.

Step zero: set up conversion tracking

Before you choose either, make sure you can measure what you're paying for.

Conversion tracking tells you which clicks became phone calls, form submissions or bookings. Without it, you're judging Google Ads on clicks, which don't pay the bills.

This isn't hypothetical. We've reviewed Google Ads accounts running with no conversion tracking at all. Money was going out every day, and nobody could tell which searches brought in work and which brought in nothing.

The same principle applies to SEO. Track calls and website clicks from your Business Profile, and form submissions from your site, so you can see whether organic visibility is turning into customers.

When Google Ads should come first

  • You're brand new. A new business has no reviews, no history and a new domain. SEO will take time to build. Ads can bring calls while it does. Our guide to SEO for a new business covers the other half.
  • The work is urgent and valuable. Burst pipes, no heat in January, a flooded basement in Binbrook. People searching for these call fast, and a single job can cover a lot of clicks.
  • You have a seasonal push. AC installation in a heat wave, furnace tune-ups in the fall. Ads let you turn up the volume for a few weeks.
  • You want to test. Ads show you quickly which searches turn into jobs. That tells you which pages are worth building for SEO.

When SEO should come first

  • Your budget can't sustain ad spend. If ads would eat your margin before they paid back, SEO is the better long-term bet.
  • You're already close. If you rank just outside the top results for searches that matter, a few fixes may get you further than a new ad budget.
  • Your customers research first. Interior design, renovations and home inspections involve reading and comparing. Helpful pages and good reviews do a lot of the selling.
  • Some searchers skip ads. Some people scroll straight past anything marked "Sponsored" to the map pack or the regular results. SEO is how you reach them.

Using both together

For most local businesses, the best answer is both, with the balance shifting over time.

Start with ads for speed and data. Use what the ads teach you, which searches bring calls in Stoney Creek versus Ancaster, to decide which SEO pages to build first. As organic rankings and map visibility grow, test whether you still need to pay for the searches you already win.

That last step matters. Where your map pack and organic results are strong, ads may add new calls, or they may just take credit for calls you'd have had anyway. Test it, rather than assuming.

If you're weighing up costs, our guide to Google Ads management fees explains ad spend versus management, and is SEO worth it? covers the payback maths.

A simple way to decide

  1. Set up conversion tracking, for ads and organic.
  2. Fix your Google Business Profile. It's free and helps both.
  3. If you need calls this month, run tightly targeted ads on your most urgent, most valuable services.
  4. At the same time, build SEO pages for the services and areas that matter most.
  5. Review every month: cost per lead from ads versus leads from organic. Shift money towards whatever is working.

FAQ

Is SEO cheaper than Google Ads?

It can cost less per lead over time, because you're not paying for each click. But it takes longer to start, and it isn't free. The real answer depends on your trade, your area and how well each is done.

Do Google Ads help my SEO rankings?

No. Google keeps ads and organic results separate, and paying for ads doesn't boost your organic rankings. Ads can teach you which searches convert, which helps you plan SEO.

What are Local Services Ads?

They're Google ads that appear above regular ads for some local searches. In Canada they cover the trades only, and you pay per lead rather than per click.

When can I cut back on ads?

When your organic and map rankings bring steady calls for the searches you're paying for. Test it by reducing ad spend on those searches and watching total calls, not just clicks.
