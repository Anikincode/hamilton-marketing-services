How customers search

A burst pipe and a new water heater are two different searches

Picture two Hamilton homeowners on the same Tuesday. One has water coming through the basement ceiling. The other wants a tankless water heater before winter. Both need a plumber, but they don't search the same way.

Emergency searches

Short, urgent, typed on a phone with wet hands. This person calls one of the first businesses on the map and rarely scrolls past it.

  • "emergency plumber Hamilton Mountain"
  • "burst pipe repair Stoney Creek"
  • "plumber open now near me"
  • "sewer backup Hamilton"

Planned searches

These people compare pages, reviews and two or three quotes before they book.

  • "water heater replacement Ancaster"
  • "sump pump installation Waterdown"
  • "tankless water heater Hamilton"
  • "bathroom rough-in plumber Dundas"

Here's the catch. The emergency call is usually won on Google Maps. The planned job is often won on your website. Work on only one and you hand the other half of the calls to someone else.

Google Business Profile

The categories that fit a plumbing company

Your primary category decides which searches you can show up for on the map. For almost every plumbing company, that's simply "Plumber". The secondary categories are where plumbers either gain ground or make a mess.

Add a secondary category only when it describes a real, regular part of your work. Each one tells Google you're relevant for a new set of searches, so a wrong one pulls you toward calls you can't take.

  • Plumber. The primary category, nearly always.
  • Drainage service. If you clear drains, run camera inspections or repair main lines.
  • Water damage restoration service. Only if you do the drying and restoration, not just stop the leak.
  • Septic system service. Only if you really pump or repair septic systems.
  • Water softening equipment supplier. If you sell and install softeners.
  • Bathroom remodeler. Only if you take on full bathroom renovations, not just fixtures.

Google renames categories now and then, so pick the closest match it offers during setup. Our guide to Business Profile categories explains how to test them.

Hide the address if customers don't come to you

Most plumbers drive to the job. Google lets a service-area business hide its address and list the areas it covers instead: the Mountain, Stoney Creek, Binbrook and so on.

What you can't do is invent a location. A virtual office in Ancaster to look local there, or a keyword-stuffed name like "Best Emergency Plumber Hamilton", breaks Google's guidelines and puts the whole listing at risk.

How service-area businesses show up on Google Maps

Service pages

One page for every job you want more of

Google ranks pages, not companies. If water heaters, drains and sump pumps all share one "Services" page, that page is trying to win a dozen searches at once. It usually wins none.

Emergency plumbing

For the "open now" searches. Your real hours stated plainly, the areas you reach, and a phone number one tap away.

Drain cleaning and camera inspection

Clogs, slow drains and main line backups. Explain what the camera shows and when a drain needs more than snaking.

Water heaters

Tank and tankless, gas and electric. Planned buyers want sizing, rent-or-buy and timing answered before they call.

Sump pumps and backwater valves

Basement flood protection. This page has to be live and indexed before the wet season, not during it.

Sewer and water line repair

Bigger jobs and bigger cheques. These buyers read the whole page and look hard at your reviews.

Each page names the job and area in its first line, answers price and timing questions, shows proof, then gives one clear way to call. That's the core of our SEO content and service pages work.

Reviews, ads and the calendar

What decides who gets the call

Showing up is half the job. The other half is being the plumber someone picks out of three.

Reviews sell the emergency

Someone standing in water glances at the stars and the last few reviews, then taps Call. Ask every customer the same day, with a direct link. Google bans paying for reviews and asking only the customers you know are happy. Here's how to get more Google reviews the right way.

Local Services Ads

Plumbing is one of the trades Google offers Local Services Ads for in Canada. They sit above the regular ads, you pay per lead instead of per click, and Google verifies the business before they run. They work best beside SEO, not instead of it. See our Google Ads management.

The plumbing calendar

Less seasonal than HVAC, but it has spikes. Frozen and burst pipes arrive with the first hard freeze. Flooded basement calls follow the spring thaw and heavy rain. Change your posts and homepage a few weeks before each one.

Common website problems

What we usually find on plumbing websites

Most plumbing sites don't fail on one big thing. They fail on small things that add up.

  • A phone number you can't tap. Shown as an image, or tucked into the footer. On a phone, that's a lost emergency call.
  • "24/7" in the header and nowhere else. When Google, your site and your voicemail disagree on hours, nobody trusts any of them.
  • Area pages with only the name swapped. Ten copies of one page with "Ancaster" replaced by "Dundas" look like doorway pages, and they rarely rank.
  • Heavy pages on mobile. On one local site we audited, the main content took 9.9 seconds to appear on a phone. Nobody with a flooded basement waits that long.

How we'd start with a Hamilton plumber

  1. Audit the Business Profile

FAQ

Questions plumbers ask us

Do I need a separate page for every part of Hamilton?

Not at first. Start with one strong page per service. Add an area page only when you can say something true and specific about working there, like the jobs you've done in Stoney Creek.

Can I show up on the Mountain if my shop is in the lower city?

Yes, but distance matters. Google weighs how close a business is to the person searching, along with relevance and prominence. That's why someone on Upper James can see a different map pack than someone downtown. Strong service pages and reviews help you reach further.

Should my Business Profile say I'm open 24 hours?

Only if someone really answers at 2 a.m. A missed emergency call turns into a one-star review quickly, and that costs more than the hours label ever brings in.

Do Local Services Ads replace SEO for plumbers?

No. They're a paid lead source that stops when the budget stops. SEO builds the profile, reviews and pages that keep working. Many plumbers run both, with ads covering the gap while organic rankings grow.
