Shopify SEO

Shopify SEO in Hamilton for small online stores

Shopify takes care of hosting, security and a sitemap. Ranking is still up to you. Here's what matters for a small store, in plain English.

Straight talk

What Shopify does for your SEO, and what it doesn't.

Let's be upfront first.

Most of our work is on websites for local service businesses, built on Wix, as static sites, or on WordPress. We don't build Shopify stores or custom themes, and we won't pretend Shopify is where most of our hours go.

What we bring to a Shopify store is the SEO groundwork that works on any platform: the right pages for the right searches, clean URLs, clear titles, structured data and speed. Shopify handles a lot of the plumbing for you. The rest is on you.

What Shopify handles

  • Hosting and SSL, with nothing to patch
  • A sitemap, generated and updated automatically
  • Canonical tags that point product copies to the main product URL
  • Title, description and URL fields on every product and collection

What it leaves to you

  • Collection pages with something worth reading
  • Product descriptions in your own words
  • Titles written for real searches
  • Keeping apps, images and speed under control
  • Redirects when products or pages change

Collections

Collection pages are where Shopify stores win search.

In Shopify, collections are your category pages. And category pages match how people actually shop.

Nobody searches your exact product name until they already know it. They search the type: "handmade soy candles", "custom printed hoodies", "gift baskets Hamilton".

Most collection pages are a grid of products with no words. Google has very little to go on, so it ranks someone else's page that explains more.

Our ecommerce SEO page goes deeper on category and product pages for small stores.

What a collection page needs

  • A title and H1 that match the search, not just "Candles"
  • A short intro: what's in the collection, who it's for, what makes yours different
  • Links to related collections
  • Answers to the questions buyers ask before they order

URLs

Shopify's URL quirks, and which ones to leave alone.

Shopify fixes part of every address. Products live under /products/, collections under /collections/, pages under /pages/. You can't remove those, and they don't stop a store from ranking. Don't fight them.

Here's what does deserve your attention.

Products at more than one address

A product viewed inside a collection can get a longer, collection-based URL. Shopify's canonical tag points to the main product URL, but your internal links should point there too.

Variants

Sizes and colours share one product page. That's usually right. Split a colour into its own product only if people search for that colour on its own.

Tag-filtered collections

Filtering a collection by tag can multiply into lots of near-identical pages. Keep those out of the index unless one targets a real search.

Deleted products

Remove a product without a redirect and its address becomes an error page. Shopify has built-in URL redirects. Point the old address at the closest product or collection.

Apps and speed

Apps make Shopify easy, and slow.

Every app can add scripts to your storefront. A review widget, a pop-up, a currency switcher, a chat bubble. Each one is small. Together they add up, and shoppers on a phone feel every one.

Uninstalling an app doesn't always remove the code it added to your theme. Check after you remove one.

Then there's structured data. Many Shopify themes add product markup on their own, and a reviews app may add its own on top. Two sets can conflict. Check with Google's Rich Results Test, and see our schema starter pack for what good markup looks like.

More on why speed matters for small businesses: page speed for local businesses.

Quick Shopify speed checks

  • Test your main collection and product pages in PageSpeed Insights, on mobile
  • Remove apps you don't use, then check the theme for leftovers
  • Resize product photos before upload
  • Go easy on slideshows and autoplay video at the top of the page

Local + online

Selling online from Hamilton? Don't forget the map.

A shop on Locke Street that also sells online has two kinds of customers.

One group is across Canada, searching for your products. They find your collection pages. The other group is nearby, searching "near me". They find your Google Business Profile on the map. A store that does both well catches both. Our local SEO page covers the map side.

FAQ

Shopify SEO questions.

Is Shopify good for SEO?

It's a solid base. Hosting, security, a sitemap and canonical tags come built in. What it can't do is write your collection pages or decide which searches to target.

Can I remove /products/ and /collections/ from my Shopify URLs?

No. Shopify sets those parts of the address. They don't stop a store from ranking, so it's not worth fighting them.

Do I need a Shopify SEO app?

Not necessarily. Titles, descriptions, URLs and redirects are already built into Shopify. An app is worth it only if it solves a specific problem, and every app adds weight.

Do you build Shopify stores?

No. We build websites on Wix and as fast static sites, and work on WordPress. On Shopify, we focus on the SEO of the store you already have.

Will moving my store to Shopify hurt my rankings?

It can, if old URLs aren't redirected. Shopify's addresses will differ from your old platform's, so every old product and category URL needs a redirect to its new home before launch.
