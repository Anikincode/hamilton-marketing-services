Does ChatGPT recommend my business? How to check properly

One question in one chat tells you almost nothing. Here's a simple, repeatable way to find out whether AI tools name your business, and why they do or don't.

Kirill Anikin · October 30, 2026 · 6 min read

Key takeaways

  • One question in one AI tool is a coin flip, not a test. Ask many versions of the question, in several tools, and write down what comes back.
  • Test from a clean session, so your own chat history doesn't steer the answer.
  • Look at the sources each tool cites. They show you where the AI learned about the businesses it names.
  • The deepest test is the hidden searches ChatGPT runs. We use a tool that reveals them, then check whether you rank for each one.

You ask ChatGPT for the best roofer in Ancaster. Your name comes up, and you relax. Or it doesn't, and you panic.

Both reactions are premature. One question, asked once, tells you almost nothing about whether ChatGPT recommends your business.

Here's how to check properly. It takes an afternoon, costs nothing, and gives you a baseline you can measure against later.

Why one question isn't a test

AI answers aren't fixed. The same question can get a different answer depending on how it's worded, where the person is, what they've asked before, and sometimes just from one try to the next.

Think of it like asking one neighbour on Concession Street for a plumber. Useful, but you wouldn't call it market research. Ask the whole street and a pattern starts to show.

That's the mindset here. You're looking for patterns across many questions, not a yes or no from one.

Step 1: Write down the questions your customers really ask

Don't test with the phrase you'd like to rank for. Test with the questions real customers type, in their words.

Aim for a mix of types:

  • Urgent: "Burst pipe in Stoney Creek, who can come tonight?"
  • Comparison: "Who's the best furnace repair company on Hamilton Mountain?"
  • Price: "How much does drain cleaning cost in Hamilton?"
  • Problem: "Why is my basement floor drain backing up?"
  • Specific service: "Who installs heat pumps in Waterdown?"
  • Your name: "What do you know about [your business name] in Hamilton?"

Your phone calls are the best source for these. What do people ask in the first minute of a call? Those are your test questions.

Step 2: Ask them from a clean session

If you've spent months asking ChatGPT about your own business, it may remember. Your answers won't match what a stranger sees.

So start fresh. In ChatGPT, use a temporary chat or a logged-out window. Do the same in the other tools your customers might use: Google's Gemini, Perplexity, Microsoft Copilot and Google's AI Mode.

Check that the tool actually searched the web. ChatGPT and Perplexity show their sources when they do. An answer with no sources may be coming from the model's memory, which can be out of date.

Step 3: Record what comes back

A simple spreadsheet is enough. One row per question, per tool:

ColumnWhat to write
QuestionThe exact wording you used
Tool and dateChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Copilot or AI Mode, plus the day you tested
Named?Yes or no, and where in the answer (first, middle, last)
Who else was namedEvery competitor the answer mentions
Sources citedThe websites the tool linked to
Details correct?Phone, area, services, hours: right, wrong or missing

Run each question at least twice. If your name appears in one run and not the other, write that down too. "Sometimes" is a real result.

Step 4: Follow the sources

The sources are the most useful part of the whole exercise.

When Perplexity names three Hamilton HVAC companies, it shows the pages it read. When ChatGPT searches, it links to its sources too. Google's AI features link to the pages they drew on.

Look for patterns. Is the same directory cited again and again? Is a "best of Hamilton" list doing the heavy lifting? Is a competitor's service page being quoted word for word?

Each repeated source is a place you need to be present and accurate. If a directory keeps showing up and you're not listed on it, that's a fix you can make this week.

Step 5: Check what it says about you

Being named with the wrong details can be worse than not being named at all.

Ask each tool directly about your business by name. Does it get your service area right? Your phone number? The services you actually offer?

When the details are wrong, trace them to the source. In our audits we've found template text naming a different Ontario city on a business's service pages, and two separate businesses sharing one phone number across their listings. Either one can end up repeated in an AI answer. Our guide to NAP consistency and citations shows how to clean up the listings side.

Step 6: Find the hidden searches

This is the step you can't easily do by hand, and it's where most of the answers are.

When ChatGPT answers a question like "who's good for furnace repair in Stoney Creek?", it often runs one or more web searches first. Those searches aren't always the customer's words. They're ChatGPT's own rewrites, and the customer never sees them.

We use a tool we built that reveals those hidden searches. Once we know what ChatGPT actually searched, we check whether your pages appear for each search, both in regular results and on the map across your service area. If you're missing from the searches, you'll be missing from the answer.

What your results mean

  • Named often, with the right details. Good. Now protect it. Our article on defensive SEO covers how rankings and listings quietly get lost.
  • Named for some questions, not others. Look at which question types you're missing. Usually it's a service or an area with no page of its own.
  • Named, but with wrong details. Fix the sources first. Then retest.
  • Never named. Start with our list of reasons your business isn't in ChatGPT. The cause is usually technical, thin content or mismatched details.

How often to retest

Keep your question list and rerun it every month or two, and after any big change to your website or listings. Use the same questions and the same clean-session setup each time, so the results compare.

Look at the trend, not one month. A single bad run is noise. Three months of slipping is a signal.

FAQ

Why does ChatGPT give different answers each time I ask?

AI tools generate each answer fresh. Wording, location, chat history and the searches the tool happens to run all change the result. That's why you test many questions, more than once each, and look for a pattern.

Which AI tool matters most for a local business?

It depends on your customers, so test them all. ChatGPT is widely used, Gemini and AI Overviews are Google's own, Perplexity shows its sources clearly and Copilot runs on Bing. The fixes that help one usually help the others.

Should I ask ChatGPT about my business by name?

Yes, as a separate check. It shows what the tool believes about you: your services, area and contact details. If it gets something wrong, find where that wrong detail lives online and correct it there.

What if a competitor is always named and I never am?

Look at the sources cited for their mentions. You'll usually find clear service pages, more reviews, or listings on sites where you're absent. That gives you a concrete list of gaps to close.
