Pricing guide

How SEO pricing works: retainer, project or hourly?

Two SEO quotes can look completely different and both be fair. Usually they're pricing different work in different ways. Here's how each model works, so you can compare them properly.

Kirill Anikin · October 30, 2026 · 5 min read

Key takeaways

  • SEO is priced three ways: a monthly retainer for ongoing work, a project fee for a fixed job, or an hourly rate for advice.
  • Retainers suit steady growth, projects suit one-off fixes and hourly suits businesses with their own marketing people.
  • Whatever the model, a quote should say exactly what's included: pages, Business Profile work, technical fixes, tracking and reporting.
  • The number of services, the size of your area, the state of your site and who writes the content move the price within any model.

Two SEO quotes land on your desk. One is a monthly fee. The other is a one-time price that looks bigger. Which is cheaper?

You can't tell until you know what each one covers. This guide explains how each pricing model works, what you should get at each level of service and how to read a quote line by line.

The three ways SEO is priced

Monthly retainer

You pay a set fee every month for ongoing work. It suits local SEO well, because rankings need steady attention: new pages, reviews, Business Profile updates, tracking and fixes.

Good for: a business that wants to grow across several services or areas, like an HVAC company covering the Mountain, Stoney Creek and Ancaster.

Watch for: vague scope. "Ongoing optimization" isn't a deliverable. Ask what gets done each month.

Project fee

You pay a fixed price for a defined job with a start and an end: an audit, a technical cleanup, a set of new service pages, a Business Profile rebuild or a move to a new platform.

Good for: a business with one clear problem, like a Dundas restaurant whose site Google can't index, or a contractor who needs proper service pages built once.

Watch for: no follow-through. A project fixes things on the day it ends. After that, nobody is watching.

Hourly

You pay for time, usually for advice: strategy calls, second opinions, reviewing someone else's work or training your staff.

Good for: a business with its own web or marketing person who needs direction, not extra hands. That's the idea behind an SEO consultant.

Watch for: open-ended hours. Agree on a cap, or on a clear question to answer by the end.

What's included at each level

Retainers usually come in levels. The names change from company to company, but the work at each level should look roughly like this. If a quote at any level is missing the basics, ask why.

Foundations

  • Google Business Profile: primary category, services, service area, hours and photos
  • Technical fixes: indexing, robots.txt, headings, titles with the right city and speed problems
  • Google Search Console set up and checked monthly
  • A set of core searches tracked, like "furnace repair Hamilton"
  • A short monthly report in plain English

Fits: a small business in one area with modest competition, like a specialist who only serves Binbrook.

Growth

Everything in Foundations, plus:

  • New service pages over time, one page per service and intent
  • Answers to the questions customers really ask, built into those pages
  • A steady system for getting reviews
  • Directory listings checked and corrected
  • Regular Business Profile posts
  • Rankings tracked across your service area, not from one spot

Fits: a trade competing across the city, like a plumber who wants calls from both the lower city and the Mountain.

Competitive or multi-area

Everything in Growth, plus:

  • Area pages with real local substance, not city-swapped templates
  • Earning links and mentions from real local sources
  • A monthly click-through check: rewriting titles on pages ranking 4 to 10 with weak clicks, then rechecking after 30 days
  • Defensive SEO: watching for spam links, deleted pages and broken redirects
  • Support for more than one location or Business Profile

Fits: a company with several locations, or one serving a wide area from Grimsby to Waterdown.

What drives the cost within a model

The level is only half of it. These move the price up or down at any level:

  • Number of services. Each one needs its own page and upkeep.
  • Size of the service area. Covering Burlington and Grimsby as well as Hamilton means more pages and more tracking.
  • State of the site. A clean site starts faster than one with a blocked robots.txt or cloned template text. When we scored 35 live local-business websites on Sep 20, 2026, the average was 34 out of 100, so most sites start with some cleanup.
  • Who writes the content. If you supply drafts or detailed notes, writing costs less.
  • Number of locations. Each location has its own Business Profile to set up and look after.

A worked example: reading a retainer quote

Say a quote offers "local SEO" for $Y a month. Before you compare it with anything, find out:

  1. How many new pages, and who writes them?
  2. Which searches are tracked, and from how many points across your area?
  3. What Business Profile work is included?
  4. How long is the contract, and who owns the accounts?

Then do the payback maths. If one job is worth $X in profit to you, the retainer pays for itself once SEO brings in Y ÷ X extra jobs a month. If a single furnace install covers the monthly fee, one extra job a month breaks even.

Do the same for a project quote of $P: it pays for itself after P ÷ X extra jobs, spread over however long the work keeps paying.

Common questions about how SEO pricing works

Is a retainer better than a project?

Neither is better. A project fixes a known problem. A retainer keeps things moving and protects what's been built. A sensible path is to start with a project, like an audit and cleanup, then move to a retainer if the numbers make sense.

Why do some SEO companies charge by the hour?

Hourly pricing suits advice: strategy, second opinions and training. It's a good fit if you have someone in-house to do the actual work.

Can I pause a monthly SEO plan?

Ask before you sign, because companies differ. Know that pausing can cost momentum, since competitors keep working while you're paused.

Does a higher price mean better SEO?

No. A higher price should mean more work. Compare what's included, not the total at the bottom.
