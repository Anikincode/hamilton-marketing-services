Geo-grid rank tracking: see your Google Maps ranking from every part of town

Your map ranking depends on where the searcher is standing. A geo-grid checks it from many points across your area at once, so you see the whole picture instead of the view from your own driveway.

Kirill Anikin · October 30, 2026 · 5 min read

Key takeaways

  • Map rankings change with the searcher's location. One check from your office tells you almost nothing about the rest of the city.
  • A geo-grid runs the same search from many points across your service area and shows your rank at each one.
  • Read it for patterns: where you're strong, where you fade, and which competitor takes over.
  • Compare like with like: same searches, same grid, same time each month.
  • A grid tracks the map pack only. Use Search Console for your website's organic rankings.

"We're number one on Google!" says the owner, holding up a phone in the shop on Upper James.

They might be. From that exact spot. Drive to Dundas and search again, and they might not be on the map at all.

That's the problem geo-grid rank tracking solves. It shows where you rank on the map from all over your service area, not just from where you happen to be standing.

Why one ranking from one spot misleads you

Google says local results depend on relevance, distance and prominence. Distance is the one that moves. The map pack a searcher sees depends on where they are, or on the place they name in the search.

So "where do we rank for furnace repair?" has no single answer. It has a different answer on the Mountain, in the lower city, in Stoney Creek and in Ancaster.

Checking from your own phone makes it worse. Your phone knows where you are, and it may remember the businesses you search for and visit. It's like asking your mother whether you're popular.

What a geo-grid is

A geo-grid is a set of evenly spaced points laid over a map of your service area. A tracking tool runs the same search as if it were standing at each point, and records where your business appears in the map results.

The result is a map covered in numbered dots. Each dot is your rank from that spot. Most tools colour them: green where you're near the top, yellow in the middle, red where you're low or missing.

We use BrightLocal for our geo-grid tracking. Other tools do the same job. The method matters more than the brand.

What it looks like in Hamilton

Hamilton is a good city to see why this matters. It's spread out, and the escarpment splits the lower city from the Mountain, with mountain accesses like the Jolley Cut and the Claremont Access connecting the two.

Picture an HVAC company based near Upper James on the Mountain. A grid for "furnace repair" might show:

  • Strong green dots across the central Mountain, around Upper James and Upper Wentworth
  • Yellow as the grid reaches down into the lower city
  • Red out in Waterdown, Dundas and Grimsby

That isn't a disaster. It's an honest picture, and it gives the owner real choices. Do they actually want Waterdown jobs? Is there a gap in the lower city they could close? Or should they own the Mountain completely before worrying about anywhere else?

That's our first pillar in practice. We track the searches that lead to calls across the service area, using a geo-grid, not one ranking from one spot.

How to set up a geo-grid that's useful

  1. Pick a few searches that bring calls. "Furnace repair Hamilton", "AC installation Hamilton", "emergency plumber". Not every phrase you can think of. Tools usually charge per scan, so choose carefully.
  2. Centre the grid on your business location, the one Google has verified, even if your address is hidden.
  3. Size it to your real service area. If you never work in Grimsby, a grid that reaches Grimsby just paints red dots you don't care about.
  4. Pick sensible spacing. Too wide and you miss the detail. Too tight and you pay for points that all say the same thing.
  5. Run it on a schedule. Same searches, same grid, roughly the same day each month. That's the only way the comparison means anything.

If you're a service-area business, read how service-area businesses show up on Google Maps first. It explains why the grid centres on your verified location, not on the towns you list.

How to read the results

Don't stare at the average. Look for the shape.

  • Where's your stronghold? Usually close to your location. That's your base to protect.
  • Where do you fade? The edge of the green tells you how far your current strength reaches.
  • Who takes over? Many tools show which competitor ranks at each point. If the same business wins every red dot to the east, look at what they're doing right.
  • What changed? Put this month next to last month. Did the green grow, shrink or stay put? Did anything happen in between, like a category change or a new page?

What to do with weak areas

A red patch is a question, not an instruction. Work through it in this order:

  1. Do you actually want work there? If not, ignore it.
  2. Is your profile relevant? Check your primary category and services. A grid that's red everywhere, even next door, often points to relevance, not distance.
  3. Do you have a strong page for that service? Google says web position is part of prominence. A better service page can lift the whole grid.
  4. Do you have real work there? Reviews from customers in that area, and photos of jobs there, show you really serve it.
  5. Is the map the right tool for that area at all? Sometimes organic pages or ads are the more realistic way to reach a town far from your base.

And accept the physics. Distance is a real factor. A business on the Mountain may never own the map in Grimsby, and that's fine if Grimsby isn't where the money is.

What a geo-grid can't tell you

  • It tracks the map pack, not your website. For organic rankings, use Google Search Console.
  • It's a snapshot. Results shift during the day and from one device to another. One scan is a reading, not a verdict.
  • It doesn't show calls. Your Business Profile's performance report shows calls, website clicks and direction requests. Rankings only matter if those follow.
  • It can't fix anything. It tells you where to look. The work is still categories, reviews, pages and consistency.

FAQ

What is geo-grid rank tracking?

It's a way of checking your Google Maps ranking from many evenly spaced points across your service area at once. Each point shows where you rank from there, so you can see where you're strong and where you fade.

Why is my ranking different on my phone?

Because map results depend on where you are, and your phone may also remember the businesses you search for and visit. A geo-grid gives a more neutral view from many points.

How often should I run a geo-grid?

Monthly is usually enough for a local business, plus an extra scan after a big change like a new primary category. Keep the searches and grid the same so the results compare fairly.

Does a geo-grid show my website rankings?

No. It tracks the map pack. For your website's organic rankings, use Google Search Console, which shows the real searches, impressions and clicks for each page.
