SEO in downtown Hamilton, where your competitors are a block away

Downtown, a customer can find several places that sell what you sell without crossing the street. Here's how local search works when the competition is that close, and what we'd fix first.

Where it is

The busiest, tightest-packed part of Hamilton

Downtown and the rest of the lower city sit below the escarpment that everyone here calls the Mountain.

King Street and Gore Park are the middle of it. Around them, James Street North, Locke Street, Ottawa Street and Westdale, right beside McMaster, each pull their own crowd.

People get here every way there is. Hamilton GO Centre and West Harbour GO bring commuters straight in. The Jolley Cut and the Claremont Access bring drivers down from the Mountain.

That density is the whole story for search. Out in the suburbs, a business can be the only one of its kind for a long way. Downtown, the café, print shop or accounting office next to yours is close enough to wave at.

Who's searching

Four kinds of downtown customer

They all use Google. They just use it in slightly different moods.

Commuters

Someone stepping off the train at West Harbour GO searches with a thumb, not a city name. "Coffee near me" or "print shop near me" is the entire query.

Students and staff near McMaster

A student in Westdale who types "late night food" isn't planning a trip across the city. Google knows that, so it keeps the results close to where they're standing.

Event crowds

The art crawl and Supercrawl bring people to James Street North who don't live downtown. Plenty of them pick where to eat or drink on the spot, from a phone.

Clients of downtown offices

Accountants and other professional offices get searched by name and by service. For them, a clean profile and a quick site count for more than any location trick.

How search behaves here

When everyone is close, distance stops being your edge

Google says local results come down to three things: relevance, distance and prominence. You can read it in its own words on the Business Profile help page about local ranking.

Here's the problem downtown. Every competitor is about as close to the searcher as you are, so distance cancels out. That leaves relevance (does your profile match the search?) and prominence (are you well known and well reviewed?).

The circle where you show up also tends to be small. A café on Locke Street can be first for someone standing outside and missing for someone a short drive away, because that person has their own nearby options.

Searchers also name the neighbourhood instead of the city. "Brunch James Street North" and "café Locke Street" are different searches from "restaurant Hamilton". A site that only ever says "Hamilton" gives Google nothing to match them with.

What we see in audits

We audited a site where a page built for one part of Toronto had a different neighbourhood's name in its title. The page was saying it was somewhere it wasn't.

Downtown, where customers think street by street, that mistake costs more. Your titles and headings should name the part of town you're really in.

Where we'd start

What we'd fix first for a downtown business

No big rebuild. These are the details that decide crowded map packs.

  1. Put the pin and the address exactly right

    In a building with several units, a missing suite number or a pin on the wrong side of the block sends people to the wrong door. We make your profile, your site and your main listings match, character for character.

  2. Pick the primary category that matches the search

    A James Street North restaurant listed under a vague category, or a print shop listed as a graphic designer, is competing for the wrong searches. The primary category decides what you can show for at all.

  3. Set special hours for event nights

    If you stay open late for the art crawl or Supercrawl, add it to your profile's special hours. Google labels who's open and who's closed right in the results, and a closed label loses the click.

  4. Say where you are the way customers say it

    Your contact and about pages should use the street, the neighbourhood and the nearest GO station. That helps a first-time visitor find you, and it gives Google a clear local signal.

  5. Track rankings from your block

    We run geo-grid tracking in BrightLocal on a tight grid around your location, because downtown results can change from one part of the core to the next.

FAQ

Downtown Hamilton SEO questions

Is SEO different for a downtown business than for one in the suburbs?

Mostly in how crowded it is. With so many businesses close together, being near the searcher gives you less of an advantage, so your category, your reviews and the completeness of your profile carry more of the weight.

Should my website mention Locke Street, James Street North or Westdale?

Yes, where it's true. Describe your location the way a customer would. Don't build a separate page for every street you're near, though. One strong page per service beats a stack of thin ones, and our SEO content work is built around that rule.

Can I add "Downtown Hamilton" to my Business Profile name?

Only if it's part of your real business name, the one on your sign and your paperwork. Google's guidelines ban stuffing keywords or locations into the name, and listings that do it risk suspension.
