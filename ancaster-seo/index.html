Where it is

Up on the escarpment, at Hamilton's southwest edge

Ancaster sits in the southwest corner of the city, on top of the escarpment. It became part of the City of Hamilton in the 2001 amalgamation, and it kept its historic village core on Wilson Street.

Highway 403 and the Linc are both close by. That puts the rest of Hamilton within easy reach, which matters a lot for the businesses here that sell beyond Ancaster.

Commercial life gathers in three places, and they couldn't be more different: the old village core, the Meadowlands shopping area and Ancaster Business Park.

Who works here

One community, three kinds of business

Which one you are decides almost everything about your local search plan.

The village core

Restaurants, cafés and small shops on Wilson Street. Customers walk in. They find you on Maps, check the hours and photos, then come by. Your Business Profile is your front window.

The Meadowlands

A shopping area people drive to with an errand in mind. A search like "print shop near me" from the parking lot is a buying search, and it gets decided fast.

Ancaster Business Park

Offices, contractors and service firms near the highways that sell across the region. Some host clients; many go out to the customer instead.

How search behaves here

Small keyword numbers, big category decisions

SEO searches with "Ancaster" in them are rare: area names like Ancaster get 0–10 a month. Don't read that as "nobody here searches". People search for the thing they need, and Google works out where they are.

That puts the weight on your Business Profile categories. The primary category decides which searches you can show up for at all. Pick the wrong one and no number of reviews fixes it.

Your group changes the setup. A Wilson Street restaurant needs a precise food category, a menu and real photos. A business-park contractor needs a category for each trade it actually does, and a page on the site for each one.

Business-park firms also have a reach question. If your clients are spread across the region, leaning on "Ancaster" alone shrinks your market. An accounting office here usually wants "accountant Hamilton" as much as "accountant Ancaster".

From a real audit

We audited a Business Profile with exactly one category and a services list filled with stock items the business didn't sell. It was telling Google the wrong story about what the company does.

Fixing that is quick. It's also one of the changes that most directly affects which searches you're eligible for.

How to choose Business Profile categories

Our approach

How we'd set up an Ancaster business

  1. Work out which kind of business you are

    Walk-in, drive-to or go-to-the-customer. That single answer decides whether your address shows, which searches to chase and how far your pages need to reach.

  2. Audit the categories and services

    Primary category first, then secondary ones, then the services list. Anything you don't sell comes out. This is the heart of our Business Profile optimization.

  3. Make the website agree with the profile

    Every category you claim needs a page that backs it up. If the profile says kitchen renovation, the site needs a kitchen renovation page, not one line in a list.

  4. Point business-park firms at the whole city

    A firm near the 403 shouldn't limit itself to Ancaster. Its service pages target Hamilton, and Ancaster appears where it's true: the office location, the directions, the meeting room.

  5. Give village-core shops what walkers want

    Current hours, honest photos of the inside and outside, and a menu or price list on the site. People choosing where to walk want to see where they're going before they go.

FAQ

Ancaster SEO questions

Should an Ancaster business target "Ancaster" or "Hamilton"?

It depends on who your customers are. Walk-in businesses win with nearby searchers, whatever words they type. Firms that serve the whole region should aim their main pages at Hamilton and mention Ancaster as home base.

Which Business Profile setting should I check first?

Categories. Too few, the wrong primary one, or a services list copied from a stock set. It's fast to correct and it changes which searches you can appear in.

Our office is in Ancaster Business Park, but clients never visit. Should the address show?

If you don't serve customers at that address, Google's guidelines say to hide it and set your service areas instead. Showing it only sends people to a door that won't open for them.

How do you track rankings for an Ancaster business?

With a geo-grid sized to your customers. A Wilson Street café gets a tight grid around the village. A business-park contractor gets one stretched across Hamilton, because that's where its jobs come from. Same tool, very different maps.

Can you fix the website as well as the profile?

Yes. We build on Wix and as fast static sites, and we work on WordPress. When the site and the profile disagree about what you do, we fix both. See web design in Hamilton.
