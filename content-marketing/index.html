SEO content

SEO content writing for Hamilton service businesses

Google can only rank a page for what it's clearly about. We write one page per service and intent, built on what Hamilton customers really search and ask, then add blog posts that earn their place.

The problem

One page that says everything ranks for nothing.

Here's a page we see all the time: one "Services" page that lists everything. Drains, water heaters, sump pumps, bathroom renovations.

Google reads it and has to guess. Is this a drain page? A renovation page? Often it decides the page isn't the best answer for any of them.

Now picture a customer searching "sump pump installation Stoney Creek". A business with a page about exactly that job, in exactly that place, is the obvious match.

That's what SEO content writing means to us: pages that match one search, written so the reader picks up the phone.

The order we work in

  • Service pages first. One for each service you want calls for.
  • Area pages next, where an area is worth winning. That's part of local SEO.
  • Then blog posts that answer what buyers ask before they call.
  • Then monthly upkeep: better titles on pages that show up but don't get clicked.

One page, one intent

Why every page gets one job.

Intent is what the person actually wants when they type a search. Different intent, different page.

"Drain cleaning Hamilton"

They need someone now. That's a service page: what you do, where you go, how to book, and a phone number they can tap.

"How to unclog a basement drain"

They want to try it themselves. A blog post can help them, then point them to you when the job is bigger than a plunger.

"Drain cleaning cost"

They're comparing prices. That question deserves a clear answer on a page built for it, not a line buried in the footer.

Put two of those on one page and it gets weaker at both. Two pages chasing one search is just as bad. On one site we audited, a new page "replaced" the homepage on six searches, and all six dropped. More on one page, one keyword.

How we plan

How we decide which pages to write.

Before anyone writes a word, we work out which pages you need and in what order. These are steps 2 to 6 of our Core 30 framework.

  1. Crawl your site and your competitors'

    We list every page you have, then find the services and areas your competitors cover and you don't.

  2. Group searches by intent

    Search volume and difficulty come from Ubersuggest. Real search data comes from your Search Console. Searches that want the same page go together.

  3. Mine "People also ask"

    The questions Google shows under a search are the questions your customers have. "How long does a furnace last?" becomes a section, not a guess.

  4. Plan the page architecture

    One page per service and intent, linked in a way that makes sense to Google and to customers.

  5. Write to a set formula

    Every page answers the same core questions in the same order, so nothing important gets missed.

What's on the page

What a service page needs, to rank and to get the call.

  • The service and the place in the title and heading. "Furnace Repair in Stoney Creek" beats "Our Services". A small agency we studied showed why: its one town page titled with the exact search ranked and brought in half its traffic. Its generically titled town pages didn't.
  • Who it's for and when to call. Emergency or planned, homeowner or business.
  • What the job involves, step by step, in plain words.
  • Prices or price ranges, if you're willing to share them. It's one of the first things customers ask.
  • The areas you really serve: the lower city, the Mountain, Ancaster, wherever you actually go.
  • A phone number you can tap and a short quote form.

Our guide to service pages that rank goes through the full formula.

What we won't write

  • Made-up numbers. We've seen a homepage carrying unsupported, made-up-looking figures. That's a risk to fix before you pay for any traffic.
  • Text from another business. We found a live project page whose body came from a template for a company in another country. It tells customers nobody checked the page.
  • Pages for places you don't serve.
  • Filler written to hit a word count.

Then the blog

Blog posts that earn their place.

Once the service pages are in, a blog lets you reach people earlier, while they're still working out what's wrong.

A post on "why is my furnace short cycling" or "what a home inspection covers" answers a real question, then links to the service page that solves it.

Every post supports one service page. A post that supports nothing is usually a post you don't need. Here's when a local business actually needs a blog.

Clear, direct answers are also the kind of text AI assistants can quote. More on that in AI search optimization.

Monthly upkeep: titles that get clicked

Content isn't finished when it's published. Every month we check Search Console for pages ranking 4 to 10 with weak clicks, rewrite their titles and descriptions, then recheck after 30 days.

On our own site, Search Console showed a page at an average position of 3 to 5 with zero clicks from 233 impressions. The ranking was fine. The title wasn't doing its job.

FAQ

SEO content questions.

Do I need a blog to rank in Hamilton?

Not at first. Service pages bring in the calls. A blog helps once those are solid, by answering questions earlier, before someone is ready to book.

How many pages does my site need?

One for each service you want calls for, plus pages for the areas worth targeting. A plumber who does drains, water heaters and sump pumps across Hamilton and Stoney Creek needs far more than a home page and a contact page. A full audit gives you the exact list.

What do you need from me to write the pages?

The facts only you have: the services you sell, the areas you go to, your prices or price ranges, and how you do the job. We ask, you answer, we write. We never fill gaps with invented numbers.

Can I write the pages myself?

Yes, and owners usually know the work best. What's often missing is the structure: one intent per page, the right title, and answers to the questions customers ask.

How much does SEO content writing cost?

It depends on how many pages you need and how much research each one takes. It can be priced per page or as part of a monthly plan. Our service pages are $300 each. The content writing pricing guide explains the options.
