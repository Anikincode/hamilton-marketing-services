Local SEO

Local SEO in Hamilton that gets you into the map pack

The map pack

What local SEO really means in Hamilton.

Picture a homeowner on the Mountain with water coming through the basement wall. They grab a phone and search "emergency plumber Hamilton Mountain". Google shows a map and three businesses. Those three get the first look.

Local SEO is everything that decides whether you're one of those three, and whether you show up in the regular results underneath.

Think of it like the shelf at eye level in a grocery store. The product on the bottom shelf might be just as good. It just gets picked up less.

And the shelf moves. Google ranks map results partly by how close a business is to the person searching. A customer in Waterdown and a customer in Binbrook can type the same words and see different businesses.

So local SEO isn't one ranking. It's dozens of small rankings, one for every corner of your service area.

Two places local customers find you

  • The map pack. The map and three listings at the top of most local searches. It runs on your Google Business Profile, your reviews and how close you are.
  • The organic results. The regular links underneath. They run on your website: its pages, titles, speed and links.

What moves you

The four things that move you on the map.

Google says local results come down to relevance, distance and prominence. You can't move your address. You can work on everything else.

Your Google Business Profile

The right primary category, every real service, accurate hours and real photos. It's the biggest single piece of local SEO, so it has its own page: Google Business Profile optimization.

Reviews, steadily

A routine for asking every happy customer, and a reply from you on every review. A steady flow beats a sudden burst. Here's how to get more Google reviews without breaking the rules.

Citations and NAP

Your name, address and phone number (NAP) written the same way on every directory, social profile and your own site. Mixed details make Google less sure who you are. More on NAP consistency and citations.

Service-area pages

Pages for the places you serve, each with real local substance. If Ancaster is where you want roofing jobs, "roofing Ancaster" deserves a page of its own, not a line in your footer.

What we see

Where local businesses lose the map.

On Sep 20, 2026 we scored 35 live local-business websites on our audit rubric. The average score was 34 out of 100. The local problems repeat from one business to the next.

Two businesses, one phone number

Two separate companies shared one number across their listings. That confuses Google about which business is which, at exactly the moment you want it to be sure.

Three review counts on one site

One website showed three different review counts. When your own site disagrees with itself, nobody knows which number to trust.

One category, wrong services

A Business Profile with a single category and a list of stock services the business doesn't sell. That profile was telling Google the wrong story about the business.

Town pages with vague titles

A small agency we studied had several town pages. The one titled with the exact search people type ranked and brought in half its traffic. The generically titled ones didn't.

None of these needed a bigger budget. They needed someone to look, then fix the boring details. If you want "carpet cleaning Dundas" calls, the page and the profile have to say so, plainly.

Keyword tracking

We track rankings across your whole service area, not from one spot.

Most ranking reports check from a single point. That tells you very little in a city split in two by the escarpment. You might rank first for "furnace repair" near the Linc and not show at all in Stoney Creek.

We use geo-grid tracking in BrightLocal: the same search, checked from a grid of points across your area. You get a map of where you win and where you vanish.

We don't have a Hamilton office, and we don't need one to see Hamilton results. The grid checks from where your customers are. Here's how geo-grid rank tracking works.

What a grid shows you

  • The neighbourhoods where you're strong, and the ones where you're missing.
  • Whether a change helped, point by point.
  • Where a competitor beats you, so we know what to work on.
  • Whether you're slipping, before the phone goes quiet.

How it works

How a local SEO engagement runs.

We follow the same framework every time, so nothing gets skipped. Here's the local version.

  1. Audit your profile categories

    Your primary category decides which searches you can show for. We check it first, then the secondary categories and the services list.

  2. Clean up your name, address and phone

    Every listing and directory says the same thing, and it matches your profile exactly.

  3. Match searches to pages

    We group searches by intent and collect the "People also ask" questions Hamilton customers see. Each service, and each area worth winning, gets one page.

  4. Build or fix the pages

    Written to a set formula, with the service and the place in the title and heading. Our SEO content writing service covers how.

  5. Set up a review routine

    A simple way to ask every happy customer, and the habit of replying to every review, good or bad.

  6. Track and maintain every month

    Geo-grid tracking, plus a Search Console check: pages ranking 4 to 10 with weak clicks get new titles, and we recheck 30 days later.

FAQ

Local SEO questions.

How long does local SEO take in Hamilton?

Fixes to your Business Profile, like correcting the wrong primary category, can show up within weeks. New pages and new areas usually take a few months. It depends on how much is broken and how many competitors already do the basics well.

Can I show in the map pack where I don't have an address?

Distance matters to Google, and you can't fake it. Listing a virtual office or a mailbox breaks Google's rules and can get your profile suspended. What you can do is win the regular results in those areas with honest service-area pages, and get as strong as possible close to where you are.

Do I need a website for local SEO?

You can appear on the map with only a Business Profile. But your website backs up what the profile says, and it's what ranks in the regular results under the map. Without one, you're playing with half the board.

Do you work with businesses in Burlington and Grimsby?

Yes. We work with businesses across Hamilton, Stoney Creek, Ancaster, Dundas, Waterdown and Binbrook, plus Burlington and Grimsby. We're based in Etobicoke, and we work with you by phone, email and video call.

What does local SEO cost?

It depends on how much needs fixing and how many areas you want to win. Our monthly local SEO plans start at $1,000. The free audit tells you what you actually need before you spend anything.
