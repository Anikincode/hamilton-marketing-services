The SEO tools for local SEO we actually use, and why

Agency websites love a wall of logos. Here's ours without the wall: seven tools, what each one does for a local business, and where each one stops being useful.

Kirill Anikin · October 30, 2026 · 6 min read

Key takeaways

  • We run local SEO on seven tools: Google Search Console, Google Business Profile, Ubersuggest, BrightLocal, PageSpeed Insights, Local Viking, and a tool we built that shows the hidden searches ChatGPT runs.
  • Google's own tools measure. Third-party tools estimate. We trust measurement first.
  • For local SEO, rankings have to be tracked across the whole service area, not from one spot. That's why we use geo-grid tracking.
  • A tool is only as good as the fix that follows it.

Plenty of "best SEO tools" lists try to rank every tool on the market. This isn't one of those. It's a short list of the tools we actually use.

We use the same small toolkit across every local business we work with. Here's what each one does, what it's bad at, and how they fit together.

Google Search Console: what really happened

Search Console is Google's free report on your website. It shows which pages are indexed, which searches you appear for, your average position, and how many people click.

It's the one tool on this list that measures rather than estimates, so it's where we start every month.

Here's the kind of thing it catches. On our own site, Search Console showed a page sitting at an average position of 3 to 5 with zero clicks from 233 impressions. That's not a ranking problem. People were seeing the page and choosing something else, which points straight at the title and description. No keyword tool would have shown us that.

That's the heart of our monthly maintenance: find pages ranking 4 to 10 with weak clicks, rewrite their titles, then recheck after 30 days.

Where it falls short: it only covers Google, only your own site, and it doesn't show map pack rankings across your service area. Our guide to Google Search Console for beginners explains the reports.

Google Business Profile: the map pack

For most local searches, the map pack shows up before the regular results. Your Business Profile is what appears there.

We work in the profile itself: categories, services, hours, photos, posts and reviews. The primary category is the first thing we audit, because it decides which searches a business can show up for at all.

It also matters what's listed. We've audited a profile with a single category and a list of stock services the business didn't actually sell. That profile was telling Google the wrong story about what the business does.

Where it falls short: its performance figures show calls, direction requests and website clicks, but not where on the map you rank.

Ubersuggest: how many people search

We use Ubersuggest for keyword volume and difficulty: roughly how many people search a phrase each month, and how hard it is to rank for.

It's how we sized up the Hamilton market before building this site. For example, it puts "hamilton seo" at about 590 searches a month and "seo company hamilton" at about 330, as 12-month averages for Canada. It also showed that every SEO-plus-city search swings about ten times between the busy September-to-January stretch and the quiet spring and summer.

Where it falls short: it estimates. On one site we checked, a keyword tool estimated traffic about five times higher than Search Console showed. We use Ubersuggest to compare phrases against each other, never as proof of what a site gets.

BrightLocal: rankings across the whole service area

This is the tool behind our keyword tracking pillar.

Map pack results change with where the searcher is standing. A furnace company can rank in the top three near its shop in Stoney Creek and be nowhere for someone searching "furnace repair" from Upper James. One ranking from one spot hides that completely.

BrightLocal runs geo-grid tracking: it checks rankings from a grid of points across the service area and shows the result as a map. We also use it for local rank tracking and to check citations, the listings of your business on other sites. We explain the method in geo-grid rank tracking.

Where it falls short: each grid is a snapshot from the day it ran. We look at trends across several runs, not one.

PageSpeed Insights: is the site fast on a phone?

PageSpeed Insights is Google's free speed test. It shows how quickly a page loads, especially on mobile, and what's slowing it down.

The numbers we find can be startling. On one site, the main content took 27.7 seconds to appear on a phone. On another, 9.9 seconds. A customer with water on the basement floor won't wait for either.

Where it falls short: it lists every possible improvement, big and small, so it's easy to waste time on the minor ones. We fix the biggest delays first.

Local Viking: Business Profile posts on a schedule

Posting regularly on a Business Profile keeps it active and gives customers something current to read. Doing that by hand, every day, for several businesses isn't realistic.

We use Local Viking to schedule Business Profile posts in advance. We write the posts, and Local Viking publishes them on the right days. Our article on Google Business Profile posts covers what goes in a good one.

Where it falls short: it schedules, it doesn't write. A schedule full of thin posts is still thin.

Our ChatGPT search tool: what AI is really looking for

When ChatGPT answers a question like "who should I call for a blocked drain in Dundas?", it often runs web searches first. Those searches are usually shorter rewrites of the question, and the customer never sees them.

We built a tool that shows us those hidden searches. Once we know what ChatGPT is searching for in a trade and area, we check whether the business ranks for each one. If it doesn't, we know which page to build or fix.

Where it falls short: AI tools change often, and the same question can trigger different searches on different days. It shows a pattern, not a promise.

What about Ahrefs, Semrush and the rest?

Ahrefs, Semrush, Moz, Screaming Frog and Surfer are well known, and plenty of agencies use them. We don't review them here, because we don't run our client work on them, and we'd rather not pretend otherwise.

For a local service business, the questions that matter are simple. Are your pages indexed? Do you show on the map where your customers are? Do the clicks turn into calls? The tools above answer those directly.

How the tools fit into a month of work

  1. Search Console first: indexing problems, pages losing clicks, pages ranking 4 to 10 with weak click-through.
  2. BrightLocal next: where the map rankings moved across the service area.
  3. Business Profile: categories, services, reviews and anything changed without the owner's knowledge.
  4. PageSpeed Insights on any page we've changed or added.
  5. Local Viking: the next batch of posts written and scheduled.
  6. The ChatGPT tool and Ubersuggest when we're planning new pages.
  7. Recheck after 30 days anything we changed, so we know whether it worked.

That loop is the maintenance step of our process. The full framework is on our methodology page.

FAQ

Can I use these tools myself?

Search Console, your Business Profile and PageSpeed Insights are free. Ubersuggest has a limited free version. BrightLocal and Local Viking are paid. Our list of free SEO tools for small business shows what you can do without paying.

Why don't you use Ahrefs or Semrush?

They're capable tools, but for local service businesses the questions that matter are answered by Google's own data and by geo-grid tracking across the service area. We'd rather use fewer tools well.

How accurate are keyword tools like Ubersuggest?

They're estimates. They're good for comparing phrases, but the numbers can be far off. On one site we checked, a keyword tool's traffic estimate was about five times what Search Console showed.

Who should own my Search Console and Business Profile?

You should. Whoever does your SEO should be added as a user, while you stay the owner. If you ever change providers, your data and your profile stay with your business.
