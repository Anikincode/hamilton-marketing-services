The local SEO checklist for Hamilton businesses

Your Business Profile, reviews, listings, local pages and tracking, as one list you can tick off. Start at the top: the early sections decide whether you show on the map at all.

Kirill Anikin · October 30, 2026 · 8 min read

Key takeaways

  • Your primary Business Profile category decides which searches you can show for. Get it right before anything else.
  • Your name, phone and area should read exactly the same on your profile, your website and every directory.
  • Reviews need a system, not a burst: ask every happy customer, reply to every review, and never pay or filter.
  • Build one page per service. Build area pages only when you have something real to say about the area.
  • Track rankings across your whole service area, not from your own front door.

Local SEO isn't one big project. It's a long list of small jobs, done properly and kept up.

The trouble is that nobody hands you the list. So here it is, in the order we'd work through it for a Hamilton business. Print it, tick it off, and come back to the monthly section every month.

How to use this checklist

Work top to bottom. The early sections are foundations. If your category is wrong or your profile isn't verified, nothing further down will save you.

Next to each line, write Done, Fix or Doesn't apply. A storefront restaurant on Locke Street and a plumber who works out of a van in Binbrook will tick different boxes, and that's fine.

1. Business Profile basics

Your Google Business Profile is your listing on Google Maps and in the map pack. Think of it as your shop sign, phone book entry and opening-hours board rolled into one.

DoneTaskWhy it matters
☐The profile is claimed and verified by the ownerUntil it's verified, you can't fully control what it says
☐The business name is your real-world name, nothing addedGoogle's guidelines ban stuffing keywords like "Emergency Plumber Hamilton" into the name
☐The address shows only if customers come to youService-area businesses can hide their address. Fake addresses are banned
☐Service areas list the places you really workHamilton, Stoney Creek, Ancaster, Dundas: only what you'd actually drive to
☐Hours are correct, including holiday hoursWrong hours mean missed calls and annoyed customers
☐The phone number is a local line used only by this businessA 905, 289 or 365 number that no other business shares
☐The website link goes to the right pageUsually your homepage, or the page for that location
☐The description is written for customersPlain sentences about what you do and where, not a keyword list

Not sure whether to show your address? Read how service-area businesses show up on Google Maps before you change anything.

2. Categories and services

This is the section that matters most. Your primary category decides which searches you can show for in the first place.

DoneTaskWhy it matters
☐The primary category describes the business as a wholeGoogle asks you to pick what your business is, not everything it does
☐Secondary categories are real lines of workEach one opens a different set of searches. Only add work you want calls for
☐The services list matches what you actually sellDelete stock services you don't offer, and add the ones you do
☐Every category has a matching page on your websiteThe profile says what you are. The page proves it

We've audited a profile with one category and a list of stock services the business doesn't even sell. That profile was effectively applying for the wrong jobs. Our guide to choosing Google Business Profile categories walks through the decision step by step.

3. Photos and posts

Photos and posts show a searcher that a real, active business is behind the listing. They're also your chance to show the job, not just describe it.

DoneTaskWhy it matters
☐Photos show your real work, team, vehicles or premisesA finished deck in Waterdown says more than any stock image
☐New photos go up regularlyA profile whose newest photo is years old looks closed
☐Posts go out on a steady scheduleEach one is a fresh reason to click through to a service page
☐Every post's button points at a live pageA broken link wastes the click
☐No phone numbers in post textGoogle may reject them. Use the Call button instead

4. Reviews

Star ratings and review counts sit right there in the map pack, so people compare them. Picture someone on Upper James searching for a plumber. Two listings sit side by side: one with recent, detailed reviews, one with a handful from years ago. Which would you call?

DoneTaskWhy it matters
☐Your review link is saved and ready to sendGet it from your profile's "ask for reviews" option, and make a QR code from it
☐Every finished job gets a review requestAsking everyone is the only fair way, and the only way that scales
☐No discounts, gifts or contests for reviewsGoogle's rules ban incentives
☐No filtering: you don't ask only the happy customersSelectively asking for positive reviews is against Google's rules
☐Every review gets a replyGood or bad, a short, calm reply shows the business is paying attention
☐Your website doesn't show stale review countsWe've seen three different counts on one site. Pick one source, or show none

For scripts, timing and what to do about a bad review, see how to get more Google reviews.

5. Name, address, phone and citations

Citations are mentions of your business on other sites: directories, social profiles, industry listings. NAP is short for name, address and phone. Google compares them, so they need to agree.

DoneTaskWhy it matters
☐You've written down one master version of your name, address and phoneEverything else gets copied from it, character for character
☐Your website footer and contact page match the master versionYour own site should never disagree with your profile
☐Bing Places, Apple Business Connect and your Facebook page matchOther maps and apps show these listings to their own users
☐Industry and local directories you're on are up to dateOld addresses and old numbers hang around for years
☐There's no duplicate Google profile for the same businessDuplicates split your reviews and confuse Google
☐Your phone number isn't shared with another businessWe've seen two separate businesses on one number. Google can't tell them apart

One Hamilton detail worth deciding up front: since amalgamation, some addresses get written as "Stoney Creek, ON" and others as "Hamilton, ON". Pick the form that matches your Business Profile and use it everywhere. More on that in NAP consistency and citations.

6. Your website's local pages

The map pack and your website work as a team. Google says your position in regular web results is also part of how it judges a business for local ranking.

DoneTaskWhy it matters
☐Every service has its own page"Furnace repair" and "AC installation" are different searches, so they need different pages
☐Each service page title names the service and the cityFor example, "Furnace Repair in Stoney Creek", then your business name
☐Each page has one H1 that matches its titleClear headings tell Google what the page is for
☐Area pages exist only where you have real local substanceA page that swaps "Ancaster" for "Dundas" and changes nothing else helps no one
☐The phone number is tap-to-call on every pagePeople who need a local service are often searching on a phone
☐LocalBusiness structured data matches your profileSame name, phone and area as everywhere else

Need the structured data? Our schema starter pack has copy-ready examples for a Hamilton service business.

A quick reality check on area pages: in our own trade, searches naming Stoney Creek, Ancaster or Grimsby run 0–10 a month. Check the numbers for your own trade before building a page for every corner of the city.

7. Tracking

You can't improve what you only check from your own phone. The map pack changes with where the searcher is standing, so one check from your office tells you very little.

DoneTaskWhy it matters
☐You check your Business Profile performance every monthCalls, website clicks and direction requests, straight from Google
☐Rankings are tracked across your service areaA geo-grid shows where you rank from many points, from the lower city to the Mountain
☐Search Console is connected to your websiteIt shows the real searches that brought people to your pages
☐You keep a simple log of changesDate, what changed, why. It turns guesses into answers

8. Every month

Local SEO is more like a garden than a fence. You don't build it once. These are the monthly chores.

DoneTaskWhy it matters
☐Check the profile for edits you didn't makeGoogle and the public can suggest changes to your listing
☐Reply to every new reviewRecent replies show you're paying attention
☐Post and add photosKeep the listing current
☐Find pages ranking 4–10 with weak clicks in Search ConsoleRewrite their titles, then recheck after 30 days
☐Compare this month's geo-grid with last month'sSame searches, same grid, so the comparison is fair

That click-through check catches real money. On our own site, Search Console showed a page sitting at an average position of 3–5 with zero clicks from 233 impressions. That wasn't a ranking problem. It was a title problem.

FAQ

What's the first thing on a local SEO checklist?

Your primary Business Profile category. It decides which searches you can show for, so every other task builds on it. After that, make sure the profile is verified and your name, phone and area are correct.

Do I need an address in Hamilton to show up in Hamilton?

You need a real, eligible location, but you don't have to show it if customers don't visit you. Service-area businesses can hide the address and list the areas they serve. Distance still plays a part in who shows on the map, so a business based in Binbrook will usually find Binbrook easier to win than Burlington.

How long does local SEO take?

It depends on how much needs fixing and how competitive your trade is. Fixing a wrong category or a broken phone number can help quickly. New pages and steady reviews build up over months. Be wary of anyone who promises a date.

Should I make a page for every Hamilton neighbourhood?

Only where you can make each page genuinely different: real jobs there, local details, a reason to exist. A stack of near-identical area pages is more likely to hurt than help.

Can I do this checklist myself?

Yes. Everything here can be done by an owner with a free Google account and some patience. Where people usually want help is the website side and the monthly tracking.
