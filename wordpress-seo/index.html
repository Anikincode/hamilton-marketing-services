Why WordPress sites slip

WordPress gives you every tool. That's the problem.

WordPress is a workshop full of tools. You can build anything. You can also leave sawdust everywhere.

Many WordPress sites we look at weren't built badly. They were built, then added to for years: a plugin here, a page builder there, a theme update nobody tested.

The result is a site that's slow on phones, full of thin pages Google doesn't need, and one careless update away from breaking. For a contractor in Waterdown or a cleaning company on the Mountain, that means fewer calls and no obvious reason why.

Settings

A few checkboxes and templates decide whether Google can index the site and what every title says.

Speed

Every plugin adds code. Page builders add a lot. It all has to load before your phone number appears.

Updates

Skip them and you invite hackers. Rush them and you break titles, forms or layouts.

Settings

The WordPress settings that quietly cost you rankings.

None of these look like problems from the outside. Google sees every one.

"Discourage search engines" left on

WordPress has a checkbox under Settings, then Reading, that asks search engines not to index the site. It's easy to tick during a build and forget at launch. We've audited a live site whose robots.txt blocked all indexing, so it happens.

Messy permalinks

Plain permalinks give pages addresses made of numbers instead of words. Switch to post-name URLs early. If you change them later, redirect every old address.

SEO plugin titles on autopilot

SEO plugins fill in titles from a template. A weak template gives every page the same weak title. We've seen wrong-city titles and H1s on live sites. Picture a drain cleaning page for Ancaster with another city in its title.

Archive and attachment pages

Tag pages, author pages and image attachment pages can pile up into a heap of thin URLs. Decide which ones Google should see and keep the rest out of the index.

Two SEO plugins at once

Run two and you can end up with two sets of titles and two sets of schema. Yoast SEO and Rank Math are the common choices. Pick one and set it up properly.

The indexing checkbox has a cousin in robots.txt. Read the robots.txt mistakes that hide whole websites.

Speed

Why your WordPress site is slow, and how to fix it.

On a phone over mobile data, every plugin, script and oversized photo has to load before your customer sees what they came for.

Slow pages hurt twice. Google measures page experience, and people leave before the page finishes loading. We've seen one site take 27.7 seconds to show its main content on mobile, and another take 9.9.

A homeowner on Upper James with a leaking water heater won't wait that long. They'll tap the next result.

For a plain-English walkthrough, read page speed for local businesses.

Our WordPress speed checklist

  • Test key pages in PageSpeed Insights on mobile, one by one
  • Remove plugins you don't use, and replace heavy ones with lighter options
  • Resize and compress images before they're uploaded
  • Use caching, and check the hosting isn't the bottleneck
  • Cut sliders, video backgrounds and animations at the top of the page

Updates

WordPress updates are an SEO job too.

An out-of-date plugin is an unlocked side door.

When a WordPress site gets hacked, the attacker often adds spam pages or hidden links. Google may warn searchers about the site, and rankings fall while you're busy running your business.

But updates can break things too. A theme update can wipe a custom heading. A plugin update can change your titles or stop a quote form from sending.

That's why we treat updates as part of defensive SEO: protecting the rankings you already have from careless changes.

Safe updates, step by step

  • Back up before every update
  • Test big updates on a staging copy first
  • Check key pages, titles and forms afterwards
  • Remove plugins that are no longer maintained
  • Watch Search Console for sudden drops in indexed pages

WooCommerce

WooCommerce: WordPress SEO plus store problems.

WooCommerce turns WordPress into an online store. It inherits every WordPress issue above and adds a few of its own.

A Hamilton print shop selling banners online needs its "custom banners" category page to rank, not just its homepage. That's a different job from ranking a service page.

Our ecommerce SEO page covers category pages, product pages and duplicate content in more depth.

What we check on WooCommerce stores

  • Category pages with no text, missing the bigger searches
  • Filters and sorting that create endless duplicate URLs
  • Product descriptions copied from the manufacturer
  • Product schema that's missing, incomplete or doubled up by two plugins
  • Discontinued products deleted instead of redirected

FAQ

WordPress SEO questions.

Is WordPress better for SEO than Wix?

Neither platform ranks a site on its own. WordPress gives you more control and more ways to break things. Wix gives you less control and fewer traps. A well-built site on either can rank.

Which SEO plugin should I use?

Yoast SEO and Rank Math are the common choices. Which one matters less than running only one, and setting up its title templates and indexing rules properly.

Why is my WordPress site so slow?

Usually a mix of too many plugins, a heavy page builder or theme, uncompressed images and slow hosting. Test a page in PageSpeed Insights on mobile to see what's holding it back.

Will you rebuild my WordPress site?

We build new sites on Wix or as fast static sites, and we work on existing WordPress sites. If yours is sound, fixing it usually costs less than a rebuild and keeps your rankings safer.

Do you handle WordPress updates?

whether we offer WordPress update and maintenance plans. Either way, updates should be backed up, tested and checked afterwards, because an update that breaks a title or a form costs you calls.
