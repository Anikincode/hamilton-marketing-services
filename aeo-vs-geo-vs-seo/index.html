AEO vs GEO vs SEO: what they mean for a local business

Three acronyms, one goal: getting your business picked when someone asks a question. Here's what each one means, and which parts you actually need to act on.

Kirill Anikin · October 30, 2026 · 5 min read

Key takeaways

  • SEO gets you into Google's results and the map. AEO gets your content used as the direct answer. GEO gets your business named in answers written by AI tools.
  • Most of the work overlaps. Clear pages, consistent business details, reviews and structured data help with all three.
  • For a local business, the big change is that facts about you need to match everywhere, because AI tools pull from many sources at once.
  • Be wary of anyone selling GEO as a separate, expensive product that replaces SEO.

Every so often, someone invents a new acronym and a new invoice to go with it. AEO and GEO are the latest.

The ideas behind them are real, though. People do ask ChatGPT who to call. So here's what each term means in plain English, and what you actually need to do about it.

SEO: search engine optimization

SEO is the work of making your business show up when people search on Google. For a local business, that means two places: the regular results (the blue links) and the map pack, the box of three businesses under the map.

Example: someone on the Mountain searches "emergency plumber Hamilton Mountain". SEO is what puts your business in front of them.

AEO: answer engine optimization

AEO is the work of making your content the answer itself. Instead of a list of links, the searcher gets a direct reply: a featured snippet at the top of Google, a "People also ask" box or a voice assistant reading an answer out loud.

Example: someone searches "how often should a furnace be serviced". If your HVAC page answers that in one clear sentence near the top, you have a shot at being the answer, with your name attached.

AEO isn't really new. It's what good SEO pages have done for years: answer the question plainly, then explain.

GEO: generative engine optimization

GEO is the work of getting your business named in answers that AI tools write: ChatGPT, Gemini, Google's AI Overviews and others. These tools don't hand back a list. They write a reply, and sometimes they name businesses in it.

Example: someone asks ChatGPT, "Who's a good furnace repair company in Stoney Creek?" The answer might name a few companies. GEO is the work of being one of them.

Here's what most people don't know. To answer a question like that, ChatGPT can run web searches in the background. We built a tool that shows those hidden searches. If your business doesn't show up for the searches ChatGPT runs, it's much less likely to be named in the answer.

How the three overlap

Where you show upGoogle's results and the map packFeatured answers, "People also ask", voice assistantsAnswers written by AI tools
What winsRelevant pages, a strong Business Profile, reviews, linksShort, direct answers to real questionsConsistent facts, clear pages, mentions across the web
What you measureRankings, clicks and callsWhich questions you answerWhether AI names you, and for which questions

Look at the "what wins" row. It's mostly the same work. A clear page about furnace repair in Stoney Creek, with plain answers and your real details, helps with all three.

What actually changes for a local business

Your facts need to match everywhere

AI tools pull from your website, your Business Profile, directories and reviews. When those sources disagree, they have less reason to trust any of them.

We've seen two separate businesses sharing one phone number across their listings, which confuses Google about which business is which. We've seen one website showing three different review counts. Mess like that gives an AI tool nothing solid to repeat.

Answers need to be quotable

An AI tool can't quote a wall of text. It can quote "We repair and replace furnaces across Hamilton, Stoney Creek and Ancaster." Put plain facts where they're easy to find: what you do, where you do it, when you're open and how to reach you.

Structured data and llms.txt

Structured data, often called schema, is code that labels your business details so machines can read them without guessing. llms.txt is a newer, simpler idea: a plain text file that summarizes your site for AI tools.

Support for llms.txt varies, so treat it as cheap insurance, not a magic switch. Our explainers on schema markup for AI search and llms.txt go into both.

Rankings still matter

Because AI tools often search the web before they answer, businesses that already rank well have a head start. GEO doesn't replace SEO. It sits on top of it.

What doesn't change

  • One page per service and intent.
  • A Business Profile with the right category and real reviews.
  • A fast site that Google can read.
  • Honest, specific content written from real experience.

Where a local business should start, in order

You don't need three strategies. You need one list, done in the right order. Here's the order we'd use for a Hamilton service business.

  1. Your Business Profile. The right primary category, every real service listed and a steady flow of reviews. This feeds the map pack, and it's one of the clearest public records of what you do and where.
  2. One page per service, with the answer up top. Your "drain cleaning Dundas" page should say in its first lines what you do, where and how to book. That one habit serves SEO, AEO and GEO at once.
  3. Matching details everywhere. Same name, address, phone number and hours on your site, your profile and every directory listing.
  4. Structured data. Label your business details in code, so nothing has to be guessed.
  5. Check the answers. Ask ChatGPT and Gemini the questions your customers ask, like "who installs furnaces in Ancaster?", and note who gets named. Repeat every few months.

Notice that only the last step is truly "AI work". The rest is good local SEO done properly.

If an agency pitches GEO as a separate product that replaces all of that, be careful. Our guide to AI SEO pricing covers what's genuinely worth paying for.

Common questions about AEO, GEO and SEO

Is SEO dead because of AI?

No. AI tools still need sources, and many of them search the web to find those sources. Ranking well makes you easier to find for people and AI tools alike.

Do I need a separate AEO or GEO agency?

For most local businesses, no. The work overlaps so much that it belongs inside your SEO. A good SEO company should already be writing quotable answers, adding structured data and keeping your details consistent.

Can I check whether ChatGPT recommends my business?

Yes. Ask it the questions your customers would ask, in a fresh chat, and see who it names. Our guide on checking if AI recommends your business shows how to do it properly.

Which matters most for a Hamilton plumber or HVAC company?

SEO and the map pack, first. Someone with a burst pipe wants a phone number fast, and the map pack gives them one. Most of the AEO and GEO work comes along with doing SEO properly.
