Free checklist

SEO audit checklist for Hamilton business websites

Every check we'd run first on a local business website, grouped in the order that matters, with the free tool for each one. Work down it and fix the top sections first.

Kirill Anikin · October 30, 2026 · 9 min read

Key takeaways

  • Start with the checks that can stop Google seeing your site at all: robots.txt, indexing and redirects. Nothing else counts until those pass.
  • Many problems we find are simple ones: missing H1 headings, the wrong city in a title, text left over from someone else's template.
  • Your Google Business Profile belongs in the audit. A wrong category or a shared phone number can cost more calls than any page.
  • Use Google Search Console for real numbers. Keyword tools estimate. Search Console measures.
  • Date every audit and keep the old copies, so you can prove what changed.

Most local websites don't lose to better competitors. They lose to small, fixable mistakes nobody ever checked for.

On September 20, 2026, we scored 35 live local-business websites on our audit rubric. The average was 34 out of 100. The businesses weren't bad. Missing headings, blocked pages and stale phone numbers had simply piled up for years.

This SEO audit checklist follows the order we work in. You can run most of it yourself with free tools and a phone.

Before you start: what you need

  • Google Search Console access for your site. It's free, and it's the only place that shows how Google actually sees your pages.
  • PageSpeed Insights, Google's free speed test.
  • Your Google Business Profile login.
  • A phone. Test your site the way most of your customers see it.

Mark each line Pass, Fix or Not sure. "Not sure" is a perfectly good answer. It just means that line goes to whoever runs your website.

Save a dated copy. Name it with today's date, for example "2026-10-30 SEO audit", and never overwrite the last one. The old copy is how you prove something got fixed, or that it didn't.

1. Can Google find and index your pages?

This section comes first because a failure here switches everything else off. A great service page that Google won't index is a billboard facing a wall.

We once audited a live site whose robots.txt blocked all indexing. Google had been told to ignore the whole site, and the owner had no idea.

DoneCheckHow to checkPass when
☐robots.txt doesn't block the siteOpen yourdomain.ca/robots.txt in a browserNo "Disallow: /" line under "User-agent: *", unless you meant it
☐The site is verified in Search ConsoleSearch Console property listYou can open the Performance report
☐A sitemap is submittedSearch Console, SitemapsStatus says Success, and the page count looks like your real site
☐Your service pages are indexedSearch Console, PagesEvery service page appears as indexed
☐No money page is set to "noindex"URL Inspection on each service pageIt reports the URL is on Google
☐Only one version of the site loadsType the address with http, https, with www and withoutAll four land on the same address
☐"Discovered – currently not indexed" isn't swallowing the siteSearch Console, Pages, the reasons tableOnly a few minor pages sit there

That last line matters more than it looks. One site we audited had Google indexing about 5 of its 48 pages. The rest sat in Search Console as "Discovered – currently not indexed". If that's you, read what "Discovered – currently not indexed" means and how to fix it.

Picture a furnace company on the Mountain. If its "furnace repair Hamilton Mountain" page isn't on the indexed list, it can't rank, however good the writing is.

2. Does every page say clearly what it's for?

Titles and headings are the signs over the aisles in a hardware store. If the sign says "Paint" and the shelf holds plumbing parts, nobody finds anything.

These failures turn up in our audits, and they're cheap to fix. One business we audited had 9 of 10 service pages missing an H1 heading, still unfixed after four audits.

DoneCheckHow to checkPass when
☐Every page has its own title naming the service and the areaHover the browser tab, or check your editor's SEO settingsFor example, "Drain Cleaning in Stoney Creek", then your business name
☐Every page has exactly one H1Your editor's heading styles, or view the page source and search for "<h1"One H1 per page, naming the service
☐The city in the title and H1 matches the pageRead each one out loudYour Ancaster page says Ancaster, not Dundas or Toronto
☐No text left over from a templateSearch each page for other city names and phone numbersOnly your cities, your number and your projects
☐One page per serviceList your services beside your pagesEvery service has a page, and no two pages chase the same search
☐Meta descriptions are written, not blank or copiedYour editor's SEO settingsEach one is different and gives a reason to click
☐Service pages are easy to reachStart at the homepage and clickEvery service page is in the menu or linked from the homepage

Wrong cities and leftover template text deserve their own warning. We've found a paragraph naming a different Ontario city repeated on several service pages, a project page copied from a template for a company in another country, and a fake phone number from a cloned template sitting on 19 pages.

Wrong cities also show up in titles. One page targeting a part of Toronto had a different neighbourhood in its title. Here's how to write title tags for local SEO so that doesn't happen to you.

And watch for two pages fighting over one search. A new page on one site "replaced" the homepage on six searches, and all six dropped.

3. Is your Google Business Profile telling the same story?

For many local searches, the map shows up before the regular results. So your Business Profile is part of your SEO, even though it doesn't live on your website.

DoneCheckHow to checkPass when
☐The primary category describes the whole businessEdit profile, then Business category"Plumber" for a plumber, not one narrow service
☐Every secondary category is realSame screenEach one is work you'd happily take a call for
☐The services list is yoursEdit servicesNothing you don't sell, and nothing important missing
☐Name, phone and address match the website exactlyCompare them side by sideSame spelling, same number, same suite
☐The phone number belongs to this business aloneSearch the number on GoogleOnly your business comes up
☐The website shows one review count, or noneSearch your own site for "reviews"No old banners that disagree with each other
☐Hours are right, including holidaysEdit profile, then HoursThey match what you'd tell a caller

Each of these comes from a real audit. We've seen a profile with one category and a list of stock services the business doesn't even sell. We've seen two separate businesses sharing one phone number across their listings, which leaves Google guessing which business is which. And we've seen three different review counts on the same website.

For the full profile walkthrough, use our local SEO checklist.

4. Does it load fast on a phone?

Someone standing in a wet basement in Dundas isn't sitting at a desk. They're on a phone, and they'll tap the next result if yours stalls.

Speed problems can be dramatic. On one site we audited, the main content took 27.7 seconds to appear on mobile. On another, 9.9 seconds. That measure is called Largest Contentful Paint, and PageSpeed Insights reports it for you.

DoneCheckHow to checkPass when
☐Mobile speed on your homepage and best service pagePageSpeed Insights, Mobile tabLargest Contentful Paint is labelled good, or close to it
☐Images are sized for the webThe suggestions list in PageSpeed InsightsNo huge photos straight off a camera
☐The phone number is tap-to-callTap it on your own phoneIt dials
☐The quote form works on a phoneSend yourself a test requestIt arrives in your inbox, and the visitor sees a thank-you message

5. Are links and redirects protecting what you've earned?

Every page that ranks is a small asset. Delete it without a redirect and you throw that asset away.

We've audited sites with pages deleted and no redirect, including one that ranked for a search with real monthly volume. It's an easy way for a redesign to quietly cost a business its calls.

DoneCheckHow to checkPass when
☐Deleted or renamed pages redirectSearch Console, Pages, "Not found (404)"Every old address that had visits or links points to its closest live page
☐No broken links on your own siteClick through the menu and footer, or run a crawler toolEvery internal link works
☐The sites linking to you look normalSearch Console, LinksMostly real businesses, directories and local sites

Strange links happen. We found a network of 60 spam domains pointing links at one small business site, and built a disavow file for it. But go carefully. Google says most sites never need the disavow tool, and using it wrongly can do harm.

6. Can people trust what the site says?

Google says it wants to show trustworthy pages, and trust is certainly what decides whether someone calls. A site that makes claims it can't back up is a risk, especially before you pay for ads to send people there.

DoneCheckHow to checkPass when
☐Every number on the site is true and currentRead the homepage and About pageNo round-number boasts you can't prove
☐Photos show your own workLook at the gallery and service pagesReal jobs, real vans, real people
☐Contact details are on every pageCheck the header or footerPhone and service area are always one glance away
☐A real person stands behind the businessRead the About pageIt names the owner and says what they do

We've flagged a homepage carrying made-up-looking numbers. If you can't source it, cut it.

7. Can you measure what's working?

If you can't see which searches bring calls, every SEO decision is a guess.

DoneCheckHow to checkPass when
☐Someone opens Search Console every monthAsk who, and when they last didThere's a name and a date
☐Calls and form requests are countedYour analytics or call logYou know last month's numbers
☐Google Ads conversions are tracked, if you run adsThe conversions section of your Ads accountCalls and forms show up as conversions
☐Traffic is judged in Search Console, not a keyword toolCompare the twoDecisions use Search Console's numbers

Two real examples explain why. We've audited Google Ads accounts running with no conversion tracking, so nobody could tell which clicks became calls. And we've seen a keyword tool estimate a site's traffic about five times higher than Search Console showed. Tools model. Search Console measures.

What to fix first

You'll probably end up with more "Fix" marks than you hoped. That's normal. Work in this order:

  1. Anything that blocks indexing. Section 1, always first.
  2. Wrong information. The wrong city, the wrong phone number, someone else's text.
  3. Titles and H1s on service pages. Quick to change, and they tell Google what each page is for.
  4. Your Business Profile category and services.
  5. Mobile speed.
  6. Everything else.

Fix the roof leak before you repaint the kitchen. A faster site won't help a page Google can't index.

FAQ

How often should I run an SEO audit?

Every few months, and always after a redesign, a platform move or a big batch of edits. Save each one with its date, so you can compare like with like and prove what improved.

Can I do an SEO audit myself?

Most of this checklist, yes. Search Console, PageSpeed Insights and your Business Profile are all free. Our guide on whether you can do SEO yourself covers where owners usually get stuck and when it's worth bringing someone in.

How is this different from a technical SEO audit?

This checklist covers the whole picture at owner level: indexing, pages, profile, speed, trust and tracking. A technical audit goes deeper into crawling, redirects, speed and structured data. If you want to log that work properly, use our technical SEO audit template.

Do I need paid tools to audit my site?

Not for this list. Paid crawlers and keyword tools exist and can speed things up, but every check here works with free Google tools and a phone.
