Generative engine optimization: what GEO means for a local business, minus the hype

Every few years SEO gets a new acronym and a new line on the invoice. Strip away the hype around GEO and there's something real underneath. Most of it is work a good local SEO should already be doing.

Kirill Anikin · October 30, 2026 · 6 min read

Key takeaways

  • Generative engine optimization (GEO) means making your business easy for AI tools like ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and Google's AI Overviews to find, understand and quote.
  • For a local business, most of GEO is SEO done properly: reachable pages, clear answers, consistent details and a real reputation.
  • What's genuinely new: AI tools run their own hidden searches, read several sources and write a summary. You need to rank for those searches and be easy to quote.
  • Be wary of anyone selling GEO as a separate, secret product with guaranteed mentions.

First it was SEO. Then local SEO, voice search, AEO. Now it's GEO, and someone has probably already offered to sell it to you.

Here's the good news. Generative engine optimization isn't a mystery, and it isn't a separate universe. For a plumber on the Mountain or a landscaper in Waterdown, it's mostly the same work that wins on Google, with a few genuinely new habits on top.

What is generative engine optimization?

A "generative engine" is an AI tool that writes an answer instead of showing a list of links. ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Microsoft Copilot and Google's AI Overviews all work this way.

Generative engine optimization is the work of getting your business included, and described correctly, in those answers.

Classic SEO asks: "Does my page rank?" GEO asks: "When an AI writes an answer about my trade in my area, does it name me, and does it get my details right?"

You'll also see AEO (answer engine optimization) and a few other labels. They overlap heavily. If you want them defined side by side, our guide to AEO vs GEO vs SEO does exactly that. This article sticks to what GEO means in practice.

How do generative engines find and pick businesses?

Most of them follow the same rough loop: search, read, summarize, cite.

Ask "who's good for kitchen renovations in Ancaster?" and the tool runs one or more web searches, reads a handful of the pages that come back, writes a summary and, in many cases, links to its sources.

None of these tools publish exactly how they choose which businesses to name. But the loop tells you what they need: to find you, to understand you, and to see enough agreement about you to feel safe repeating it.

The four jobs of GEO for a local business

1. Be reachable

If crawlers can't get in, nothing else counts. We once audited a live business website whose robots.txt told search engines to ignore the entire site. Check that search engines, and the AI crawlers you want, are allowed in, and that your important pages are actually indexed.

2. Be quotable

AI tools lift sentences. Write pages that answer real questions in the first line or two, in plain English, with specifics.

"We install heat pumps across Waterdown, Dundas and Ancaster, and most installs take a day" is quotable, if it's true for you. "We provide comprehensive HVAC solutions" is not. One page per service and one intent per page makes this much easier.

Add something only you can say: what you see on real jobs, how you price, what customers ask you. Pages that repeat what every other site says give an AI no reason to pick yours.

3. Be consistent

AI tools cross-check. If your website, Business Profile, Bing listing and directories disagree on your name, phone or area, you look uncertain.

We've audited a website showing three different review counts, and two separate businesses sharing one phone number across their listings. Both are the kind of contradiction that makes a machine hesitate.

4. Be mentioned

Your own site is one voice. Reviews, directories, local news, supplier pages and association lists are others. When several independent sources describe the same Stoney Creek roofer, an AI has something to stand on.

What's actually different from classic SEO?

If most of GEO is SEO, what's new? A few things matter.

Hidden searches. AI tools often turn one question into several searches you never see. Google has described its AI Mode doing this, splitting a question into many related searches at once. We use a tool we built that reveals the hidden searches ChatGPT runs, so we can check whether a business ranks for them. That's the single most practical GEO technique we know.

Agreement across sources. A page can rank on its own. An AI answer tends to rest on several sources lining up. That makes consistency and outside mentions more important than before.

Answers move. Rankings shift slowly. AI answers can change between two tries of the same question. Measuring GEO means looking for patterns, not snapshots.

Mentions without clicks. Being named in an answer can bring a phone call without a website visit. That's harder to measure, but it's still a customer.

How to measure GEO without fooling yourself

  • Keep a fixed list of customer questions and test them regularly, in several tools, from a clean session. Our guide on checking whether AI recommends your business sets this up.
  • Record three things each time: were you named, which sources were cited, and were your details right?
  • Watch searches for your business name in Search Console. Some people who hear your name in an AI answer will look you up on Google afterwards, and those searches show up there.
  • Ask callers how they found you. It's old-fashioned and it works.

GEO red flags

New labels attract new sales pitches. Be careful with:

  • Guaranteed AI mentions. Nobody controls what ChatGPT or Gemini says. Google warns that nobody can guarantee a ranking, and AI answers are even less predictable.
  • Mass-produced "AI-optimized" content. Dozens of thin pages aimed at every possible question tend to make a site weaker, not stronger.
  • Impressive numbers with nothing behind them. We've flagged unsupported figures on a local business homepage as a risk before. On a page written for AI to quote, a made-up number can be repeated to customers as fact.
  • A separate GEO retainer on top of SEO that isn't being done. If the basics aren't in place, GEO has nothing to build on. Our guide to AI search optimization pricing explains what to be wary of.

FAQ

Is generative engine optimization replacing SEO?

No. AI tools find businesses largely through search, so SEO is the foundation GEO stands on. Think of GEO as SEO with extra attention to being quotable, consistent and mentioned.

Do I need a separate GEO agency?

Usually not. For a local business, the work overlaps so heavily with good local SEO that splitting it between two companies tends to create gaps, not fill them.

How long does GEO take to work?

About as long as SEO. Fixing access and accuracy problems can show up within weeks. Building reviews, mentions and rankings for the hidden searches takes months.

What's the difference between GEO and AEO?

They overlap so much that people often use them interchangeably. Our guide to AEO vs GEO vs SEO sets out the definitions and what each means for a local business.
