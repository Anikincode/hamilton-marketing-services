How customers search

Renovation buyers search by project, not by trade

Hardly anyone types "general contractor" and hires the first result. They search for the thing they want built, then spend weeks looking at photos, reading reviews and asking friends.

That changes what SEO for contractors looks like. The searches are specific, and each one is a different buyer with a different budget:

  • "kitchen renovation Ancaster"
  • "basement finishing Stoney Creek"
  • "home addition contractor Waterdown"
  • "deck builder Binbrook"
  • "bathroom renovation Dundas"

Where emergencies fit

Most renovation work is planned. The exceptions are repairs: a storm-damaged roof, a collapsed deck stair, water damage after a leak. If you take that work, give it its own page so it doesn't get lost under your showcase projects.

Think of it like a restaurant with a dining room and a takeout window. Same kitchen, different customers, different door.

Google Business Profile

Choose the category that names the work you want most

Contractors have more category choices than almost any other trade. That's a trap as much as an opportunity.

Your primary category should match the jobs you most want more of. If that's full renovations, "General contractor" or "Remodeler" fits. If it's kitchens, "Kitchen remodeler" can be sharper. Then add secondaries only for real lines of work:

  • Kitchen remodeler and Bathroom remodeler, if you do them often.
  • Deck builder, Fence contractor or Concrete contractor for exterior specialists.
  • Custom home builder, only if you really build homes from the ground up.
  • Handyman/Handywoman/Handyperson, only if you want small jobs. It will bring them.

Reviews when you only do a few jobs a year

A plumber might finish dozens of jobs a month. A renovator might finish a handful. Fewer jobs means fewer reviews, so each one carries more weight.

Ask at the walkthrough, when the client is standing in the finished kitchen and happiest. Suggest they mention the project type and the area. "Basement in Stoney Creek, finished on schedule" helps the next reader far more than "great job".

Never pay for reviews or offer a discount in exchange. Google's rules ban it.

Your website

Service pages and project pages do different jobs

A service page answers "do you do this, and what does it involve?" A project page answers "can you really do it, and what does it look like?" A contractor site needs both.

One page per project type

Kitchens, bathrooms, basements, additions, decks, whole-home renovations. Each with its own process, timeline questions and permits overview.

Real project write-ups

The area, the problem, what you built and real photos from start to finish. No stock images. No borrowed text.

Answers to planning questions

How long a basement takes, what drives a kitchen budget, whether they need to move out. These questions decide who gets the call.

Project pages are where contractors can say something no competitor can, because nobody else built that exact kitchen. That's the idea behind information gain, and it's why we build sites around real work. See our web design for Hamilton businesses.

Ads and the calendar

Paid search and the building season

Renovation leads are valuable, so the paid options deserve a careful look.

Local Services Ads

In Canada, Local Services Ads cover a set list of trade categories, and some contractor specialties are on it while others aren't. Check yours when you apply.

For general renovation work, regular search ads on project terms like "basement finishing Hamilton" are the usual paid route. Set up call and form tracking before spending a dollar. Our Google Ads management starts there.

Seasons

Outdoor work like decks, fences and additions depends on the weather. Interior work like basements and kitchens can run all year.

Plan content the same way. Publish deck and exterior pages over the winter so they're ranking when people start planning, and push basement and kitchen content when outdoor season winds down.

Common website problems

Why good contractors get outranked

Most contractor websites are built to impress. Fewer are built to be understood by Google.

  • One "Services" page for everything. Kitchens, basements and decks all crammed together, so no single search has a page that fits it.
  • Pages fighting each other. A "Renovations" page and a "Home Renovations" page aimed at the same search. On one site we audited, a new page took over six searches from the homepage, and all six dropped. More in one page, one keyword.
  • Galleries without words. Fifty photos and no captions tell Google almost nothing. A few sentences per project change that.
  • No service area. If the site never says you work in Ancaster, Dundas or Waterdown, Google has to guess.

When we start with a contractor, we audit the Business Profile categories, map every project type to one page, then write the missing pages in order of job value. Our SEO content service handles the writing.

FAQ

Contractor SEO questions

Should my primary category be General contractor or Remodeler?

Pick the one that describes the jobs you most want. If you build additions and manage whole projects, "General contractor" fits. If you mostly renovate kitchens and bathrooms, a remodeler category is closer. Test one change at a time and watch your map results.

Do project photos help my rankings?

Photos alone help people more than they help Google. Pair them with a short write-up: where the job was, what you changed and why. That gives Google something to read and gives buyers a reason to call.

Should I build a page for every town I work in?

Only where you have real work to show. A Waterdown page with three Waterdown projects is useful. A page with the town name swapped into the same text isn't, and it can drag the site down.

How long until SEO brings in renovation leads?

Profile fixes can help within weeks. New project and service pages tend to need a few months before they rank. Because renovation buyers plan ahead, the best time to start is a season before you need the work.
