How clients search

A slow, visual decision

Nobody needs an interior designer by tonight. Clients browse for weeks, save photos, ask friends and come back to the same few portfolios. Your job in search is to be one of those few.

The searches are specific to the room, the style or the stage of a project:

  • "interior designer Ancaster"
  • "kitchen design Hamilton"
  • "interior decorator Dundas"
  • "home staging Burlington"
  • "office interior design downtown Hamilton"

Google is only one stop

Many clients find designers through social media and referrals first. Then they search your name on Google to check you out.

That means two jobs. Rank for the searches above, and make sure your own name brings up a complete profile, good reviews and a portfolio that matches what they saw elsewhere.

Design is less seasonal than most trades, but it has a rhythm. Staging follows the listings market, and homeowners often start planning a renovation months before any contractor arrives. That long lead time is good news for SEO: a project page published today can still be bringing in the right client a year from now.

Google Business Profile

Designer, decorator or stager?

For this trade, picking the category is partly a professional question, not just a marketing one.

  • Interior designer. The obvious primary, but check that your professional standing allows the title first. confirm Ontario rules on who may use the title "interior designer"
  • Interior Decorator. The fit for styling, colour, furniture and finishes work.
  • Home staging service. If you stage homes for sale as a regular service.
  • Kitchen remodeler. Only if you manage the renovation itself, not just the design.

Pick the closest match Google offers during setup; its category names get revised from time to time.

Studio or service area?

If clients visit your studio, show the address. If you always go to them, choose the service-area option and name the towns you work in, like Ancaster, Dundas and Burlington.

Either way, keep your business name as it really is. Adding "Best Interior Designer Hamilton" to the name breaks Google's guidelines.

Your website

A portfolio Google can actually read

Google can't admire a beautiful photo. It reads words. A gallery of fifty images with no text is, to a search engine, nearly a blank page.

Service pages

Full-home design, kitchens and bathrooms, single rooms, e-design, staging and commercial spaces. One page each, with your process and how you charge.

Project stories

The brief, the constraints, the choices and the result. A title like "Opening up a dark living room in Westdale" says far more than "Living Room 3".

Image descriptions

Short alt text that says what's in each photo. It helps people using screen readers, and it helps Google understand the image.

Project stories are where you add something no other site has: your reasoning. That's what Google calls helpful, original content, and it's the idea behind information gain. Our SEO content service can help you write them.

Speed, reviews and ads

Beautiful, but slow, is still slow

Design websites tend to be heavy. Big photos, sliders and video backgrounds look great on a studio monitor and crawl on a phone.

One local site we audited needed 27.7 seconds before its main content showed up on a mobile phone. Most people give up long before that. Resizing and compressing images, and loading them only when they scroll into view, fixes most of it. Read page speed for local businesses.

Reviews

Design clients write longer, more personal reviews, but there are fewer of them. Ask at the reveal, when the room is finished and the client is delighted. Reviews that mention communication and staying on budget carry the most weight with the next client.

Ads

Local Services Ads in Canada are for the trades, so interior design isn't eligible. Search ads on specific terms like "kitchen designer Hamilton" can work, but only with a fast landing page and tracking on the consultation form.

When we build design sites, speed is decided before the first photo goes up. That's how we approach website design for every Hamilton client.

AI search

When people ask AI for a designer

More clients now ask ChatGPT or Google's AI Overviews for help: "Who does modern kitchen design in Ancaster?" Those answers draw on websites, listings and reviews.

  1. Say plainly what you do

    Your services, style, areas and how you work, in clear sentences an assistant can quote.

  2. Keep your details consistent

    Same name, phone and website on your profile, your site and every directory.

  3. Show real work

    Project stories with locations and outcomes give AI tools something specific to point to.

FAQ

Interior designer SEO questions

Do I need SEO if most clients come from referrals?

Referrals still search your name before they call. SEO makes sure what they find looks as good as the recommendation. And it brings in clients who don't know anyone who knows you.

Should I choose Interior designer or Interior Decorator?

Choose the one that accurately describes your work and your professional standing. If you do both design and decorating, the other can be a secondary category.

How many photos should a portfolio project have?

Enough to tell the story, before and after, the details and the full room, plus a few paragraphs about the project. A short story with good photos beats a long gallery with no words.

Does home staging need its own page?

Yes. Staging clients are usually sellers or their agents working to a listing date, which is a very different person from a family planning a full redesign. A separate page lets each one find the service they came for, in words that fit them.

Should I put my design fees on the website?

If you can, explain how you charge: hourly, flat fee or by project. It saves you from calls that were never a fit and helps the right clients book with confidence.
