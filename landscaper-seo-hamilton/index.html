How customers search

Three kinds of landscaping customer

The person who wants a new backyard, the person who wants the lawn cut every week, and the person whose driveway is buried. All three search for "landscaping", but they're buying very different things.

Design and build

Big, planned, photo-driven. They browse for weeks before calling.

"interlock patio Stoney Creek" · "landscape design Ancaster" · "retaining wall Dundas"

Recurring maintenance

Lower ticket, but worth the most over a season. They want reliability and a clear price.

"lawn care Waterdown" · "weekly lawn mowing Hamilton Mountain" · "spring cleanup Binbrook"

Urgent and weather-driven

Snow on the driveway, branches down after a storm. These people call fast and decide on proximity.

"snow removal Binbrook" · "storm cleanup Hamilton" · "fall cleanup near me"

Each group needs its own page, its own words and often its own season. A single "Landscaping Services" page asks one page to sell a patio, a mowing contract and a snow plan at once. It's like a garden centre putting every plant on one shelf.

Google Business Profile

Categories that fit a landscaping company

"Landscaper" is the natural primary category for most companies. The secondary categories should follow how you really make money.

  • Landscaper. The primary for most design-build and full-service companies.
  • Lawn care service. If mowing and maintenance contracts are a real line. For a lawn-only company, this may be the better primary.
  • Landscape designer. If you sell design as its own service, with drawings.
  • Snow removal service. If you plow or clear walks in winter.
  • Paving contractor or Lawn sprinkler system contractor. Only for companies that do driveways or irrigation as regular work.
  • Tree service. Only if you have the crew and equipment for it.

Google's category names change occasionally. Choose the closest one it offers you.

Your profile is a seasonal shop window

Landscaping is visual, and the Business Profile is where people see your work first. Add fresh photos of finished jobs through the season: a new patio in Ancaster, a cleaned-up yard on the Mountain.

Posts should change with the calendar. Spring cleanup offers in March, patio builds in summer, fall cleanup in October, snow contracts before the first storm. Here's how to write Business Profile posts that work.

Service pages

Pages to build, and when to build them

The landscaping calendar runs the page plan. A page needs time to be found and trusted, so each one should go live a season ahead of its demand.

  1. Winter: design and hardscape

    Landscape design, interlock patios, walkways and retaining walls. Planners research during the cold months, so these pages should be ranking by spring.

  2. Late winter: spring cleanup and lawn care

    Cleanup, aeration, overseeding and weekly maintenance. The searches start as soon as the snow melts.

  3. Summer: fall cleanup

    Leaf removal and garden bed prep. Publish it while you're still busy with summer jobs.

  4. Fall: snow removal

    Residential and commercial plowing. People sign seasonal contracts before the first big snowfall, not after.

The key rule: never delete a seasonal page when its season ends. Keep the address, update the content each year, and let it build up trust. We've seen a site delete pages with no redirect, including one that ranked for a search with real monthly volume. That ranking was simply gone.

Reviews, ads and reach

Getting chosen across the whole city

Landscaping crews cover a lot of ground. Your visibility on Google Maps doesn't automatically follow the trucks.

Reviews with photos

A finished yard is your best advertisement, and neighbours notice. Ask every customer for a review when the job wraps up, and invite them to add a photo. A review with a picture of the new patio sells the next patio.

Reply to each one by name. Don't offer a discount for reviews; it breaks Google's rules.

Map reach

A company based in Waterdown can rank well there and be invisible in Binbrook. We use geo-grid rank tracking to see where you appear across the whole service area, then build up the weak spots.

Local Services Ads and search ads

Google has offered Local Services Ads for landscaping and lawn care in Canada, as part of its trade categories. Check what's available for Hamilton when you apply, since the list changes.

Search ads help in the first weeks of spring, before new pages have settled. Turn them down as organic calls grow. See how we run Google Ads for local businesses.

Common website problems

What holds landscaping websites back

The work is beautiful. The websites often aren't built to show it, or to rank.

Huge photos

Full-size camera images straight onto the page make it crawl on a phone. Resize and compress them, then add a short description of each.

Stock photos

Picture-perfect gardens from a photo library don't convince anyone. Real job photos from Hamilton yards do.

Design and mowing on one page

Two buyers, two budgets, one page. Neither search gets a page that fits it properly.

FAQ

Landscaping SEO questions

When should a landscaper start SEO for spring?

In the fall or early winter. Fresh pages often need a few months before Google ranks them well, so work started in April tends to pay off in summer, not spring.

Should I keep my snow removal page up in summer?

Yes. Leave it live and update it each fall. A page that stays up keeps its history with Google. One that disappears every spring starts from zero every winter.

Is Landscaper or Lawn care service the better primary category?

Go with the work that brings in most of your revenue. Design-build and full-service companies usually pick "Landscaper". Companies that mostly mow and maintain lawns often do better with "Lawn care service".

Do before-and-after photos help?

They help people decide, which is half the battle. Put them on project pages with a few lines about the job and the area, and add them to your Business Profile through the season.

Can I compete with bigger landscaping companies?

Often, yes. The map pack leans heavily on distance and relevance, so a smaller company with a well-built profile and clear service pages can win searches close to home.
