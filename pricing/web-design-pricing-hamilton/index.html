Website design cost in Hamilton: what changes the price

A website quote is really a list of decisions. How many pages, which platform, who writes the words and whose photos. Change one, and the price moves.

Kirill Anikin · October 30, 2026 · 5 min read

Key takeaways

  • The biggest price drivers are the number of pages, the platform, who writes the copy and where the photos come from.
  • A service business needs one page per service. That page count often decides the price more than the design does.
  • Budget for running costs too: the platform or hosting, the domain, updates and edits.
  • A cheap site that's slow on a phone, or that loses your old rankings, can cost more than it saves.

"How much is a website?" is like "how much is a truck?" A used pickup and a fitted-out service van are both trucks. The real question is what you need it to do.

For a local service business, a website has one job: turn searches into calls. Here's what changes the cost of building one that does it.

1. How many pages

Page count is often the biggest driver, and for a service business it isn't optional. Every service you want to rank for needs its own page, because every service is its own search.

A plumber who does drain cleaning, water heaters and emergency repairs needs at least those three pages, plus a homepage, an about page and a contact page. Add area pages for Stoney Creek or Ancaster, and the count grows again.

Area pages are only worth building if each one has real local substance. Cheap sites often clone one page and swap the city name. We've found a paragraph naming a different Ontario city left behind on several service pages that way.

2. The platform

We build on Wix and as fast static sites, and work on WordPress. Each one changes the price in a different way:

PlatformUpfrontRunning costsGood for
WixQuicker to build from a templateA Wix subscription, paid to WixOwners who want to edit their own site easily
WordPressDepends on the theme and pluginsHosting, plugin licences and regular updatesSites that need lots of features or flexibility
Static siteBuilt page by pageLight hosting; edits usually go through a developerFast, simple sites that don't change often

No platform ranks better on its own. How the site is set up matters far more. Our pages on Wix SEO and WordPress SEO cover each platform's quirks.

3. Who writes the words

Copy is where rankings come from, and it's the line many quotes leave vague. There are three options:

  • You write it. Cheapest, but it takes time and needs editing for search.
  • The designer fills it in. Often generic, and sometimes left over from the template.
  • An SEO writer does it. Costs more, but each page is built around a real search and your real facts.

Our guide to SEO content writing cost explains how copy is priced.

4. Photos

Real photos of your team, your trucks and your finished jobs build trust in a way stock photos can't. A Waterdown landscaper showing their own patios says more than a generic garden picture ever will.

Options run from photos you take on your phone, to photos collected over several weeks of jobs, to a professional shoot. Each one changes the price.

5. Features

Extras add cost: online booking, quote forms with photo uploads, project galleries, an online store or a second language. Ask which ones customers will actually use.

For most trades, a phone number that's easy to tap and a short form that works on a phone matter more than anything fancy.

6. SEO setup at launch

A site built for search has the basics done before it goes live:

  • A unique title and main heading on every page, with the right city
  • Fast loading on a phone
  • Structured data with your business details
  • Google Search Console connected and a sitemap submitted
  • robots.txt checked, so Google isn't blocked

Speed deserves its own mention. We've seen a site whose main content took 27.7 seconds to appear on a phone. That's a site paying to lose visitors. More in our guide to page speed for local businesses.

7. Moving an existing site

If your current site ranks for anything, a redesign carries a hidden job: mapping every old page to a new one and setting up redirects. Skip it, and you can lose what the old site earned.

We've seen a page deleted with no redirect, even though it ranked for a search with real monthly volume. Our guide to website redesigns and SEO covers how to avoid that.

How to get website quotes you can compare

Website quotes are hard to compare because each one describes a different site. Send every designer the same short brief and ask them to answer it line by line:

  • The full page list, including one page per service and any area pages
  • The platform, and what you'll pay each month to keep it running
  • Who writes the copy, and whether it's written for search
  • Where the photos come from
  • Which SEO basics are done before launch: titles, headings, structured data, speed, Search Console
  • How old pages will be redirected, if you already have a site
  • Who owns the site and the domain when it's finished

Now you're comparing the same thing. A quote for a kitchen renovator in Ancaster that includes written copy and redirects isn't more expensive than one that doesn't. It's a different, more complete job.

Running costs after launch

  • Platform subscription or hosting
  • Domain name renewal
  • Updates, especially on WordPress
  • Edits and new pages as your business changes
  • SEO, if you want the site to keep climbing

A worked example: when does a website pay for itself?

Use your own numbers. If one new customer from the website is worth $X in profit, and the site costs $W to build, it pays for itself after W ÷ X new customers.

Now picture a cheaper site that loads slowly on a phone. It saves you some of W up front. But if it turns away even one caller a month who gives up waiting, it loses X every month, and the saving is gone before long.

Common questions about website cost

Why do website quotes vary so much?

Because they're quoting different things: different page counts, platforms and amounts of writing. Ask each designer for a page list and who writes the copy, then compare like with like.

Is a template website good enough?

It can be, if the template is fast and every word and image is replaced with your own. The risk is leftover text. We've found a project page copied from a template for a company in another country.

Do I pay monthly for a website?

Usually something, yes: a platform subscription or hosting, plus your domain. Edits, updates and SEO may cost extra, depending on who you hire.

Will a new website hurt my rankings?

It can, if old pages are deleted without redirects or Google is accidentally blocked at launch. Both are avoidable with a redirect map and a launch-day check.
