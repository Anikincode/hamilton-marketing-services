How customers search

People search by appliance, brand and symptom

The fridge is warm, the milk is going off, and the freezer is full. That homeowner isn't researching. They're looking for someone who can come soon, and they'll describe the problem three different ways before they find you.

By appliance

"fridge repair Hamilton" · "washer repair Stoney Creek" · "dishwasher repair Waterdown" · "oven repair Ancaster"

By symptom

"dryer not heating" · "fridge not cooling but freezer works" · "washer won't drain" · "dishwasher leaking"

By brand

Owners often type the name on the door first, then the job: the brand plus "fridge repair Hamilton Mountain" or "washer repair Dundas".

Most appliance calls are urgent repairs. A smaller share is planned: installations, a second opinion before replacing, or a landlord with several units. The urgent calls are won on the map. The rest are often won by the page that best explains the problem.

Google Business Profile

Set up as a service-area business, with the right categories

Most appliance technicians drive to the customer. That shapes how your profile should look.

"Appliance repair service" is the natural primary category. Add secondaries for the appliances you really fix often:

  • Refrigerator repair service. For the most urgent call in the trade.
  • Washer & dryer repair service. If laundry is a big share of your work.
  • Dryer vent cleaning service. Only if you sell it as a separate job.
  • Appliance parts supplier. Only if customers can buy parts from you.

Category names can change, so pick whatever is closest in Google's list when you set up.

Working from home is fine

Plenty of technicians run the business from a home office or a van. Google lets you keep that address private and show the places you travel to instead, such as Hamilton Mountain, Stoney Creek and Grimsby.

Don't list a home address as if customers can drop in, and don't stuff "Appliance Repair Hamilton" into your business name. Both break Google's guidelines. Read about service-area businesses on Google Maps.

Service pages

Build pages the way people describe the problem

One "Appliance Repair" page can't rank for fridges, washers, dryers and ovens at once. Split it up, then go one level deeper where it pays.

  1. One page per appliance

    Fridge, freezer, washer, dryer, dishwasher, oven and range. Each with common faults, what a visit involves, and how your call-out fee works.

  2. Symptom pages that link up

    "Dryer not heating" or "fridge not cooling" answers a specific worry, then sends the reader to the dryer or fridge page to book.

  3. Brand pages, carefully

    Only for brands you service often and stock parts for. Never call yourself "authorized" or "certified" for a brand unless you are.

This is the same one-page-one-intent structure we use for every trade. Here's more on service pages that rank.

Reviews, ads and seasons

What wins the urgent call

When three repair companies show up on the map, the customer picks in seconds. Here's what tips it.

Reviews that mention the fix

"Fixed our washer in one visit and explained the fee upfront" is gold. Ask before you leave the house, while the machine is running again, and follow up with a text link.

Reply to every review. If someone complains about a diagnostic fee, explain it politely. Google bans buying reviews and trading discounts for them, so skip both.

Seasonal patterns

Appliance repair is steadier than most trades, but heat waves are hard on fridges, and nobody wants a dead oven before a holiday dinner. Post about both on your profile ahead of time.

Paid options

Local Services Ads in Canada are limited to certain trade categories. Check whether appliance repair is offered in the Hamilton area when you apply.

Search ads on urgent terms like "fridge repair Hamilton" can work well, as long as calls are tracked from day one. We set that up first in our Google Ads work.

Common website problems

Where appliance repair sites leak calls

Appliance repair sites are often built fast and copied across cities. That shortcut has a cost.

  • The wrong city in titles and headings. We've seen a page meant for one neighbourhood carry a different neighbourhood's name in its title. Google ranks it for the wrong place, and the right customers never see it. More in title tags for local SEO.
  • Brand logos everywhere. A wall of manufacturer logos can read as a claim of authorization you don't have. Name the brands in text instead.
  • Hidden call-out fee. People hate surprises at the door. A clear explanation of how you charge filters out the wrong calls and earns the right ones.
  • Traffic tools taken as gospel. We've seen a keyword tool estimate a site's traffic at about five times what Search Console really showed. Tools model; Search Console measures.

FAQ

Appliance repair SEO questions

Should I make pages for each appliance brand?

Only for brands you repair often and know well. A few solid brand pages beat dozens of thin ones. And don't call yourself an authorized service centre unless the manufacturer says you are.

Should I show my service call fee on the website?

We'd recommend it, or at least explain how it works and whether it goes toward the repair. Being upfront cuts down on arguments at the door and on angry reviews.

Are symptom pages like "dryer not heating" worth writing?

Yes, when they answer the question honestly and point to your service page. Some readers will fix a simple problem themselves. Many will realize they need a technician, and you've just earned their trust.

I work out of my home. Can I still show up on Google Maps?

Yes. Choose the service-area setup, keep the home address private and name the parts of the city you drive to. You can rank on the map without a storefront.

How do I compete with the big national repair brands?

On the map, distance and relevance count for a lot, so a local technician with a complete profile and steady reviews can win nearby searches. Clear pages and honest pricing do the rest.
