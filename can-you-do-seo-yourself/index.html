Can you do SEO yourself? What an owner can fix alone

Yes, and more of it than most SEO companies admit. Here's what you can do this month, the mistakes to avoid, and when it makes sense to hand it off.

Kirill Anikin · October 30, 2026 · 6 min read

Key takeaways

  • You can do a lot of local SEO yourself: your Google Business Profile, reviews, page titles and keeping your details consistent.
  • Google's own guides, Search Console and PageSpeed Insights are free, and they're enough to get started.
  • The expensive DIY mistakes are deleting pages without redirects, blocking Google by accident and copying template text.
  • Get help when the problems are technical, a redesign is coming, or your time is worth more on the tools.

Yes, you can do SEO yourself. In fact, one of the most valuable local SEO jobs there is, setting up your Google Business Profile properly, needs no technical skill at all.

SEO isn't magic. It's a list of jobs. Some are easy, some are fiddly and a few can do real damage if you get them wrong. Here's how to tell them apart.

What you can do yourself this month

Get your Google Business Profile right

Your Business Profile decides whether you show up on Google Maps at all. Start with the primary category. It decides which searches you can appear for, so a furnace company listed only under a general category will struggle to show for "furnace repair Hamilton".

Then list every service you really sell, and delete the ones you don't. We've audited a profile with a single category and a list of stock services the business didn't sell. That points Google at the wrong searches.

Two rules from Google's Business Profile guidelines: use your real business name with no keywords stuffed in, and never use a fake address. If you work at customers' homes, you can hide your address and show a service area instead. Our guide to Business Profile categories goes deeper on the first step.

Ask every customer for a review

Reviews are the one job an owner can do better than any agency, because you're the one standing in the customer's kitchen. Make it a habit: finish the job, text your review link, thank them when they post.

Reply to every review, good or bad. Never buy reviews or offer anything in return for them. Our guide on how to get more Google reviews covers the details.

Fix your page titles

The title is the blue link people click in Google. Each page needs its own, with the service and the area: "Furnace Repair in Stoney Creek | Your Company Name".

Check for the wrong city, too. We've seen a page aimed at one part of Toronto with a different neighbourhood in its title. It happens when pages get copied and nobody rereads them.

Titles matter even when you already rank. Our own site had a page at average position 3 to 5 with zero clicks from 233 impressions. That wasn't a ranking problem. It was a title nobody wanted to click.

Give each service its own page

One page should chase one search. "Drain cleaning Dundas" and "water heater installation Dundas" are different people with different problems. A single "Services" page that lists both usually ranks for neither.

Keep your details the same everywhere

Your business name, address and phone number should match on your website, your Business Profile and every directory listing. We've seen two separate businesses sharing one phone number across their listings, which leaves Google unsure which business is which.

Set up Google Search Console

It's free, and it's Google's own report on your site: which searches you show up for, how often people click and which pages Google hasn't indexed. Set it up once, then check it monthly.

Free resources worth your time

  • Google's SEO Starter Guide. Plain, official and free.
  • Google Business Profile Help. The rules for your profile, straight from Google.
  • Google Search Console. Your real search data, not an estimate.
  • PageSpeed Insights. Shows how fast your pages load on a phone, and what's slowing them down.
  • Our local SEO checklist. Work through it from top to bottom.

Paid tools exist too, from keyword tools like Semrush and Ahrefs to site crawlers like Screaming Frog. You don't need any of them to start. Our list of free SEO tools for small businesses has more options.

Where DIY SEO goes wrong

This is where most people slip up. Not by doing too little, but by changing the wrong thing.

  • Deleting pages without a redirect. We've seen a page deleted with no redirect, even though it ranked for a search with real monthly volume. If you remove or rename a page, point the old address at the new one.
  • Blocking Google by accident. One live site's robots.txt file told Google to ignore every page. One line in one file, and the whole site was shut out of search.
  • Copying a template without reading it. We've found a paragraph naming a different Ontario city on several service pages, and a project page copied from a template for a company in another country.
  • Two pages chasing the same search. On one site, a new page "replaced" the homepage on six searches, and all six dropped.
  • Changing everything at once. If you rewrite every title in the same week, you'll never know which change helped.

When to get help

DIY SEO is a great start. It stops being the right call when:

  • You've done the basics for months and nothing has moved.
  • Search Console shows most of your pages aren't indexed. On one site we audited, Google had indexed about 5 of its 48 pages.
  • You're about to redesign or move platforms. That's when rankings get lost.
  • You serve several areas, like the Mountain, Stoney Creek and Burlington, and need pages that are genuinely different for each.
  • Your time is worth more on the tools. If an hour on the job earns you $X and SEO is eating several hours a week, that's a real cost.

The middle path: get a plan, do the work

You don't have to choose between doing everything and handing everything off. Plenty of owners get a professional look at their site, then make the fixes themselves.

Common questions about doing your own SEO

How much time does DIY SEO take?

It depends on how much needs fixing. The first round, your profile, titles and Search Console, is the heaviest. After that, it's mostly asking for reviews and checking Search Console once a month.

Do I need to pay for SEO tools?

Not to start. Google Search Console, Google Business Profile and PageSpeed Insights are all free. Paid tools help with keyword research and tracking, but they estimate. Search Console measures.

Can I hurt my rankings by doing SEO myself?

Yes, but mostly in a few specific ways: deleting or renaming pages without redirects, blocking Google in robots.txt, or stuffing keywords into your Business Profile name. Avoid those and the rest is low risk.

Can I do SEO on Wix or Squarespace?

Yes. Both let you edit titles, descriptions and headings. Each platform has its own traps, so learn where the settings live before you change them.

Is DIY SEO enough to get into the map pack?

In a small area with little competition, it can be. For a busy search like "emergency plumber Hamilton", you'll usually need steadier work on reviews, pages and your profile than most owners have time for.
