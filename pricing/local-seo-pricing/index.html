Local SEO pricing: one-time setup vs ongoing work

Local SEO has two price tags. One for getting your Business Profile and site set up properly. One for staying in the map pack once you're there.

Kirill Anikin · October 30, 2026 · 5 min read

Key takeaways

  • Local SEO is priced as one-time setup, ongoing monthly work, or both.
  • Setup fixes your Google Business Profile, your details across the web and the local basics on your website.
  • Ongoing work covers reviews, posts, new pages, tracking across your area and protecting what you've earned.
  • Setup alone can be enough in a small, quiet market. Busy trades covering the whole city usually need ongoing work too.

Getting into the Google map pack is one job. Staying there is another.

That's why local SEO usually comes with two price tags: setup and ongoing work. Here's what each one covers, what changes the price and how to decide which you need.

What one-time setup covers

Setup is about getting the foundations right once. It usually includes:

  • Business Profile categories. The primary category decides which searches you can show for. Get it wrong, and you'll struggle to appear for your main service.
  • Services. Every real service listed, and the ones you don't sell removed. We've audited a profile with one category and a list of stock services the business didn't sell.
  • Service area. If you work at customers' homes, you can hide your address and show the areas you serve instead, like the Mountain, Stoney Creek and Ancaster.
  • Hours, photos and description. Accurate hours, real photos of real jobs and a plain description.
  • Consistent details. Your name, address and phone number made to match across your website and directories. We've seen two separate businesses sharing one phone number across their listings, which leaves Google unsure which is which.
  • Website basics. Titles and headings with the right city, a page per service and structured data with your business details.
  • A baseline. Where you rank today, measured across your area, so later progress is real.

Our Google Business Profile optimization service covers this part in detail.

What ongoing work covers

Once the foundations are in, monthly work keeps you moving and protects what you've built:

  • Reviews. A steady system for asking every customer, and a reply to every review.
  • Business Profile posts. Regular posts keep your profile active. We schedule daily posts for clients who want them.
  • New pages. Service and area pages added over time, each with one clear purpose.
  • Tracking across your area. Map rankings change with where the searcher is standing. Geo-grid tracking shows whether you appear on Upper James, in Dundas and out in Binbrook, not just from one point.
  • Watching the profile. Anyone can suggest edits to a Business Profile, including competitors. Someone needs to check that your hours, categories and details haven't been changed.
  • Defensive work. Spam links, broken pages and careless edits caught before they cost calls.

Setup only, or ongoing too?

Your situationWhat usually fits
One location, a small area and few competitors, say a specialist who only serves BinbrookSetup, then an occasional check-up
A busy trade across the whole city, like an emergency plumber covering the Mountain and the lower citySetup plus ongoing work
Several locations, each with its own profileSetup for each, plus ongoing work
A new business with no reviewsSetup, then a strong review habit before anything else

What changes the price

  • Number of locations. Each location has its own profile to set up and look after.
  • Size of your service area. Covering Hamilton plus Burlington and Grimsby means more tracking and more pages than covering Dundas alone.
  • How messy your listings are. Old addresses, old phone numbers and duplicate profiles take time to clean up.
  • Your review gap. If competitors have far more reviews, you need a stronger review push.
  • Your website's condition. A site with wrong-city titles or missing headings needs fixing before local pages can help.

What cheap local SEO gets wrong

  • Fake addresses. A virtual office or a made-up Hamilton address breaks Google's Business Profile rules and can get your profile suspended.
  • Keyword-stuffed names. Adding "Emergency Plumber Hamilton" to the end of your business name is against the rules too.
  • Directory blasts. Hundreds of listings created at once, while nobody fixes the wrong ones that already exist.
  • Tracking from one spot. A single ranking from one location tells you very little about the rest of your area.

Questions to ask before you pay for local SEO

Whoever you hire, these questions tell you quickly whether a local SEO quote is worth the money.

  1. Which primary category would you choose for me, and why? A good answer is specific to what you sell, not the broadest category available.
  2. Will you change my business name? The right answer is no, unless your real name has changed.
  3. How will you track my rankings? Look for tracking across your area, from the Mountain to the lower city, not one screenshot.
  4. Who owns the Business Profile? You should, with the company added as a manager.
  5. How will reviews be asked for? A simple, steady habit you can keep up, with no paid or fake reviews.
  6. What exactly happens each month? Posts, pages, tracking, checks. If they can't list it, they can't report on it.

A worked example: is it paying?

Your Business Profile shows how many people called you, clicked through to your website or asked for directions. That's your starting point.

Say a booked job is worth $X in profit, and local SEO costs $Y a month. If you win one job for every N calls from your profile, each call is worth X ÷ N to you. Divide Y by that, and you have the number of extra calls a month that covers the cost.

Setup works the same way, just once. If setup costs $S, it has paid for itself after S ÷ X extra jobs.

Common questions about local SEO pricing

Is a Google Business Profile free?

Yes. Creating and managing a Business Profile costs nothing. What you pay for in local SEO is the time and know-how to set it up properly and keep it working.

Can I pay for setup and do the rest myself?

Yes, and it suits a lot of small businesses. After setup, the most important ongoing job is asking every customer for a review, which you can do better than anyone.

Why does local SEO cost more for more locations?

Each location has its own Business Profile, its own reviews and its own competitors. More locations means more profiles to set up, watch and track.

Are daily Business Profile posts worth paying for?

They keep your profile active and give customers fresh reasons to call. They won't fix a wrong category or a lack of reviews, so get those right first.
